Hong Kong jewelry and watch sales rise 20.3% on the year in September

The category, which also covers clocks and gifts, sold HK$4,706 million on provisional figures, up on a year earlier but below August's figure. Over nine months it is the fastest-growing outlet type in the government's table.

By Carat Capital

9 November 2026 Corrected

What changed

The category grew faster. Hong Kong's jewellery, watch and valuable-gift outlets sold HK$4,706 million in September, 20.3% more than a year earlier, on provisional government figures. August's rise was 12.2%.

What it means

  • If you sell to Hong Kong counters: the rise is on a year earlier; against August, sales fell 8.7%.
  • If you track the year: nine-month sales are up 22.4%, the fastest of any outlet type.
  • What this is not: a volume figure. The category is measured in value, and the government gives no cause for it.

Provisional: up on 2025, below August.

Up on the year. Category sales rose 20.3% on September 2025, provisional figures show. August's rise is now 12.2% after revision.

Provisional: down on August. September's HK$4,706 million was 8.7% below August's revised HK$5,153 million. The release adjusts only total retail for the season, by quarter.

The category leads the year.

Nine months ahead. Category sales reached HK$45,820 million from January to September, up 22.4% on 2025. No other outlet type in Table 1 rose faster over the nine months.

Nearly a seventh. The category made 14.1% of September's provisional HK$33,357 million in total retail sales, which rose 6.4%.

The government's reasons cover total retail.

Commentary covers the total. A government spokesman ties total sales growth to solid local consumption sentiment, and says visitor arrivals and an earlier Mid-Autumn Festival this year also contributed. The release gives no cause for the jewellery category.

What to watch

  • December 2026C&SD's October figures, with September revised. The test: whether the category's rise on a year earlier holds in October.

Corrections1 note

Clarified, 10 November 2026: the headline said sales rose 20.3% in September without saying against what; the rise is on September 2025, and against August sales fell 8.7%. A subhead, 'The government names visitors.', read as a cause for the category; the government's reasons cover total retail only, and the subhead and the What to watch test now say so. What was right as published: every figure on the page.

What would change this call

If October shows the category falling on a year earlier, September reads as festival timing, which is how the government explains total sales. A revised September below 20% would weaken the call too.

How the department counts the category

C&SD publishes the value of retail sales by broad type of retail outlet each month, provisional for the latest month and revised a month later. The category is 'jewellery, watches and clocks, and valuable gifts'. Values are in Hong Kong dollars and are not adjusted for prices; the release gives a separate volume figure only for total retail.

Method

C&SD release, https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202611/03/P2026110300398.htm, read 9 November 2026, 200, 20,205 bytes: 'sales of jewellery, watches and clocks, and valuable gifts (+20.3% in value)'; total retail 'provisionally estimated at $33.4 billion, increased by 6.4%'; the commentary credits 'a pick-up in visitor arrivals and sales of festive items associated with the earlier arrival of Mid-Autumn Festival' for overall sales. The word 'jewellery' appears once in the release text, in the outlet list, and not in the commentary (grep of the text, 1 hit). Table 1 PDF, read 9 November, 200, 498,358 bytes: category row 5,153 (August, revised) / 4,706 (September) / 45,820 (January to September) / +12.2 / +20.3 / +22.4; all retail 32,076 / 33,357 / 299,449 / +5.6 / +6.4 / +8.3. Carat Capital's arithmetic: 4,706 / 5,153 = 0.913 -> 8.7% below August; 4,706 / 33,357 = 14.1%. The nine-month column was read row by row: the next-fastest outlet type is electrical goods at +20.6%. Carat Capital's 6 October page gave August as HK$5.14 billion, the provisional figure; it is revised to HK$5,153 million.
