Gold & Metals

$1.19bn of gold jewellery carries India's September exports

Total gem and jewellery shipments rose 9.4% to $2.95 billion, with studded gold up 50.2%. Cut and polished natural diamonds fell 5.4% in dollars while lab-grown polished rose 40.7%.

By Carat Capital

18 October 2026 Corrected

What changed

Gold carried the month, not diamonds. India's gem and jewellery exports rose 9.4% to $2.95 billion in September. Gold jewellery climbed 34% to $1.19 billion while cut and polished natural diamonds fell 5.4% to $1.29 billion.

What it means

  • If you export plain gold to the Gulf: Over six months plain gold jewellery fell 8.8% to $2.53 billion while the gold price rose 31.2%. The council's chairman names Middle East demand as the difficulty.
  • If you cut natural polished: September's 5.4% dollar decline was a 2.2% rise in rupees. Over six months value fell 7% while volume rose 4.6% to 8.398 million carats.
  • What this is not: A volume count. Every line is dollar value, so a 34% rise can mean more gold, dearer gold, or both.

Studded gold led everything

Half again. Studded gold jewellery exports jumped 50.2% to $812.6 million in September, the largest single move in any category the council reported.

Plain gold rose too. Plain gold jewellery exports increased 8.8% to $378 million for the month, a far smaller gain than the studded line beside it.

The category total. Gold jewellery overall climbed 34% to $1.19 billion, which the source names the standout category of the month.

Natural polished fell in dollars only

Two currencies, two answers. Cut and polished natural diamond exports fell 5.4% year on year in dollars to $1.29 billion in September, but rose 2.2% measured in Indian rupees.

Lab-grown grew faster. Polished lab-grown diamond exports increased 40.7% to $151.4 million for the month, from a base about a ninth the size of natural polished.

Volume beat value. Across six months natural polished export value fell 7% to $5.81 billion while volume rose 4.6% to 8.398 million carats.

The chairman names a recovery

Three reasons given. GJEPC chairman Kirit Bhansali pointed to improving diamond prices, higher volumes and growth in rupee terms as signs of a 'gradual recovery' in the segment.

Bullion hurt plain gold. Over six months plain gold jewellery exports fell 8.8% to $2.53 billion as gold prices rose 31.2% year on year to $4,415.70 an ounce.

The Gulf drags. Bhansali said demand remained challenging, particularly in the Middle East, which the source names an important destination for India's plain gold jewellery.

What to watch

  • Mid-November 2026October's monthly release, which would show whether studded gold holds a growth rate above 50%.
  • OngoingWhether natural polished turns positive in dollars. It has risen in rupees while falling in dollars.
  • MonthlyWhether studded gold keeps its six-month gain of 31.3%. The council's chairman credits the India-UK trade agreement and lower US tariffs.

What would change this call

The October figures. GJEPC's own statement and JCK's report agree on every figure here, so the risk is not the reading but the trend: if studded gold slows sharply in October, September's 50.2% was a single strong month, not a shift in what India exports.

Why a dollar fall and a rupee rise are both true

India exports jewellery priced in dollars and keeps its accounts in rupees, so every export figure has two growth rates and the gap between them is the currency's move over the same window. September's cut and polished natural diamond exports fell 5.4% in dollars and rose 2.2% in rupees, which is not a contradiction: it is the rupee having weakened against the dollar by roughly the difference. Neither figure is the more honest one. A Surat cutter paying wages in rupees experienced a rising business; an Antwerp dealer paying in dollars experienced a cheaper supplier. The same two-currency effect runs the other way through the gold jewellery line, and it is the reason the six-month plain gold figure looks so much worse than the monthly one. Plain gold jewellery is sold largely by weight with a thin making charge, so when bullion rises 31.2% year on year to $4,415.70 an ounce, the dollar value of a given tonnage rises while the tonnage a buyer can afford falls. Studded jewellery carries stones and labour that do not move with the gold price, so the same bullion move compresses it far less. That is the mechanism behind a month in which studded gold grew 50.2% and plain gold grew 8.8%, and behind a half-year in which plain gold fell 8.8% outright. The metal did not help both lines equally because the metal is a different share of each.
