Producers hold the line on rough

De Beers-style supply discipline is back: allocations trimmed, prices defended. Polished has stabilized after two brutal years — the question is whether midstream margins recover before credit patience runs out.

B-02

The 86% gap becomes the strategy

With lab-grown 1ct goods under $750, natural sellers have stopped competing on price and started competing on account of provenance, rarity and resale. Watch the marketing budgets, not the price lists.

Positioning · Global
B-03

Traceability goes from virtue to invoice

Origin platforms are no longer CSR decoration — G7 rules and retailer mandates are making sourcing paperwork a condition of sale. Compliant goods are starting to command a measurable premium.

Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
The SightTerm

De Beers' ten-times-a-year allocation sale, where approved buyers — sightholders — purchase rough in pre-assembled boxes at prices set by the producer. Refusing a box has consequences; so does needing one.

Four Cs vs. the fifthConcept

Carat, color, clarity, cut set the grade — but the trade increasingly prices a fifth C: certainty of origin. Two identical stones can part ways on price over a paper trail.

Midstream squeezeDynamic

Miners set rough prices, retailers set polished prices, and the cutters and traders in between absorb the difference. When both ends tighten at once, Surat and Antwerp bleed first — the industry's recurring crisis.

MemoPractice

Goods consigned to retailers on memorandum — sold before they're paid for. Memo keeps counters full and balance sheets fragile; its terms are one of the best barometers of trade confidence.

Diamonds This week — August 17

GJEPC put eight customs grievances to Delhi's Chief Commissioner in Mumbai, and was asked for a flow chart of the rough trade

The Gem and Jewellery Export Promotion Council met Manish Kumar, Chief Commissioner of Customs, Delhi, at its head office in Mumbai on 24 August, following Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman's nomination of him to meet the trade. GJEPC fielded Kirit Bhansali, chairman; Shaunak Parikh, vice chairman; Anoop Mehta, convener of the diamond panel; Ashish Borda, convener of PMBD; Mital Doshi, convener of BITC; and Sabyasachi Ray, executive director. The Bharat Diamond Bourse sent vice president Mehul Shah and customs convener Anup Zaveri. This desk counts eight distinct grievances in the council's own account: rough valuation assessments; repeated examination including continued 100% checking at Mumbai Customs; underuse of the Risk Management System and of importer compliance history; inconsistency between Mumbai and Surat; absence of specialised trade expertise in valuation, on the argument that a general valuer cannot reach a transaction-level market value for rough; misdeclaration proceedings arising from minor weight or description differences; detention of consignment exports and re-imports over small variations; and inconsistent interpretation caused by frequent personnel changes. Kumar agreed a risk-based approach could reduce unnecessary examination while keeping safeguards against revenue leakage, said the concerns would be examined with the Mumbai and Surat formations, and requested a consolidated flow chart of the rough diamond trade process plus a comprehensive list of issues. No consignment count, detention figure, clearance time, relief or timeline was published, and this desk estimates none.

Source — GJEPC, via Heera Zhaveraat and Solitaire International, 24 August 2026
Diamonds This week — August 17

A rough stone passes visual screening as natural at the Israeli Diamond Institute and is classified Type IIa by infrared

The Israeli Diamond Institute's technology department, in an article dated 25 August 2026 on its own site and bylined to staff gemologist Sergey Lazon, reports a rough stone submitted on Tuesday 18 August whose recorded data matched expected parameters and which externally appeared to be natural rough. Microscopy found small clouds and inclusions of the kind natural rough carries, together with minor defects the institute describes as atypical of natural diamond and consistent with CVD growth. A dedicated CVD screening device returned a preliminary indication of laboratory origin, which the institute itself qualifies by noting such devices can give ambiguous readings where inclusions are weak. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy returned what the institute calls an unambiguous result and classified the stone as Type IIa, the nitrogen-poor category most laboratory-grown diamond falls into and one the institute says is difficult to attribute to natural origin given this rough's structure and shape. The account gives no carat weight, measurements, colour, price or country of submission. It states that the Gemological Institute of America identified a comparable stone days earlier and attributes that to a report by IDEX Online rather than to the GIA directly, a claim hedged twice in the original and carried hedged here. Single-source: the testing laboratory describing its own test, with no independent outlet reporting it and no submitting party named.

Source — Israeli Diamond Institute, 25 August 2026 (Hebrew)
Diamonds This week — August 17

India runs a prototype indigenous HPHT press at IIT Madras against a $594 million import bill

Diamond City, the Gujarati fortnightly serving the Surat trade, reported on 24 August 2026 that the India Centre for Lab-Grown Diamond (InCent-LGD) has completed successful trials of a domestically designed high-pressure high-temperature diamond press at IIT Madras and grown HPHT rough on it. The press is a prototype built under the 242 crore rupee research programme announced for IIT Madras in the 2023 union budget, three years before the trial. Components for a full-scale commercial press have been manufactured across units in north India and shipped to IIT Madras for integration, assembly and final testing. Gem and Jewellery Export Promotion Council sources put an official announcement of the capability as likely in November 2026 and the first full-scale press as expected complete by December 2026; every date is an expectation reported through third parties. InCent has issued no statement of its own and this desk found no English-language trade coverage at press time, so the item is carried single-source and labelled so in the body, in Plate I's note and here. India bought roughly $594 million of rough lab-grown diamonds from China and Hong Kong in FY2025-26 on the same report's figures, which it converts to more than 4,900 crore rupees, an implied rate of about 82.5 rupees to the dollar. On that implied rate the 242 crore rupee programme is close to $29 million, or about 4.9% of a single year's import bill, on this desk's arithmetic and the outlet's own conversion rather than a market rate this desk has verified. India already holds a commanding share of world production by chemical vapour deposition and Surat cuts and polishes more than 90% of the world's diamonds; the gap is HPHT press capacity, where China's scale has made it the supplier of record.

Source — Diamond City, 24 August 2026 (Gujarati)
Diamonds This week — August 17

Indian imports of Russian rough fell from $1,195 million to $307 million, and Surat dismisses a Yakutia cluster

Diamond City reported on 24 August 2026 that Indian imports of Russian rough ran at $1,195 million in FY2022-23 and $307 million in FY2025-26, a fall of $888 million or 74.3% on this desk's arithmetic. Russia once supplied roughly 30% of world rough. The report states that an executive order signed on 6 August 2026 establishes a diamond-cutting cluster between the Republic of Sakha (Yakutia) and the Smolensk region, where Alrosa's Kristall cutting works sits; that Russia is preparing an 8% export duty on rough; and that miners will be obliged to offer rough and semi-polished goods to the cluster's domestic manufacturers before the open market. The outlet carries its own secondary-research disclaimer on that policy material and this paper attributes the detail to it rather than presenting it as a verified reading of the order. Dinesh Navadiya, chairman of the Indian Diamond Institute, and Surat manufacturers argue that Botswana and Russia have both attempted domestic cutting before without matching Surat's scale or cost, where more than 800,000 skilled workers process nine of every ten rough stones in the world. Dubai-based traders and Surat importers add that with demand slow and margins thin nobody is funding new factories or relocating cutters, and that the United States takes about 30% of India's gem and jewellery exports while Russian goods remain under sanction. The report gives no capacity figure, timetable, employment target or redirected volume for the cluster, so there is no quantity against which Surat's confidence can be tested.

Source — Diamond City, 24 August 2026 (Gujarati)
Diamonds This week — August 17

Gem Diamonds' first half averages $1,395 a carat, up 38%, on 42,624 carats for $59.5 million

Gem Diamonds' H1 2026 trading update, published 28 July 2026 and covering 1 January to 30 June, reports revenue of $59.5 million on 42,624 carats sold at an average of $1,395 a carat, against $1,008 in the first half of 2025, a rise of 38%. Eight diamonds sold above $1.0 million each for aggregate revenue of $16.1 million, which is 27% of the half's revenue on this desk's arithmetic. The highest price of the period was $32,908 a carat for a 52.24-carat white, about $1.72 million for the stone. The update also names a 346.99-carat recovery at Letšeng, the Lesotho Jubilee, and reports no price against it. Reconciling against the first quarter: Q1 revenue of $32.1 million is the 16,727-carat first export sale at $1,501 a carat, being $25.1 million, plus a parcel of ten stones above 10.8 carats totalling 363 carats for $7.0 million, which sum exactly to the stated figure and to 17,090 carats, a blended Q1 rate of $1,878. The half less the quarter leaves about $27.4 million on 25,534 carats, an implied Q2 average near $1,073, roughly 43% below the Q1 blended rate; that subtraction is this desk's arithmetic and is not a company figure. For comparison, De Beers' realised price for the same half was $105 a carat on revenue of $1.58 billion, down 19%, with an underlying loss of $188 million, 23% narrower, as this paper reported on 31 July 2026.

Source — Gem Diamonds H1 2026 trading update, 28 July 2026 / Gem Diamonds Q1 2026 trading update via Mining Indaba and TipRanks / Carat Capital, 31 July 2026
