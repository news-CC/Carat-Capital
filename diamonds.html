What’s moving now

Supply · Gaborone

Producers hold the line on rough

De Beers-style supply discipline is back: allocations trimmed, prices defended. Polished has stabilized after two brutal years — the question is whether midstream margins recover before credit patience runs out.

Positioning · Global

The gap becomes the strategy

Natural sellers have stopped competing with lab-grown on price and started competing on provenance, rarity and resale. Watch the marketing budgets, not the price lists. The dated gap itself is on the price pages, not here.

Regulation · Antwerp

Traceability goes from virtue to invoice

Origin platforms are no longer CSR decoration — G7 rules and retailer mandates are making sourcing paperwork a condition of sale. Compliant goods are starting to command a measurable premium.

The working vocabulary

The trade’s terms, plainly told. More in the Field Guide.

The Sight Term
De Beers' ten-times-a-year allocation sale, where approved buyers — sightholders — purchase rough in pre-assembled boxes at prices set by the producer. Refusing a box has consequences; so does needing one.
Four Cs vs. the fifth Concept
Carat, color, clarity, cut set the grade — but the trade increasingly prices a fifth C: certainty of origin. Two identical stones can part ways on price over a paper trail.
Midstream squeeze Dynamic
Miners set rough prices, retailers set polished prices, and the cutters and traders in between absorb the difference. When both ends tighten at once, Surat and Antwerp bleed first — the industry's recurring crisis.
Memo Practice
Goods consigned to retailers on memorandum — sold before they're paid for. Memo keeps counters full and balance sheets fragile; its terms are one of the best barometers of trade confidence.

  4. This week, August 20 to September 23

    Three undated figures come off the desk pages

    Corrected 2 October 2026: the standing briefing cards on diamonds.html and retail-tech.html each carried an undated lab-grown discount to natural of 86%, diamonds.html also carried "lab-grown 1ct goods under $750", and gold-metals.html carried "$4,000 is the floor - tested three times, held three times". All four figures were written as literal strings inside build.py, so they could not move when the data moved, which is the fault the chief ruled on for the LGD1 chip on 23 September 2026. The 86% figure was correct for the period of this paper's own dated report of it; against the paper's own current record it is 88.1% at 25 July 2026, and gold's own tape mark is $4,183.90 at 2 October 2026. Every one of the four has been removed rather than restated, per the standing rule that an unverified figure is null and never invented; the cards now point the reader to the dated price pages and the tape, which are driven from content/prices.json, content/lab-prices.json and the tape files. No dated article was affected and no article figure has changed. Found by the Editor's own once-per-edition sweep of the builder for figure literals, which the chief ordered on 23 September 2026; the sweep pattern used then matched assignments only and missed figures held inside tuples, which is why these four survived it.

    Source: caratchief ruling of 2026-09-23 on figure literals in build.py; content/prices.json; content/lab-prices.json; newsroom/tape/2026-10-02.json
