The briefing — what's moving now

Updated each edition
B-01

Producers hold the line on rough

De Beers-style supply discipline is back: allocations trimmed, prices defended. Polished has stabilized after two brutal years — the question is whether midstream margins recover before credit patience runs out.

Supply · Gaborone
B-02

The 86% gap becomes the strategy

With lab-grown 1ct goods under $750, natural sellers have stopped competing on price and started competing on account of provenance, rarity and resale. Watch the marketing budgets, not the price lists.

Positioning · Global
B-03

Traceability goes from virtue to invoice

Origin platforms are no longer CSR decoration — G7 rules and retailer mandates are making sourcing paperwork a condition of sale. Compliant goods are starting to command a measurable premium.

Regulation · Antwerp

Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
The SightTerm

De Beers' ten-times-a-year allocation sale, where approved buyers — sightholders — purchase rough in pre-assembled boxes at prices set by the producer. Refusing a box has consequences; so does needing one.

Four Cs vs. the fifthConcept

Carat, color, clarity, cut set the grade — but the trade increasingly prices a fifth C: certainty of origin. Two identical stones can part ways on price over a paper trail.

Midstream squeezeDynamic

Miners set rough prices, retailers set polished prices, and the cutters and traders in between absorb the difference. When both ends tighten at once, Surat and Antwerp bleed first — the industry's recurring crisis.

MemoPractice

Goods consigned to retailers on memorandum — sold before they're paid for. Memo keeps counters full and balance sheets fragile; its terms are one of the best barometers of trade confidence.

Diamonds This week — August 17

The lab-grown one-carat reference reads $705, drawn from offers spanning 17.6 times

On 17 August 2026 CaratRadar's reference average for a one-carat round lab-grown diamond read US$705 a carat, on a page dated the same day which states that it refreshes daily; this is the figure carried on this paper's tape. The series this paper has published runs US$727 earlier in August, US$711 on 11 August 2026, US$707 on 14 August 2026 and US$705 on 17 August 2026, a decline of 0.84% over six days and 3.03% from US$727. The same source page shows the offers underlying that average running from US$185 to US$3,247 a carat for a one-carat round, a spread of 17.6 times, this desk's division; at two carats the cushion range runs US$345 to US$6,984 and at three carats US$449 to US$12,050. A second source disagrees and both are carried. This paper's own Natural Diamond Price List, compiled 25 July 2026 from a live retail sample, puts the one-carat round lab-grown at US$550 a carat against a natural one-carat round at US$4,581, a multiple of 8.3; CaratRadar's US$705 is 28.2% above that US$550, this desk's arithmetic, across a three-week gap and two different samples of the same market. Neither figure is presented as correct against the other: one is a live retail asking average from one aggregator, the other a six-retailer median from another, and no clearing exchange exists to settle the difference. Separately, Marty Hurwitz, executive director of the Grown Diamond Trade Organization, told JCK in an article published 14 August 2026 that lab-grown diamonds are a help and not a hindrance to the jewelry industry, and identified the sector's principal challenge as bringing Gen Z consumers into jewellery stores at all, noting that consumers continue to buy the category without macro-level marketing behind it.

Source — CaratRadar (read 17 August 2026) / Carat Capital Natural Diamond Price List (25 July 2026) / JCK (14 August 2026) / Carat Capital arithmetic and archive
Diamonds Week of August 10

Sarine's first-half deficit widened 21-fold on a revenue decline of about $0.9 million

Sarine Technologies reported results for the six months ended 30 June 2026 on 11 August 2026. Revenue was $14.4 million, down 6% on the first half of 2025, and the group recorded a net loss of $3.5 million against a net loss of $166,000 in the comparable half. No dividend was declared. Dividing one deficit by the other gives 21.1 times, this desk's arithmetic on the two published figures. Taking 6% off the published revenue implies revenue of roughly $15.3 million in the first half of 2025, which puts the revenue decline at about $0.9 million against a deficit that widened by $3.334 million, or approximately three and a half times the fall in the top line; those three figures are this desk's arithmetic on rounded published figures and are published by no source. The company attributes the wider loss to higher operating expenses, driven by a weaker US dollar against the Israeli shekel, and to reduced natural-diamond processing volumes in the face of lab-grown competition. Against that, revenue from GCAL grading rose more than 50% in the half, revenue from the MVP rough-planning service more than doubled, and Kitov.ai, the industrial-inspection associate in which Sarine holds 33%, booked $1.5 million of revenue and also more than doubled. The company's own framing is that a 6% revenue decline is modest relative to a significantly greater contraction in rough diamond supply. Sarine sells the scanning, planning and grading equipment and services that stones pass through on the way to a polished parcel, so its revenue tracks stones processed rather than stones sold at retail. This paper published global rough production down 8% to 98.8 million carats in 2025 on 27 July 2026, and Lucara Diamond selling 24% fewer carats in its second quarter on 12 August 2026. This is the first coverage of Sarine Technologies in this paper's archive.

Source — Rapaport (11 August 2026) / Minichart (11 August 2026) / Carat Capital arithmetic and archive
Diamonds Week of August 10

Kiran Gems USA says 0.30 to 0.70-carat goods are selling at 1990s prices

On Rapaport's Heard on the Street, published 12 August 2026, Senil Lakhani, president of Kiran Gems USA, said that diamonds below one carat and up to 1.5 carats are less expensive than ever before and are selling at prices from the 1990s and early 2000s. He identified the 0.30, 0.40, 0.50 and 0.70-carat range as the bread and butter for America and placed the relevant price points at $1,000, $2,000 and $5,000 rather than at the half-million and million-dollar level. Kiran Gems is one of the largest diamond manufacturers in the world. This paper has reported the same goods from the index side through August: RapNet monthly moves for July had 0.30-carat goods up 1.6% and 0.50-carat goods up 1.8% against a flat one-carat index, published here on 4 August 2026, and the colour split inside that flat month had K to M goods averaging 1.7% against 0.1% for D to F and G to J colours in IF to VVS and VS clarity, published here on 12 August 2026. Those figures are directions for a single month; the characterisation of a 1990s price level is a level, and no index reachable by this desk publishes a 1999 comparison for 0.30 to 0.70-carat goods, so the level claim is reported as a named executive's characterisation of his own market and not as a price. On the same panel, Jeff Angel, head buyer at RDI Diamonds, said he can locate a diamond of traceable Botswana origin in about five minutes and recently filled a million-dollar order in which every stone was required to come from Botswana. Other participants in the episode included Ari Jain of House of Diamonds, Angelo Palmieri of GCAL by Sarine and Kerri Lavine of Diamanti NYC, moderated by Heather Chambers.

Diamonds Week of August 10

Lucara's realised price a carat rose 23.7% while both its sales channels cut prices

Lucara Diamond released its second-quarter 2026 results on 7 August 2026. The company sold 58,553 carats from its Karowe mine in Botswana during the quarter, against 77,167 carats in the second quarter of 2025, and recorded revenue of $41.0 million against $43.7 million, a fall of 6% on a 24% fall in carats sold. Dividing the published revenue by the published carats gives a realised price of $700.22 a carat for the quarter against $566.30 a year earlier, a rise of 23.7%; on the first half the same division gives $454.10 a carat against $493.21, a fall of 7.9%. All four of those per-carat figures are this desk's arithmetic and are published by no source. The company's own commentary states that average prices per carat declined across both channels, with tender prices falling 5% and Clara prices falling 17%, which it attributes to continued pricing pressure on mid-range and lower-grade stones. The whole of the realisation gain is therefore composition rather than price. It is not a function of more large stones reaching the surface either: specials, the stones above 10.8 carats, were recovered at 176 in the quarter against 242 a year earlier. Second-quarter net income was $15.6 million against $12.5 million; first-half net income was $1.0 million against $12.4 million, a fall of 92%, which implies a first-quarter loss of roughly $14.6 million by subtraction, this desk's arithmetic. The plant processed 761,848 tonnes of ore and recovered 90,082 carats at a grade of 10.9 carats per hundred tonnes, of which 6,973 carats came from tailings, at an operating cost of $23.76 a tonne, down 11% from $26.76. Full-year guidance was maintained at revenue of $100 million to $130 million, carats recovered and carats sold each at 340,000 to 360,000, ore processed at 2.6 to 2.9 million tonnes, operating costs of $27.50 to $31.00 a tonne, underground project capital of up to $110 million and sustaining capital of up to $11.5 million. Against $62.8 million of first-half revenue and 138,297 carats sold, that requires $37.2 million to $67.2 million and 201,703 to 221,703 carats in the second half, or 59% to 62% of the year's sales after the halfway point, all derived by this desk. Cash stood at $243.6 million at 30 June against bonds payable of $342.8 million with nothing drawn on the working capital or project facilities. The Karowe underground project has $275.9 million of completion cost remaining including contingency, with full production expected in the first half of 2028 and open-pit mining on track to conclude in the fourth quarter of 2026; in July 2026 the Botswana Department of Mines licensed the 15-person auxiliary winder and the 105-person man and material winder on the production shaft.

Source — Lucara Diamond Q2 2026 results via CNW (7 August 2026) / Rapaport / Carat Capital arithmetic
Diamonds Week of August 10

July's flat one-carat index hid a 1.7% gain in K to M colours

Rapaport's Diamond Pulse for August 2026 published a colour-band breakdown of July's one-carat polished moves. One-carat goods in D to F and G to J colour, in IF to VVS and VS clarity, averaged a gain of 0.1% in July. K to M colours averaged a gain of 1.7%. K to M goods in VS clarity swung from a 0.1% loss to a 2.8% gain. This paper led its edition of 4 August 2026 on the finding that the RapNet Diamond Index for one-carat polished was unchanged across July, closing a slide that had run without a positive print since June 2025, alongside gains of 1.6% for 0.30-carat goods, 1.8% for 0.50-carat goods and 0.2% for 3-carat goods. The colour breakdown shows that the flat headline is an average of a near-flat top and a rising bottom, and that the recovery this paper has described as bottom-up since 14 July 2026 is running from the bottom of the colour scale as well as the bottom of the size chart. The ratio between the two bands is seventeen times, this desk's division of 1.7 by 0.1. The finding rests on a single source, and that source is the owner of the index reporting on its own index; there is no second publisher of RapNet colour-band moves against which to check it. The remaining figures in the same newsletter are already in this paper: global rough production down 8% to 98.8 million carats in 2025 was published here on 27 July 2026, De Beers' first-half revenue down 19% to $1.58 billion on 31 July 2026, and the Section 301 tariff bands of 10% and 12.5% on jewellery and gemstone imports on 25 July 2026.

Source — Rapaport Diamond Pulse, August 2026 / Carat Capital archive
