B-01

Producers hold the line on rough

De Beers-style supply discipline is back: allocations trimmed, prices defended. Polished has stabilized after two brutal years — the question is whether midstream margins recover before credit patience runs out.

B-02

The 86% gap becomes the strategy

With lab-grown 1ct goods under $750, natural sellers have stopped competing on price and started competing on account of provenance, rarity and resale. Watch the marketing budgets, not the price lists.

Positioning · Global
B-03

Traceability goes from virtue to invoice

Origin platforms are no longer CSR decoration — G7 rules and retailer mandates are making sourcing paperwork a condition of sale. Compliant goods are starting to command a measurable premium.

Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
The SightTerm

De Beers' ten-times-a-year allocation sale, where approved buyers — sightholders — purchase rough in pre-assembled boxes at prices set by the producer. Refusing a box has consequences; so does needing one.

Four Cs vs. the fifthConcept

Carat, color, clarity, cut set the grade — but the trade increasingly prices a fifth C: certainty of origin. Two identical stones can part ways on price over a paper trail.

Midstream squeezeDynamic

Miners set rough prices, retailers set polished prices, and the cutters and traders in between absorb the difference. When both ends tighten at once, Surat and Antwerp bleed first — the industry's recurring crisis.

MemoPractice

Goods consigned to retailers on memorandum — sold before they're paid for. Memo keeps counters full and balance sheets fragile; its terms are one of the best barometers of trade confidence.

Diamonds This week — August 17

Gem Diamonds' first half averages $1,395 a carat, up 38%, on 42,624 carats for $59.5 million

Gem Diamonds' H1 2026 trading update, published 28 July 2026 and covering 1 January to 30 June, reports revenue of $59.5 million on 42,624 carats sold at an average of $1,395 a carat, against $1,008 in the first half of 2025, a rise of 38%. Eight diamonds sold above $1.0 million each for aggregate revenue of $16.1 million, which is 27% of the half's revenue on this desk's arithmetic. The highest price of the period was $32,908 a carat for a 52.24-carat white, about $1.72 million for the stone. The update also names a 346.99-carat recovery at Letšeng, the Lesotho Jubilee, and reports no price against it. Reconciling against the first quarter: Q1 revenue of $32.1 million is the 16,727-carat first export sale at $1,501 a carat, being $25.1 million, plus a parcel of ten stones above 10.8 carats totalling 363 carats for $7.0 million, which sum exactly to the stated figure and to 17,090 carats, a blended Q1 rate of $1,878. The half less the quarter leaves about $27.4 million on 25,534 carats, an implied Q2 average near $1,073, roughly 43% below the Q1 blended rate; that subtraction is this desk's arithmetic and is not a company figure. For comparison, De Beers' realised price for the same half was $105 a carat on revenue of $1.58 billion, down 19%, with an underlying loss of $188 million, 23% narrower, as this paper reported on 31 July 2026.

Source — Gem Diamonds H1 2026 trading update, 28 July 2026 / Gem Diamonds Q1 2026 trading update via Mining Indaba and TipRanks / Carat Capital, 31 July 2026
Diamonds This week — August 17

Qatar Diamond Exchange signs the Singapore, Shanghai and Tokyo bourses, thirty-three days before saying so

The Qatar Diamond Exchange signed memoranda of understanding with the Diamond Exchange of Singapore, the Shanghai Diamond Exchange and the Tokyo Diamond Exchange. Each was signed by QDE director Ghanim Nasser Al Saadi with the counterparty's president: Fabio Cascapera, Lin Qiang and Michio Iwasaki respectively. The agreements frame member introductions, bilateral visits and reciprocal participation in tenders, auctions and viewings, and commit the parties to the World Diamond Council's System of Warranties and to Responsible Jewellery Council standards. None of the three has been published and none is reported to carry a volume commitment, fee schedule or term. The signings took place at the 41st World Diamond Congress in Singapore, held by the World Federation of Diamond Bourses and the International Diamond Manufacturers Association from 12 to 15 July 2026. QDE announced them in a statement dated Monday 17 August 2026, at least thirty-three days after the congress closed, and Rapaport carried the news on 23 August, day thirty-nine. This paper reported the exchange's own launch on 28 July 2026, day thirteen, three weeks before the agreements signed at that congress were made public. For scale, Dubai posted $41.7 billion of diamond trade in 2025. QDE operates at the Ras Bufontas free zone with sorting, vaulting, independent valuation and tender rights, and Qatar joined the Kimberley Process as a full member in 2021.

Source — Rapaport, 23 August 2026 / Gulf Times, 17 August 2026 / WFDB congress announcement / Carat Capital, 28 July 2026
Diamonds This week — August 17

Israel's first-half diamond trade breaks into four categories and the rough legs fell twice as fast

Figures published on 16 August 2026 by the Israeli financial broadcaster Emess, in a report by Gadi Fox, give the first categorical split of a half-year this paper had carried only in aggregate. Net polished exports were $625.7 million against $804.5 million a year earlier, down 22.3%. Net polished imports were $531.3 million against $609 million, down 12.7%. Net rough exports were $191.2 million against $292.6 million, down 34.6%, and net rough imports $208.3 million against $324.6 million, down 35.8%. Rapaport also carried the polished export figure at the same $625.7 million; neither outlet names a source, so the match is corroboration of one release rather than a second count. Grouped by category, the two polished legs fell 18.1% and the two rough legs 35.3%, a ratio of 1.94 on this desk's arithmetic. Rough imports were 40.3% of polished exports a year ago and are 33.3% now. These are net figures and are a different series from the $2.4 billion gross half-year export total, the lowest half on record, that this paper printed on 27 July 2026 from the Diamond Controller's Office. The release carries no monthly path and no unit volumes, and the United States tariff change of late July falls outside the period. Corrected 24 August 2026: the 10% rate was the Section 122 tariff, which sunset on or about 24 July; the measure that took effect that day was a new Section 301 forced-labour tariff, under which Israel's rate is 12.5%.

Source — אמס Emess, 16 August 2026 18:50, by Gadi Fox / Rapaport, The Diamond Pulse, August 2026 / Carat Capital, 27 July 2026
Diamonds This week — August 17

Okavango Diamond Company names Yoram Dvash board chairman

Okavango Diamond Company, Botswana's state-owned rough trading arm, has appointed Yoram Dvash chairman of its board, reported 20 August 2026. He succeeds Gape Kaboyakgosi, who left in November after five years, so the seat had been vacant for most of a year. Dvash stepped down as president of the World Federation of Diamond Bourses two months earlier, having held that office since 2020 and completed the maximum two consecutive three-year terms, and he was previously chairman of both the Israel Diamond Institute and the Israel Diamond Exchange. He said he looks forward to working with the board, management and the government of Botswana. Under the ten-year sales agreement signed with De Beers in February 2025, Okavango receives 30% of Debswana's production for the first five years and 40% for the second five, with a conditional five-year extension that would move the split to 50-50; the agreement in principle announced in July 2023 had projected ODC reaching 50% within the base term and the signed document does not. No revenue figure, tender calendar or strategic target was published with the announcement. This paper reported Dvash's succession at the WFDB by Mehul Shah on 15 July 2026 and Okavango's tender arrangement with the Dubai Diamond Exchange on 1 August 2026. Correction, 23 August 2026: an earlier version said Dvash remains chairman of the Israel Diamond Institute and the Israel Diamond Exchange; he was previously chairman of both.

Source — Rapaport, 20 August 2026 / National Jeweler, 26 February 2025 / Carat Capital, 15 July and 1 August 2026
Diamonds This week — August 17

Vasant Mehta, GJEPC chairman from 2008 to 2010, has died at 81

Vasant Mehta, a former chairman of India's Gem and Jewellery Export Promotion Council, has died at 81, reported 18 August 2026. He ran M/s V. Rameshchandra & Co in Mumbai. He was the council's vice chairman in 1991-92, vice chairman again in 2006-08 and chairman from 2008 to 2010, a term that spans the global financial crisis and the credit contraction it produced across India's cutting and polishing sector. He convened the council's banking, insurance and taxation committee for years afterwards and advised the legal committee of the Bharat Diamond Bourse. Internationally he represented Indian interests at the World Diamond Council, the World Federation of Diamond Bourses, the International Diamond Manufacturers Association, the World Jewellery Confederation and the Kimberley Process, and he served the Mumbai Diamond Merchants Association. Gaetano Cavalieri, president of the World Jewellery Confederation, described him as first and foremost a servant of the industry. This paper carries no independent verification of the date or circumstances of the death beyond the trade report and stated so in print.

Source — Rapaport, 18 August 2026
