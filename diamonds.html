B-01

Producers hold the line on rough

De Beers-style supply discipline is back: allocations trimmed, prices defended. Polished has stabilized after two brutal years — the question is whether midstream margins recover before credit patience runs out.

The 86% gap becomes the strategy

With lab-grown 1ct goods under $750, natural sellers have stopped competing on price and started competing on account of provenance, rarity and resale. Watch the marketing budgets, not the price lists.

Positioning · Global

Traceability goes from virtue to invoice

Origin platforms are no longer CSR decoration — G7 rules and retailer mandates are making sourcing paperwork a condition of sale. Compliant goods are starting to command a measurable premium.

Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
The SightTerm

De Beers' ten-times-a-year allocation sale, where approved buyers — sightholders — purchase rough in pre-assembled boxes at prices set by the producer. Refusing a box has consequences; so does needing one.

Four Cs vs. the fifthConcept

Carat, color, clarity, cut set the grade — but the trade increasingly prices a fifth C: certainty of origin. Two identical stones can part ways on price over a paper trail.

Midstream squeezeDynamic

Miners set rough prices, retailers set polished prices, and the cutters and traders in between absorb the difference. When both ends tighten at once, Surat and Antwerp bleed first — the industry's recurring crisis.

MemoPractice

Goods consigned to retailers on memorandum — sold before they're paid for. Memo keeps counters full and balance sheets fragile; its terms are one of the best barometers of trade confidence.

Diamonds This week — August 17 to September 2

The one-carat lab-grown reference falls $13 to $687, the largest single-day move and the lowest reading this tape has recorded

CaratRadar's average asking price for a one-carat lab-grown diamond read $687 on 1 September 2026, down $13 or 1.8571% from $700, on a page carrying its own dateline of 1 September and 1,204,718 active listings. This tape's record begins 5 August 2026, so both the low and the largest-move claims are scoped to twenty-one sessions. The series peaked at $711 on 8 August and fell in steps of one to three dollars thereafter (710, 707, 705, 704, 703, 701, 700); the largest previous single-day move was $3 on 14 August, or 0.4225%, making today's step 4.4 times larger. Cumulative fall from the 8 August high is $24, or 3.3755%. No natural comparison exists for the same date: this paper's RapNet Diamond Index mark is monthly and still dated 24 July 2026. The most recent natural data is Rapaport's Diamond Pulse published early August reporting July, in which one-carat prices reversed June's declines across nearly every category, K-M VS moving from a 0.1% loss to a 2.8% gain, D-F SI declines narrowing to 0.2% from 2.1% and G-J SI reversing to a 0.2% gain from a 0.3% decline.

Diamonds This week — August 17 to September 2

Antwerp's average exported polished carat reaches $2,666.92, up 17.34%, while its average exported rough carat falls 25.82% to $82.66

The Antwerp World Diamond Centre's General Statistics workbook for July 2026, last saved 26 August and downloaded and read directly by this desk, records polished exports for January-July 2026 of 1,581,439.42 carats worth $4,217,580,162, against 1,599,866.76 carats worth $3,636,115,968 a year earlier: 18,427.34 fewer carats (-1.15%) carrying $581,464,194 more (+15.99%). Rough exports ran 19,917,971.55 carats worth $1,646,417,080 against 17,960,158.83 carats worth $2,001,229,753: 1,957,812.72 more carats (+10.90%) for $354,812,673 fewer dollars (-17.73%). Per carat that is $2,666.92 polished against $2,272.76 (+17.34%) and $82.66 rough against $111.43 (-25.82%), widening the polished-to-rough ratio from 20.40x to 32.26x. Antwerp imported polished at $1,897.93 the carat and exported at $2,666.92, a mix difference of 40.52%; on rough it imported at $84.74 and exported at $82.66, down 2.46%. The July month alone shows polished exports at $2,182.56 the carat against $2,112.43, a rise of only 3.32%, while rough fell 20.12% to $92.70 - the polished surge is a first-half effect that is already fading.

Diamonds This week — August 17 to September 2

DMCC establishes a standalone Lab-Grown Diamond Vertical, publishing 2025 volume up 91.5% to 76.9 million carats on value up only 7.5% to $1.3 billion

Announced 30 August 2026. The two growth rates imply a 2024 base of 40.16 million carats worth about $1.209 billion, or $30.11 a carat, against $16.91 a carat in 2025 - a fall of 43.86% in the average value of a lab-grown carat moving through Dubai, on DMCC's own arithmetic. DMCC puts the rise since 2022 at 109% from 36.8 million carats and sizes the global sector at about $30 billion. Set against the emirate's full 2025 ledger as this paper reported it on 15 July ($41.7 billion on 359.5 million carats), lab-grown is 21.39% of Dubai's carats and 3.12% of its dollars; the average carat crossing Dubai was worth $115.99 against $16.91 for the average lab-grown carat. Ahmed Bin Sulayem, executive chairman and chief executive, said lab-grown diamonds now need dedicated market infrastructure.

Diamonds This week — August 17 to September 2

OFAC issues General License No. 104B, moving the grandfathered Russian diamond deadline to 1 September 2027

The United States Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued General License No. 104B on 27 August 2026, moving to 1 September 2027 a deadline that had stood at 1 September 2026, an extension of 365 days. The licence covers loose, nonindustrial Russian-origin diamonds purchased before the sanctions took effect and not yet entered into the American market. Stones of one carat and above must have left Russia before 1 March 2024; stones between 0.50 and 1.00 carat before 1 September 2024. General License No. 103, governing imports of diamond jewellery, is unchanged. Measured to the new deadline, a qualifying one-carat stone will have been outside Russia at least 1,279 days, three and a half years, and a qualifying half-carat stone at least 1,095 days, three years to the day. Neither the licence as reported nor any trade body reached by this desk publishes a carat weight, value or count for the population still waiting, so the extension cannot be priced. The licence number itself is the second amendment to General License No. 104. This desk fetched OFAC's own recent-actions listing on 29 August 2026; the page returned HTTP 200 but renders its results dynamically and produced no diamond entry to a plain fetch, so the licence detail is carried from Rapaport's report of 27 August 2026, which the bureau independently confirmed against the page's own publication metadata and against direct text matches on the licence number and the new date.

Source — Rapaport News, 27 August 2026; OFAC recent-actions listing, fetched 29 August 2026
