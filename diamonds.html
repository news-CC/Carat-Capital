Producers hold the line on rough

De Beers-style supply discipline is back: allocations trimmed, prices defended. Polished has stabilized after two brutal years — the question is whether midstream margins recover before credit patience runs out.

The 86% gap becomes the strategy

With lab-grown 1ct goods under $750, natural sellers have stopped competing on price and started competing on account of provenance, rarity and resale. Watch the marketing budgets, not the price lists.

Positioning · Global

Traceability goes from virtue to invoice

Origin platforms are no longer CSR decoration — G7 rules and retailer mandates are making sourcing paperwork a condition of sale. Compliant goods are starting to command a measurable premium.

Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
The SightTerm

De Beers' ten-times-a-year allocation sale, where approved buyers — sightholders — purchase rough in pre-assembled boxes at prices set by the producer. Refusing a box has consequences; so does needing one.

Four Cs vs. the fifthConcept

Carat, color, clarity, cut set the grade — but the trade increasingly prices a fifth C: certainty of origin. Two identical stones can part ways on price over a paper trail.

Midstream squeezeDynamic

Miners set rough prices, retailers set polished prices, and the cutters and traders in between absorb the difference. When both ends tighten at once, Surat and Antwerp bleed first — the industry's recurring crisis.

MemoPractice

Goods consigned to retailers on memorandum — sold before they're paid for. Memo keeps counters full and balance sheets fragile; its terms are one of the best barometers of trade confidence.

Diamonds This week — August 17 to 28

OFAC issues General License No. 104B, moving the grandfathered Russian diamond deadline to 1 September 2027

The United States Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued General License No. 104B on 27 August 2026, moving to 1 September 2027 a deadline that had stood at 1 September 2026, an extension of 365 days. The licence covers loose, nonindustrial Russian-origin diamonds purchased before the sanctions took effect and not yet entered into the American market. Stones of one carat and above must have left Russia before 1 March 2024; stones between 0.50 and 1.00 carat before 1 September 2024. General License No. 103, governing imports of diamond jewellery, is unchanged. Measured to the new deadline, a qualifying one-carat stone will have been outside Russia at least 1,279 days, three and a half years, and a qualifying half-carat stone at least 1,095 days, three years to the day. Neither the licence as reported nor any trade body reached by this desk publishes a carat weight, value or count for the population still waiting, so the extension cannot be priced. The licence number itself is the second amendment to General License No. 104. This desk fetched OFAC's own recent-actions listing on 29 August 2026; the page returned HTTP 200 but renders its results dynamically and produced no diamond entry to a plain fetch, so the licence detail is carried from Rapaport's report of 27 August 2026, which the bureau independently confirmed against the page's own publication metadata and against direct text matches on the licence number and the new date.

Source — Rapaport News, 27 August 2026; OFAC recent-actions listing, fetched 29 August 2026
Diamonds This week — August 17 to 28

Antwerp imported 1,590,026 carats of rough in July for $241,050,713, the lowest monthly volume in its five-year published series

AWDC's own machine-readable monthly series puts July 2026 rough imports at 1,590,026.12 carats for $241,050,713, an average of $151.60 a carat against June's $88.63, a rise of 71.05% on this desk's arithmetic. The published series runs from January 2022 and covers 55 complete months; July 2026 is the lowest of them, June 2026 (1,834,966.27 carats) the second lowest and May 2026 (1,989,371.16 carats) the third. Against July 2025 (3,118,013.12 carats for $414,238,026) carats are down 49.01% and value down 41.81%. The May to July three-month average is 5,414,363.55 carats for $634,237,819, or $117.14 a carat; the series high is $183.74 in April 2022. July exports were 2,751,303.49 carats for $255,048,440, $92.70 a carat, or 1.73 carats out for every carat in. The per-carat figure is a trade-flow average driven by mix, not a price index.

Source — AWDC, Antwerp diamond industry data, monthly rough series, read 28 August 2026
Diamonds This week — August 17 to 28

India's gem and jewellery exports to New Zealand rose 50.56% to $25.46 million, with gold jewellery carrying about 93% of the increase

GJEPC published FY2025-26 New Zealand figures on 27 August at the close of a delegation running 20 to 26 August: exports of $25.46 million against $16.91 million, up 50.56% (recomputed 50.5618%). Gold jewellery was $17.47 million, up 84%, back-solving to about $9.49 million a year earlier and a rise of about $7.98 million against a total rise of $8.55 million, or about 93%. Cut and polished diamonds $5.76 million, silver jewellery roughly doubled to $0.91 million, imitation jewellery up 13.16% to $0.43 million, lab-grown diamonds up 2% to $0.63 million. Named categories sum to $25.20 million of $25.46 million, 98.98%. The India-New Zealand FTA signed 27 April 2026 has not entered into force. Delegation led by Vijay Mangukiya; Consul General Madan Mohan Sethi attended. Council data on its own trade, labelled as such.

Source — GJEPC, Auckland visit release, 27 August 2026
Diamonds This week — August 17 to 28

GJEPC put nine customs grievances to Delhi's Chief Commissioner in Mumbai, and was asked for a flow chart of the rough trade

The Gem and Jewellery Export Promotion Council met Manish Kumar, Chief Commissioner of Customs, Delhi, at its head office in Mumbai on 24 August, following Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman's nomination of him to meet the trade. GJEPC fielded Kirit Bhansali, chairman; Shaunak Parikh, vice chairman; Anoop Mehta, convener of the diamond panel; Ashish Borda, convener of PMBD; Mital Doshi, convener of BITC; and Sabyasachi Ray, executive director. The Bharat Diamond Bourse sent vice president Mehul Shah and customs convener Anup Zaveri. This desk counts nine distinct grievances in the council's own account: rough valuation assessments; repeated examination including continued 100% checking at Mumbai Customs; underuse of the Risk Management System and of importer compliance history; inconsistency between Mumbai and Surat; absence of specialised trade expertise in valuation, on the argument that a general valuer cannot reach a transaction-level market value for rough; misdeclaration proceedings arising from minor weight or description differences; detention of consignment exports and re-imports over small variations; the need for better coordination between customs and other government agencies, with greater consistency of procedure and clearer guidance for officers handling the sector; and inconsistent interpretation caused by frequent personnel changes. Corrected 28 August 2026: this entry and the article first said eight, omitting the inter-agency coordination concern. Kumar agreed a risk-based approach could reduce unnecessary examination while keeping safeguards against revenue leakage, said the concerns would be examined with the Mumbai and Surat formations, and requested a consolidated flow chart of the rough diamond trade process plus a comprehensive list of issues. No consignment count, detention figure, clearance time, relief or timeline was published, and this desk estimates none.

Source — GJEPC, via Heera Zhaveraat and Solitaire International, 24 August 2026
Diamonds This week — August 17 to 28

A rough stone passes visual screening as natural at the Israeli Diamond Institute and is classified Type IIa by infrared

The Israeli Diamond Institute's technology department, in an article dated 25 August 2026 on its own site and bylined to staff gemologist Sergey Lazon, reports a rough stone submitted on Tuesday 18 August whose recorded data matched expected parameters and which externally appeared to be natural rough. Microscopy found small clouds and inclusions of the kind natural rough carries, together with minor defects the institute describes as atypical of natural diamond and consistent with CVD growth. A dedicated CVD screening device returned a preliminary indication of laboratory origin, which the institute itself qualifies by noting such devices can give ambiguous readings where inclusions are weak. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy returned what the institute calls an unambiguous result and classified the stone as Type IIa, the nitrogen-poor category most laboratory-grown diamond falls into and one the institute says is difficult to attribute to natural origin given this rough's structure and shape. The account gives no carat weight, measurements, colour, price or country of submission. It states that the Gemological Institute of America identified a comparable stone days earlier and attributes that to a report by IDEX Online rather than to the GIA directly, a claim hedged twice in the original and carried hedged here. Single-source: the testing laboratory describing its own test, with no independent outlet reporting it and no submitting party named.

Source — Israeli Diamond Institute, 25 August 2026 (Hebrew)
