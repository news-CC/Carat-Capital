Stuller puts jewellery stores at 26% of doors, 48% of dollars

Harold Dupuy of Stuller told the American Gem Society's Conclave that jewellery is taking share from big-box stores. The presentation published neither a period nor a denominator for the two shares.

By The Retail Desk18 September 2026Brief 20 sec · Full read 1 min
What changed

Jewellers punch above their count. Jewellery-only stores are 26% of national doors and about 48% of the dollars, Stuller's Harold Dupuy told the American Gem Society's Conclave in Orlando.

What it means · The Desk’s View

If you run a jewellery store: the share of dollars is nearly double the share of doors.

If you plan next year's travel: Conclave moves to Kansas City, 2 to 5 May 2027.

What this is not: a measured market share. No period or denominator was published.

The depthMethod, sources, corrections · open what you need
01What would change this call+

A published denominator. If the 26% counts only doors that sell jewellery rather than all US retail doors, the 48% is a much smaller claim than it reads as, and the gap between the two shares narrows.

02Why doors and dollars are different counts+

A door is one selling location. A dollar share is revenue. The two diverge whenever a category's average ticket differs from the market it sits inside, which is why a jewellery store with one location can carry the revenue of several general-merchandise doors. The useful version of this comparison names its universe: all US retail doors, all doors selling jewellery, or a panel of them. Without that, a gap between 26% and 48% is a direction and not a measurement, and it cannot be compared with any other year's figure.
