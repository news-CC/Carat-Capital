LVMH jewellery and watches rise 8% to €2.5bn in the quarter

The division grew eight times faster than the group in the three months to 30 September, when total LVMH revenue rose 1%. Nine-month divisional sales reached €7.73 billion, up 4%, against a group figure down 1.6%.

By Carat Capital

17 October 2026

What changed

The division outgrew the group. LVMH's jewellery and watches took €2.5 billion in the three months to 30 September, up 8%, while group revenue rose 1% to €18.5 billion. Over nine months the group fell 1.6%.

What it means

  • If you supply Tiffany or Bulgari: The division is the only segment LVMH names as improving, and it is 13.5% of group revenue in both reported periods on this paper's division.
  • If you read China exposure: LVMH attributes the quarter's softness to China and names the United States, Europe and Japan as improving. No country figures are given.
  • What this is not: A like-for-like read against the half year. The report carried here does not say whether the 8% is organic or reported, and the 9% printed in July was organic.

Eight against one

The quarter's two numbers. Jewellery and watches took €2.5 billion in the three months to 30 September, up 8%. Group revenue was €18.5 billion, up 1%.

Nine months diverge further. The division is up 4% at €7.73 billion while the group is down 1.6% at €57.15 billion. One line is growing and the whole is not.

Share stayed put. On this paper's division the segment is 13.5% of group revenue in the quarter and 13.5% across nine months. Growing faster has not yet changed its weight.

The figures tie out

Subtraction confirms it. Nine months at €7.73 billion less the €5.23 billion first half this paper printed in July leaves exactly €2.5 billion, matching the reported quarter.

The pattern repeats. At the half year the division grew about 9% organically while group revenue fell 3%. The third quarter repeats that shape at a smaller gap.

One label is missing. The report does not state whether this quarter's 8% is organic or reported growth. The 9% printed in July was explicitly organic.

What the company credits

Tiffany leads the list. LVMH names the HardWear, Knot and Sixteen Stone lines and a continuing store-renovation programme. The Blue Book 2026 Hidden Garden collection moved from New York to Venice.

Bulgari claims a record. The group says its Eclettica high jewellery and prestige watch line generated record revenue. No figure is attached to that claim anywhere in the report.

China is the drag. Softer activity in China is named as the limit on organic growth, against improvement in the United States, Europe and Japan. No country revenue is published.

What to watch

  • Late January 2027LVMH's full-year results, where the organic and reported splits for the division are published together.
  • OngoingWhether the division's 13.5% share rises. It has grown faster than the group for two reported periods without moving.
  • OngoingA figure behind Bulgari's record claim. The group has named a record and published no number for the line.

What would change this call

LVMH's own release. Every figure here is taken from one trade report of a company announcement, and the single most important qualifier, whether the 8% is organic or reported, is absent from that report. If the quarter's growth is reported rather than organic, it is not comparable with the 9% this paper printed in July, and the two-period narrative in this article weakens. The group's own document settles it and has not been read here.

Organic and reported, and why the gap matters at LVMH

Luxury groups publish two growth rates and the difference between them is rarely small. Reported growth is what the accounts show, including the effect of currency movements and of businesses bought or sold during the period. Organic growth strips both out and tries to say what the existing business did at constant exchange rates. For a group that sells roughly half its goods outside the euro and reports in euros, the gap between the two can run several points in either direction in a single quarter, which is enough to turn a decline into a rise or the reverse. LVMH ordinarily publishes both for the group and for each division, and this paper's July article on the half year was careful to attach the word organic to the 9% it printed for jewellery and watches. The report relied on tonight does not carry that label for the 8%, and this paper will not supply it. The practical consequence is narrow but real. The claim that the division outgrew the group eight times over in the quarter holds either way, because both figures come from the same report on the same basis. The claim that growth slowed from 9% to 8% does not hold, because those two numbers may be measuring different things, and that is why this article does not make it.
