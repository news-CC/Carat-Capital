$55,000 sapphire necklace leads Bonhams California on 24 September

Four sapphires of 3.25 to 5.65 carats sit in the top lot of a sale of more than 200 pieces. Tiffany, Hermes and David Webb fill out the estate names.

By The Saleroom Desk6 September 2026Brief 25 sec · Full read 1 min
What changed

The estate end prices. Bonhams sells more than 200 lots in California on 24 September. A multicoloured sapphire and diamond necklace carries the top estimate at $55,000, with four sapphires between 3.25 and 5.65 carats.

What it means · The Desk’s View

If you buy estate goods: this is the band a shop can actually restock from, not a trophy sale.

If you price signed pieces: a David Webb bangle at $25,000 to $35,000 is the comparable.

What this is not: a market. Five lots do not set an estate price level.

IThis is the restocking band

Five figures, not seven. The whole published top five sits between $25,000 and $55,000. A shop buying estate goods can bid in that band; it cannot bid in Hong Kong's.

Signed names carry it. Tiffany, Hermes and David Webb appear among the makers. A signature is what lifts an estate lot above the melt and stone value beneath it.

IIWeight is where the value hides

One suite, 97.45 carats. The three-piece gem suite holds 36.85 carats of emerald, 33.75 of ruby and 26.85 of sapphire, and is estimated at $35,000 to $55,000.

Diamonds price lower. A line necklace of 37.70 carats carries $25,000 to $35,000, under $1,000 a carat at the top of the range.

IIIWhat the preview does not say

No sale total. Bonhams publishes no aggregate estimate for the 200-plus lots, and the five named are a selection made for the trade press.

01What would change this call+

A hammer well above $55,000 on the top lot, with the suite following. That would make this a coloured-stone result rather than an estate-restocking sale, and the reading of it as a trade buying band would not hold.

02How an estate lot is estimated+

A house estimating estate jewellery starts from the intrinsic value of the stones and metal, applies a discount for the cost of breaking the piece up, then adds a premium for the maker's signature, the period and the condition of the mount. Unsigned pieces tend to be estimated close to intrinsic value, which is why a signed bangle carrying little diamond weight can be estimated alongside a necklace holding thirty-seven carats.

03Method · the desk’s arithmetic+

Estimates, carat weights and the lot count are Rapaport's, published 2 September 2026, reporting Bonhams' Fine Jewelry: California sale of 24 September 2026. The 97.45-carat figure is the sum of the suite's 36.85 carats of emerald, 33.75 of ruby and 26.85 of sapphire, computed by Carat Capital; the source gives them separately and also lists 5.85 carats of diamonds in the same suite, which is excluded from that sum. The under-$1,000-a-carat reading on the line necklace divides $35,000 by 37.70 carats and is Carat Capital's arithmetic. Rapaport publishes a single figure for the top lot rather than a range, and no aggregate estimate for the sale, so neither a low estimate nor a sale total is stated.
