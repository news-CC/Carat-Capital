De Beers pays 1bn pula into Botswana fund, JCK reports

The transfer is the initial payment under the 2023 deal, which Botswana said would total P10 billion over 10 years. JCK puts it at about $73 million.

By Carat Capital

22 October 2026

What changed

The first payment lands. De Beers transferred its initial contribution of 1 billion pula to Botswana's Diamonds for Development Fund on 21 October, JCK reports. JCK puts it at about $73 million.

What it means

  • If you follow Debswana's sales: Later payments are 5% of De Beers' share of the distribution, so they move with sales.
  • If you sell on Botswana provenance: The fund now has money and a board, but no named programmes.
  • What this is not: The P10 billion. Nine-tenths of the commitment is still to come.

The 2023 deal set the sum

Agreed three years ago. Botswana's minerals ministry said in July 2023 that De Beers would make an initial contribution of P1 billion to the fund.

Payments track sales. An annual base contribution of 5% of De Beers' share of the distribution follows, until the total reaches P10 billion over 10 years.

Pledged, then paid. De Beers' 2025 report had booked US$70m committed to the fund.

Governance came before money

A board first. JCK says the money arrived only after the fund's governance was in place. Akinwumi Adesina, a former African Development Bank president, chairs a 10-member independent board.

Plans still pending. The fund says it is completing its strategy and business plan. It aims to use the payment to draw in private, domestic and international capital.

Diamonds still pay for it

Exports lean on stones. Diamonds account for 62.3% of Botswana's export earnings, JCK reports. Diamond export revenue fell about 29% year on year in the first five months of 2026.

What to watch

  • Not datedThe fund's strategy and business plan, and its first named programmes.
  • Next annual paymentThe first 5% base contribution, and how far lower sales shrink it.
  • OngoingBotswana's talks to raise its De Beers stake, and whether the fund terms change with ownership.

What would change this call

A De Beers or Botswana statement giving a different sum or date for the transfer. Then the payment rests on JCK alone and is reprinted.

How the fund is paid

Under the 2023 statement of principles, De Beers pays an initial P1 billion, then a yearly base of 5% of its share of the distribution. Payments stop when the total reaches P10 billion over 10 years.
