Victorins lists over 250 lots for its 20 September sale

The Fredericksburg, Virginia house carries a Cartier Libre Noeud diamond watch at $52,500 to $105,750. A Patek Philippe Calatrava carries $27,000 to $53,850, both the house's own estimates.

By The Auction Desk

17 September 2026

What changed

The date is verified. Victorins Auctions lists its September Edition sale for 20 September on its own catalogue page, with over 250 lots of jewellery, watches and estate property.

What it means

  • If you buy at regional sales: five signed lots carry published estimate bands. Registration runs through LiveAuctioneers.
  • If you track Cartier resale: the Libre Noeud band is a spread of just over two times.
  • What this is not: a major-house sale. No result exists until 20 September.

The date checks out

Confirmed at the house. Victorins Auctions' own upcoming-catalogue page carries the September Edition sale at 20 September, 4pm.

One platform. The house directs bidding to LiveAuctioneers, where its next listed catalogue after this one is a separate Fall Edition.

Two watches carry the top

Cartier leads. A Cartier Libre Noeud 18K white gold diamond ladies' watch carries $52,500 to $105,750, the sale's highest published band.

Patek follows. A Patek Philippe Calatrava in 18K rose gold carries $27,000 to $53,850.

Jewellery tops at thirty thousand

Two at the mark. A Bvlgari Monete three-coin bracelet and a Cartier Byzantine bangle each carry $30,000 at the high end.

Wide bands throughout. Three of the five named lots carry high estimates at about twice their low, which leaves the result open.

What would change this call

A published result. Five estimate bands say what a house hopes for; a hammer price says what the market paid, and the two have parted company at this level before.

Why a high-over-low ratio is worth computing

An estimate band is a house's public statement of uncertainty. A narrow band, say a third above the low, says the specialist has comparable results and expects the lot to land close to them. A band at twice the low says the house does not know, usually because the piece is signed but not dated, or the condition is unreported, or there are few recent comparables for that exact reference. Three of the five lots here sit at about two times, which for a buyer means the low estimate is doing most of the work as a reserve signal and the high is closer to an aspiration. It is also why this page prints the ratio: the spread is the information the band carries beyond its two endpoints.

Method

The sale date was verified at the primary before anything else was written, per this paper's standing rule that an unverified date does not enter a what-to-watch list. Victorins Auctions' own upcoming-catalogue page was fetched on 17 September 2026 and reads September Edition: Vintage Jewels, Watches & Estate, Sep 20, 2026 4:00 PM. The house's LiveAuctioneers profile was fetched separately and lists a different, later catalogue, the Fall Edition, as upcoming; that is a second sale, not a contradiction, and the individual catalogue page returned a bot-protection error rather than a date, so no LiveAuctioneers date is printed here. The lot estimates come from Auction Daily's write-up of the house's release of 19 August 2026, attributed as house estimates rather than independent valuations. The five ratios in Table II are this desk's own division. Archive test, both periods named: zero hits for Victorins across the 382-article archive; the paper has covered regional-house jewellery and watch sales before, most recently Penzance on 2 September 2026 and Bonhams on 8 September 2026, both different houses and both completed sales rather than previews.
