Auctions

A 1983 Garrard 'Conundrum' egg makes £123,500, about four times its metal

A 22-carat gold Cadbury's 'Conundrum' egg, named Cuckoo Cuckoo and made by Garrard & Co. of London in 1983, sold for £123,500, about $166,600, at Batemans Auctioneers & Valuers in Stamford, Lincolnshire on 1 August 2026. Forbes reported the result on 8 August and the auction house describes it as a world auction record for a bejewelled egg of its type. The price is close to six times the pre-sale estimate and about eighteen times the £6,500 the egg fetched at its previous appearance; Forbes dates that earlier sale to 2018 while the Batemans catalogue entry for the same lot is dated July 2017, and this paper carries both readings rather than resolving them. The catalogue puts the egg at 323.6 grams of 22-carat gold, engraved and enamelled in translucent green, blue, orange and white with a cat, a weather vane, stars, mice, a castle and a treasure hunter finding a golden egg. At 91.7% fineness that is roughly 296.6 grams, or 9.54 troy ounces, of pure gold, worth about $41,400 at the $4,341.30 an ounce this paper's tape carries from Kitco's 5:00pm New York close on 7 August. That metal figure is derived by this desk from the stated weight and fineness and is not published by any source. The egg was one of twelve individually designed 22-carat eggs commissioned by Cadbury's in 1983 for a nationwide treasure hunt, with Perspex caskets buried across the United Kingdom each holding a clue to one egg; a thirteenth was made in secret. Greg Bateman, managing director at Batemans, called the eggs a fascinating piece of British cultural history.

Gold & Metals

The PBoC adds 640,000 ounces in July, its largest month since October 2023

The People's Bank of China increased its official gold reserves by 640,000 troy ounces, about 19.9 metric tonnes, during July 2026, published with the monthly reserve data on 7 August. Holdings rose to 76.08 million troy ounces at the end of July from 75.44 million at the end of June, roughly 2,366 tonnes against 2,346. It was the twenty-first consecutive month in which the bank reported a purchase and the largest single-month addition since the 740,000 ounces recorded in October 2023. The gold reserve was valued at $306.35 billion against $303.72 billion a month earlier, within total official foreign-exchange assets of $3.419 trillion, up from $3.416 trillion, putting gold at about 8% of the reserve. Set against this paper's reporting of 2 August, which carried World Gold Council figures showing central banks buying 289 tonnes in the second quarter, up 62%, with China taking 33 tonnes across those three months, July alone runs at close to double China's second-quarter monthly pace. The same World Gold Council data put first-half central-bank demand at 345 tonnes, the smallest first half since 2022's 241 tonnes, after a first quarter revised down to 57 tonnes. July was also the month gold closed at $4,041.70, up about half a per cent and its first monthly gain since February, so the increase in pace came into a market that had barely moved. At the $4,341.30 an ounce carried on this paper's tape, 640,000 ounces is worth about $2.78 billion, a figure derived by this desk from the published ounce count rather than reported.

Diamonds

India's lower house passes a fifteen-year tax exemption for foreign rough sellers

The Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 passed the Lok Sabha on 6 August 2026, proposing an income-tax exemption for qualifying non-resident companies selling rough diamonds in India between 1 October 2026 and 31 March 2041, a fifteen-year window. The exemption is conditional on the sale taking place inside a Special Notified Zone; the two relevant zones sit inside the Bharat Diamond Bourse in Mumbai and the Surat Diamond Bourse. The class of qualifying seller covers miners, brokers, aggregators, auction operators and sightholders, and a qualifying company must furnish information when required. The existing regime offers a safe harbour presuming taxable profit at 1.25% of gross revenue, with Indian corporate tax at rates reaching about 33% of profit where the safe harbour is not elected. The stated policy aim is to reduce the movement of rough and encourage direct sales within India, positioning Mumbai and Surat against Dubai and Antwerp. The Gem and Jewellery Export Promotion Council welcomed the introduction. The Bill has cleared the lower house only and requires Rajya Sabha passage and presidential assent before any exemption takes effect; no date has been set for a hearing in the upper house, which makes the 1 October start date an intention rather than a commitment. For comparison, this paper reported on 2 August that a single tender cleared at 100% in Dubai against 75% in Johannesburg, and on 15 July that Dubai's diamond trade was worth $41.7 billion in a year.
