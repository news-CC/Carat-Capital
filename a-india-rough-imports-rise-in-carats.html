India's rough imports rise 65.65% in carats, 15.14% in value

September's 130.17 lakh carats cost $1.05bn, GJEPC provisional data show. On Carat Capital's division, the average imported carat cost about 30% less than a year earlier.

By Carat Capital

19 October 2026 Corrected

What changed

Carats outran dollars. India's rough diamond imports rose 65.65% by weight in September but only 15.14% by value, to $1.05 billion, GJEPC provisional data show.

What it means

  • If you sell rough to India: Volume is back, value is not: the average carat cost about 30% less than a year earlier.
  • If you buy polished from Surat: More, cheaper rough is entering the cutting pipeline.
  • What this is not: A price index. A cheaper average can mean a cheaper mix.

Key figures

Rough imported, Septemberup 65.65% year on year130.17 lakh ct
Rough import valueup 15.14%$1.05bn
Average value per caratCarat Capital's division−30.5%
April–September caratsvalue down 16.81%592.24 lakh ct

Source: GJEPC master statement of provisional data for September 2026, dated 13 October 2026, and GJEPC's statement of 14 October 2026. First reported by Angel One on 15 October. Per-carat changes are Carat Capital's division.

Weight up, value lagging

A cheaper carat. Dividing value growth by volume growth, 1.1514 by 1.6565, gives 0.695. The average imported carat was worth about 30.5% less than a year earlier.

Six months agree. From April to September, carats rose 16.47% to 592.24 lakh while value fell 16.81% to $5 billion. One lakh is 100,000 carats.

Polished moved the other way

Natural exports fell. Cut and polished natural exports fell 5.44% to $1.29 billion in September, while six-month export volume rose 4.64%.

Lab-grown rose. Polished lab-grown exports rose 40.72% to $151.43 million in the month.

What to watch

  • Mid-NovemberGJEPC's October figures, which cover the pre-Diwali month and test whether the rough intake holds.
  • MonthlyAWDC's Antwerp figures for September, which show whether the rough trade moved the same way at the other end.
  • OngoingA rough-import line from GJEPC itself. Its 14 October statement carries exports only, so the rough figures remain Angel One's reading.

The story so far

What would change this call

A per-carat price from a producer or tender house showing September rough flat or higher. Then the 30% fall is mix, smaller and cheaper goods, not price.

How the import line is built

GJEPC compiles India's gem and jewellery trade from customs data and publishes provisional monthly totals by category, in dollars and in lakh carats. Rough imports carry no quality split, so value per carat mixes price with assortment.
