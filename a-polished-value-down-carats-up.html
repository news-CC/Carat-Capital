India's polished exports fall 4.93% in value, rise 3.23% in carats

Total gem and jewellery exports reached $2.30 billion in August, up 3.14% year on year on the council's figures. Studded gold jewellery added 51.22% to $642.85 million.

By The Diamond Desk20 September 2026Brief 20 sec · Full read 1 minCorrected · see The depth
What changed

Value and volume parted. India's cut and polished diamond exports fell 4.93% in value in August while rising 3.23% in volume, to 13.36 lakh carats.

What it means · The Desk’s View

If you sell polished into India's export trade: More carats moved for less money. The average realised value a carat fell 7.9%.

If you model Indian demand for rough: Volume held; the price the goods fetched did not.

What this is not: A September reading. These are August figures, published on 12 September.

IMore carats, less money

Eight points apart. Polished value fell 4.93% while polished volume rose 3.23%, a spread of just over 8 percentage points on the same goods in the same month.

The rate fell. At $692.00 a carat against $751.51 last August, the average realised value fell 7.92%.

IIStudded gold carried the total

One line rose half. Studded gold jewellery exports rose 51.22% to $642.85 million, the largest of the four lines read here.

The total still rose. Gross gem and jewellery exports reached $2.30 billion, up 3.14%, with polished diamonds falling inside it.

IIIThe print is eight days old

Published 12 September. GJEPC's August figures were reported on 12 and 13 September, and no later monthly print has been published since.

The depthMethod, sources, corrections · open what you need
01What would change this call+

A September print that reverses the split. One month in which value and volume move apart is a month, not a trend, and the next release tests it.

02How the series is built+

GJEPC publishes India's gem and jewellery export and import figures monthly, in US dollars and in rupees, broken into lines including cut and polished diamonds, rough diamonds, gold jewellery, studded gold jewellery, silver jewellery, coloured gemstones and lab-grown diamonds. Cut and polished diamonds are reported in both value and carat volume, which is what allows the two to be compared. The council reports gross exports, not net of re-imports, and reports a year-on-year change against the same calendar month of the prior year rather than against the previous month. Value is reported at declared export value, so a change in it mixes price and mix.

03Method · the desk’s arithmetic+

The four published figures come from GJEPC's August release as carried by Fashion Value Chain, which is the only one of the three outlets cited here whose text actually carries the headline $924.51m and -4.93%. KNN India carries the volume line, 13.36 lakh carats and +3.23%, and the gross and studded-gold lines, but not the polished-value figures. PressReader's copy of The Hindu International answers with a page but no article text without a subscription, so nothing can be read out of it. The lead figure is therefore single-outlet and is no longer described as corroborated. GJEPC's own statistics page is listed as the standing address for the series and is the primary the diamonds desk's own sweep failed to reach this month, which is why this print is eight days old rather than same-day. One figure here is not published anywhere: the implied value a carat. It is Carat Capital's division and is labelled as such in the key figures, in the table and in the table note, where the arithmetic is printed in full so it can be checked. The prior-year rate is reconstructed from the published percentage change rather than from a published 2025 value, and that reconstruction is stated rather than hidden. The comparative is printed on its base: 692.00 over 751.51 is 92.08% OF last August's rate and therefore 7.92% BELOW it. 04Corrections1 note

Citation corrected, 21 September 2026. As published on 20 September this article's method said three outlets carried the same numbers and credited The Hindu International and KNN India with the polished-value figures, with Fashion Value Chain read only for the studded-gold line. Fetched again on 21 September, KNN India's text carries no $924.51m and no -4.93% at all, and the PressReader copy of The Hindu International returns no readable article text without a subscription. Fashion Value Chain carries both figures in full and is the source for them. Every figure printed on 20 September was correct as published and none has changed; what was wrong was the description of how well corroborated they were. The table note has been corrected in the same step. Found by this paper's standards desk.
