Watches

Rolex made Drake a one-off. The only verified price is $500,000

A gem-set Daytona described as a Rolex piece unique reached Drake on 16 August. Rolex has published nothing. The one hard figure is the $500,000 a Drake-owned GMT-Master II fetched this year, five times dealer value.

By The Watches Desk

19 August 2026

What changed

A gem-set Rolex Daytona described as a one-of-one piece unique was announced by Drake on Instagram on 16 August and reported by JCK on 18 August.

What it means

  • The story the trade should take from this is the one nobody has written, which is that Rolex has made the same physical act worth a fortune in-house and worth a write-down everywhere else. That is a deliberate and well-defended position, and the gem-setting trade lives inside its consequences.
  • If the house is now doing pieces uniques for clients with public profiles, the interesting question is not what Drake's watch is worth. It is whether an owner who wants stones can one day ask Rolex for them, because the day that becomes a service rather than a gift, an entire aftermarket loses its reason to exist.
  • Until Rolex says something, that remains a question and this paper prints it as one.

Source: what is established and what is not

One priced watch, and one that is not

The bracelet runs baguette diamonds into light and dark blue sapphires, the bezel and lugs are sapphire-set, the dial and case sides are paved, and the caseback is engraved Freeze the World in blue enamel. No reference number has been published, no stone count, no carat weight and no price.

The only number anyone can stand behind here is five hundred thousand dollars. It belongs to a different watch.

That figure is worth the trade's attention because it was an actual transaction.

A Rolex GMT-Master II, reference 116758SANR, previously owned by Drake and seen in the video for a track from his 2011 album, sold earlier this year through the dealer Wind Vintage at $500,000, the full advertised ask, after four offers arrived shortly after listing.

EveryWatch data puts that at roughly five times the average dealer value for the reference. The watch was unpolished and complete with documents and box. Whatever the premium was for, it was not gem-setting: the reference is a factory yellow-gold and diamond model, and the multiple attaches to who wore it.

The same stones, two different values

What has not been established is the part the trade press has been repeating most confidently. This desk found no statement from Rolex on the Ice Gradient Daytona, and the claim that the watch is factory work rather than aftermarket rests on Drake's own post and on trade and enthusiast reporting of it.

One dealer publication that asserted the watch is entirely factory offered no documentation or certification alongside the claim, and the same piece named a prior one-off, a platinum Yacht-Master said to have been made for a Singaporean client in 2024, which if correct means this is not the precedent-breaker it is being called.

A value of two to five million dollars circulating with the story is explicitly one writer's ballpark on a watch that is not for sale, and this paper does not print it as a valuation.

The checkable trade economics sit in the gap between two Rolex positions rather than in the watch.

Rolex operates no bespoke programme for clients, and its documented service position is the reason the aftermarket gem trade exists at arm's length: modification performed outside its own network voids the warranty, and its service centres decline watches carrying non-original parts.

A stone set into a case by an outside jeweller therefore subtracts from what the house will do for that watch. The identical stone set into the identical case in Geneva does not.

Drake's own warning is the clearest statement of that asymmetry anyone has made this year: "Please do not get your local jeweler to recreate."

For the counter, the useful reading is about provenance rather than about Rolex. The one priced event in this story is a standard-reference watch that carried a celebrity's history and cleared five times the usual dealer number without any modification at all.

A jeweller taking in a piece with a name attached is pricing a document trail, not a bezel.

The Ice Gradient Daytona has no price and no reference and will almost certainly never trade, which makes it a marketing object; the GMT-Master II had all three and is the one a valuation can be built from.
