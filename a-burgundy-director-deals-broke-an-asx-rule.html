Burgundy board says US$50.6m director deals broke an ASX rule

Three 2025 advance-sale agreements sent rough to Choron BV, controlled by director Anshul Gandhi, without prior shareholder approval. ASX lists Burgundy as long suspended, with three periodic reports overdue.

By Carat Capital

21 October 2026

What changed

The board concedes the breach. Burgundy Diamond Mines' board acknowledged on 9 October that three 2025 rough-sale agreements with a director's company, worth about US$50.6 million, needed shareholder approval first.

What it means

  • If you hold Burgundy: Trading has been suspended since 29 September 2025. ASX's deadline for trading to resume is 29 September 2027.
  • If you lend against rough: Burgundy funded itself by pre-selling inventory to an insider.
  • What this is not: An underpricing finding. The expert found fair market value, but judged the dealings 'not fair but reasonable'.

Three pre-sales to a director

Advances bought the rough. Between May and July 2025, Choron BV paid Burgundy in advance for independently valued rough inventories, then took the diamonds. It also held security over the stones.

A director controlled Choron. Choron was a related party because Anshul Gandhi, a Burgundy director, controlled it. Each deal exceeded the approval threshold of about US$3.10 million, 5% of US$62.04 million in equity.

No vote came first. The agreements were signed on 31 May, 30 June and 24 July 2025 without prior shareholder approval.

Ratified after the fact

Suspension, then disclosure. Burgundy requested a trading suspension on 29 September 2025 and announced the breach on 7 October 2025.

Shareholders voted in April. An independent expert's report followed on 27 March 2026. Shareholders not associated with Choron ratified the dealings on 30 April 2026.

Governance review due. Burgundy is appointing a joint company secretary; its chief financial officer has held the role alone. ASX directed a further announcement, expected around 30 November.

ASX lists missing reports

Three reports outstanding. ASX's 16 October list of long-suspended entities names Burgundy. Its full-year accounts, annual report and half-year accounts, due 31 March, 30 April and 11 September 2026, are outstanding.

Two deadlines stand. The oldest report must be lodged by 31 March 2027, and trading must be able to resume by 29 September 2027. ASX removes entities that miss either.

Ekati went into receivership. Burgundy's Ekati mine in Canada's Northwest Territories was placed in court receivership in July 2026.

What to watch

  • About 17 NovemberBurgundy expects the outcome of its external governance review.
  • About 30 NovemberThe supplementary announcement ASX directed, on that review.
  • 31 March 2027ASX's deadline for the oldest overdue report, the full-year accounts.

The story so far

  1. 17 July 2026A British Columbia court placed Ekati in receivership after a sale process drew no viable bid.

What would change this call

Lodged accounts and a path to resumed trading before March. Then the breach closes as a remedied governance failure.

How ASX Listing Rule 10.1 works

A listed company needs shareholder approval before it disposes of a substantial asset to a related party, such as a director's company. Burgundy's notice puts substantial at 5% of equity interests.
