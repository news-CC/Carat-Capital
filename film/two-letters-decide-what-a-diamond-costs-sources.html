Simplification ledger

  • 'Two letters': a colour grade (D–Z) and a clarity grade (IF–I3) are each one or more characters; the film treats each grade as 'a letter' for the argument.
  • 'The same stone' in the cold open means the same specification (one carat, round brilliant, G colour, same cut) with clarity varied; the ladders are index aggregates applied to one observed price, not two specific stones.
  • 'About sixty per cent of the price': the two ladders read 62.5% and 59.6% top to bottom; each is spoken exactly where it is used and rounded only in the coda.
  • 'Most buyers cannot see': the list's own labels — SI1 'often eye-clean', the VS2→SI1 step 'invisible to the eye in most stones', G–J 'near colourless'. Not every stone; the words 'most' and 'often' are kept.
  • 'The lamps are moving': HRD Antwerp's Mumbai lab is a reopening with 30% more capacity, and GCAL's SEEPZ site certifies jewellery, not loose stones. Both are stated as such in 'wheels'.
  • 'Written in Mumbai and paid for in New York': the 55% figure is Antwerp's polished going to the United States, not New York specifically.
  • 'Seventy years': GIA's grading scales date from 1953 (73 years).
  • The receipt's retail figures are dated 30 July 2026; the trade legs are 14 September 2026. Both dates are spoken.

Figures declined

  • The CaratRadar 1 ct lab-grown reference on the tape ($655, 20 Sep) — a different specification and source from the list's $545; not mixed into the receipt.
  • India's August polished export print (value −4.93%, carats +3.23%, $692/ct) — natural goods of every size; not comparable to a one-carat retail figure and not attributed to lab-grown.
  • GIA's account of why the scale starts at D (A, B, C already used by older systems) — a seductive detail with no bearing on the argument.
  • Gaolathe's 'diversify or perish' line — a second quote; the house allows one, and Sheintal's is the one that names the argument.
  • A D-flawless-against-K-SI2 multiplied figure — the grid this paper will not print.
