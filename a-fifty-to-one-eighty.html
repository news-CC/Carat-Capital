Oak and Luna prices its Arad capsule from $50 to $180

The 24-piece styling kit tops out at panther-shape drop earrings in gold-plated brass. Cocktail rings run $120 in silver and $140 in gold tone, JCK reports, with agate, lapis and aventurine as the stones.

By Carat Capital

29 September 2026

What changed

The ceiling is $180. Oak and Luna priced a 24-piece Abby Arad capsule from $50 to $180. No piece in the kit is listed above $180.

What it means

  • If you sell to Gen Z: The whole range sits under $200, with gold tone and plated brass doing the work. Nothing in it needs a finance plan.
  • If you build a price ladder: Check your own entry point against $50 and your ceiling against $180. This kit has no step above that.
  • What this is not: Fine jewellery pricing. These are gold-tone and gold-plated brass pieces with agate, lapis and aventurine.

Six named prices, one ceiling

Fifty to one-eighty. The report names a $50 hat charm at the bottom and $180 panther-shape drop earrings at the top. The earrings are gold-plated brass with tassel chains.

Rings fill the middle. A gold-tone stacking ring is $110, and cocktail rings run $120 in silver and $140 in gold tone. Agate, lapis and aventurine are the stone options.

One piece does two jobs

Necklace, then belt. The $150 Tassel Tie necklace is styled as both a necklace and a belt. A second use is a second reason to carry the same unit.

Movement is the pitch. Tassels recur through the collection because they move with the wearer. Oak and Luna says "clothes get you halfway there; jewelry finishes the look".

A capsule sold as a kit

Twenty-four pieces. The collection is presented as a styling kit of 24 pieces rather than as separates. Abby Arad worked on it with the brand for nearly a year.

Personalisation included. A Gothic Letter necklace on a black cord carries an oversize initial. Personalised product raises the cost of a return.

What to watch

  • Q4 2026Whether the under-$200 ceiling holds through the holiday season or a higher tier is added.
  • OngoingGold-tone and plated-brass pricing, which moves with metal only indirectly, unlike solid-gold ladders.
  • OngoingWhether other stylist collaborations land at the same $50 entry point.

What would change this call

A higher tier. The ceiling is the claim here, and it is drawn from six named prices out of twenty-four pieces. A solid-gold or diamond-set piece inside the same kit above $180 would remove the ceiling and make this an ordinary range rather than a deliberate cap.

Why a ceiling matters more than an average

An average price tells a brand what it took per order. A ceiling tells it who can buy without deciding. Below roughly two hundred dollars a jewellery purchase behaves like an accessory: it is bought on impulse or as a gift, it does not get discussed with a partner, and it does not wait for a payday or a discount code. Above that line, conversion drops sharply and the sale starts to need the machinery of considered purchase, finance options, saved carts, retargeting and a returns policy generous enough to remove the risk. That is why a range with a hard top is a distribution decision rather than a pricing accident. The materials follow from it: gold tone and gold-plated brass keep the ceiling intact where solid gold at these weights would breach it immediately, and semi-precious stones such as agate and lapis do the visual work that a diamond would price out of the range.
