Botswana targets US jewellery exports on a 0% import tariff

Minerals minister Bogolo Joy Kenewendo told Rapaport News the country wants a direct route to the American market. She spoke during Climate Week NYC, which its organiser dates 20 to 27 September.

By Carat Capital

29 September 2026

What changed

Botswana names a lever. Bogolo Joy Kenewendo said Botswana will use its 0% US import tariff to grow jewellery exports to the United States. She spoke during Climate Week NYC.

What it means

  • If you import from Botswana: The push is finished jewellery, not rough or polished. A 0% line is the stated advantage over competing origins.
  • If you manufacture in India or Dubai: A tariff-free origin competing on the same US shelf changes the landed comparison. Check your own duty line against zero.
  • What this is not: A new tariff, a figure, or a target. No export volume, value or date was given.

The tariff is the argument

Zero into America. Kenewendo named Botswana's 0% US import-tariff rate as the reason to push finished jewellery there. She said the country wants a more concerted route to the American market.

Made in Botswana. She said manufacturers inside the country are already exporting to the United States. No volume or value was attached to that.

Diamonds paid for the schools

Revenue became policy. Kenewendo tied diamond revenue to legislation letting every child finish high school, and to university places without student loans. She gave no figures for either.

Diversification alongside. She described diamonds as the cornerstone of the economy while the country pursues its Botswana Economic Transformation Programme.

Bounded, not dated. The remarks are placed at Climate Week NYC, which its organiser dates 20 to 27 September. The outlet describes the interview as last week.

No document behind it. This is an interview, not a policy release. No tariff schedule, gazette or trade notice was published alongside it.

What would change this call

A tariff line. The claim rests on one minister's characterisation of a rate in an interview. A published US schedule entry showing a duty above zero on Botswana jewellery, or a Botswana notice setting out the terms, would either confirm the lever or remove it.

Why a 0% line matters more downstream than upstream

Rough and polished diamonds already move across most borders at low or no duty, so a tariff exemption changes little for a miner or a cutter. Finished jewellery is different. It is a manufactured good, it attracts duty in many markets on the full value of the piece rather than on the stone alone, and that duty is charged on top of gold, labour and margin. A few percentage points on the landed value of a finished ring can decide whether a producing country can sell it into a foreign retail chain at all, or whether it is cheaper for that chain to buy the stone loose and set it somewhere else. This is the whole logic of beneficiation, the policy Botswana has pursued for two decades: capture the cutting, then the setting, then the brand, so the value stays where the stone came out of the ground. A zero duty line into the largest jewellery market is the single external condition that makes the last step arguable.
