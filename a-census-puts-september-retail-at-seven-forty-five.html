Census puts September retail sales at $745.0bn, up 0.8%

Sales were 6.3% above September 2025. A 28 September annual revision restated August at $739.3bn, against $773.9bn first published, and the release carries no jewellery line.

By Carat Capital

19 October 2026

What changed

Spending rose again. US retail and food services sales rose 0.8% in September to $745.0 billion, the Census Bureau's advance estimate shows. August's gain was revised up to 1.3%.

What it means

  • If you compare months: Use the revised series: August is $739.3 billion, not the $773.9 billion first printed.
  • If you sell jewellery: There is no jewellery line. NRF's clothing and accessories group, which includes jewellery, rose 2.2% on the year.
  • What this is not: A weaker August. Census revised its history.

The series was rebased

History moved on 28 September. Census revised monthly retail estimates using its 2023 and 2024 Annual Integrated Economic Survey. The August release now says it no longer carries the latest estimates.

The year looks firmer. Sales were 6.3% above September 2025. July to September ran 5.5% above the same months a year earlier.

August sits lower. August stands at $739.3 billion in the October release, 4.5% below the $773.9 billion first printed.

Jewellery has no line

No category exists. The advance release publishes no jewellery store category, so the trade reads it only as a measure of overall demand.

NRF's proxy rose. NRF's card-based data put clothing and accessories, which includes jewellery, up 2.2% on the year and 0.5% on the month, Rapaport reports.

What to watch

  • 17 NovemberOctober's advance estimate, the first month of the holiday quarter.
  • November–DecemberWhether holiday spending tracks NRF's $1.013 trillion forecast.
  • OngoingFurther revisions to August and September in the next two releases.

What would change this call

A large downward revision to September in the 17 November release. That would turn a second monthly gain into a flat quarter.

How the advance estimate is built

Census surveys a sample of retailers about two weeks after each month ends and publishes an advance estimate, adjusted for season, holidays and trading days but not for prices. It is revised in the following two releases and rebased each year on the annual survey.
