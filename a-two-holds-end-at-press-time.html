Gold & Metals

Platinum's $1,693 and palladium's $1,140 end two holds

Both marks were held at 05:00 when Kitco and a second board disagreed. An hour later the two pages agree and the holds end. Gold sheds $21.70 and silver 11.5 cents.

By The Gold & Metals Desk

12 October 2026 Corrected

What changed

Two held marks clear. Platinum and palladium could not be marked earlier this morning because two price pages disagreed on direction. An hour later both pages agree, at $1,693.00 and $1,140.00.

What it means

  • If you price platinum group metals: The $1,685.00 platinum mark and the $1,132.00 palladium mark are both superseded. The press-time figures are the printed ones.
  • If you read a single price board: One board showed palladium unchanged for four straight reads while the metal was trading. A second board caught it.
  • What this is not: A close, a fix or a settlement. These are spot bids off a live board at one minute of an open session, not a session close.

Source: Carat Capital's own press-time reading of the Kitco board at 06:05 EDT on 12 October 2026, corroborated on the CNBC quote webservice at 06:05 EDT. All four figures are the bid, the convention Carat Capital has carried since 31 August 2026. Prior marks are the paper's own printed marks of 11 October, looked up with tools/prev-mark.py rather than derived.

Two pages had to agree

The rule is simple. A mark is printed only when a second independent page corroborates the first. At 05:00 platinum failed that test and palladium looked frozen.

Platinum split the pages. Kitco read platinum's bid at $1,686.00, its own change field up a dollar. CNBC read $1,686.16, its change field down $1.05. The levels agreed; the directions did not.

Palladium would not move. Kitco's palladium cell printed bid $1,132 and ask $1,172 with zero change for four consecutive reads through an open session.

An hour changed both

Platinum cleared cleanly. Kitco now reads platinum bid $1,693 and ask $1,703, against CNBC's $1,694.26, inside the band. Both pages read the metal higher.

The palladium cell moved. Kitco's palladium reads bid $1,140, ask $1,180, with a session high of $1,188 and a low of $1,127. The cell is live, not stale.

CNBC agreed there. CNBC reads palladium at $1,156.49, up $8.68, inside Kitco's band. Both pages put the metal up about three-quarters of a percent.

Gold and silver fell

Gold shed twenty-one. Gold's bid reads $4,171.90, which is $21.70 below the 11 October mark. CNBC reads $4,173.22, inside the band.

Silver barely moved. Silver's bid reads $60.584, down 11.5 cents on the mark. An hour earlier it read $60.400, so the metal recovered through the hour.

The session is open. CNBC reported regular market status at 06:05 with a distinct timestamp for each metal. No closure is claimed for any of the four.

What to watch

  • Tomorrow, 05:00 ETWhether palladium's Kitco cell keeps moving. Four flat reads through an open session was the reason it was held.
  • OngoingWhether the platinum divergence returns. It resolved inside an hour and the same conflict held the mark on 18 August.
  • OngoingWhether Kitco's palladium bid-ask band narrows from $40. A band that wide can contain a conflicting print and hide a disagreement.

CNBC's reading was therefore sixteen cents ABOVE Kitco's bid and inside that board's $1,686.00–$1,696.00 band, not below it. The two pages did disagree in their own change fields, +1.00 against −1.0507 on a previous close of 1,687.2109, and that is what the hold rested on; they agreed on the level, and the figures this article gave for the disagreement were wrong. The bad numbers came from this desk's own tape note of 12 October rather than from either source page, and this paper cannot reconstruct how they arose. What would change this call

A third page. Both resolutions here rest on two sources agreeing, and two can be wrong together, particularly when one board's bid-ask band is $40 wide and can swallow a conflicting print. A London fix, an exchange settlement or a dealer's own screen would each be a harder test than a second web board. These are also spot bids at a single minute of an open session: all four will have moved by the close, and nothing printed here is a settlement.

What the two-page rule does and what it costs

The rule is that no price goes into print on one source. A second, independent page must corroborate the first, and the test is whether that second page's reading falls inside the first page's own bid-ask band and agrees on the direction and rough size of the move. When it does not, the previous printed mark stands and the age of that mark is disclosed. The cost is visible this morning: at 05:00 two of the four metals could not be marked, and a reader checking the board would have seen a platinum figure three days old beside two fresh ones. The benefit is also visible, and it is the palladium case. A board cell that has frozen looks exactly like a market that has not traded, and nothing inside that one page can tell the two apart. The second page showed palladium ticking while the first showed it flat, which is enough to withhold a mark but not enough to replace it, because a wide band can contain a nearby live print by accident. An hour later the first board moved $8 and the question answered itself. The rule did not identify which page was right. It identified that the question was open, which is the whole job.
