Watches

Cartier Portico No. 4 sells for CHF 4.8m, 22.5% above No. 3

The 1924 mystery clock hammered at CHF 3.9 million against a CHF 3.6 million high estimate at Phillips in Geneva. The house says every lot in the two-day sale sold, and calls the clock a Cartier record.

By Carat Capital

9 November 2026 Corrected

What changed

The clock cleared its estimate. Phillips sold the Cartier Portico Mystery Clock for CHF 4,815,000 in Geneva on 8 November, including premium. Its CHF 3.9 million hammer was 8.3% above the high estimate.

What it means

  • If you value Cartier objects: No. 4 made 22.5% more than No. 3's CHF 3,932,000 of May 2025, both with premium.
  • If you consign to Geneva: Phillips says every lot sold, for CHF 92,045,568.
  • What this is not: a price trend. It is one clock against one sister, at one house.

The hammer beat the estimate.

Estimates exclude premium. Phillips sets estimates on the hammer, so the hammer is the like-for-like test. It cleared the CHF 3.6 million high estimate and made 2.2 times the CHF 1.8 million low.

Premium added CHF 915,000. The house's schedule is cumulative and took the price to CHF 4.8 million. Carat Capital's arithmetic on it matches the gap between the hammer and the Sold For to the franc.

No. 4 outsold its sister.

Six were made. Cartier made six Portico mystery clocks between 1923 and 1925, Phillips says. No. 4, from 1924, is the only one with a hexagonal dial.

Last offered in 1996. The clock belonged to Alain Cartier, grandson of Louis Cartier, and was last offered in 1996, Phillips says. The house calls the result a world auction record for any Cartier timepiece.

Phillips counts a CHF 92m sale.

Phillips says all sold. The house says all 217 lots sold for CHF 92,045,568, including premium. It calls it the first live watch auction above US$100 million.

The lots add up. The 216 Sold For prices on the sale page sum to CHF 92,045,567, one franc under the house figure. Lot 189, a box offered on a condition, reads Sold on its page with no price.

Serial 022 sold. The F.P. Journe Résonance serial 022 sold for CHF 4,936,000 in the same session.

Journe led the makers. Phillips says 13 F.P. Journe watches made CHF 31.8 million across the sale.

What to watch

  • 13 November 2026Richemont, which owns Cartier, reports interim results. The test: what the group says about Cartier.
  • Next Cartier clock at auctionThe next Portico or mystery clock offered. The test: whether it, too, hammers above its high estimate.

Clarified, 10 November 2026: a bold lead-in said 'Every lot sold.' in this paper's voice. That is Phillips' claim, and lot 189's page reads Sold with no price, so the lead-in now says 'Phillips says all sold.' What was right as published: every price and total on the page.

What would change this call

One clock is one room of bidders. If the next Cartier mystery clock offered hammers inside its estimate, this result reads as a single contest for a rare object, not a re-rating of Cartier's 1920s clocks.

How a Phillips price is built

The hammer is the winning bid. Phillips adds a buyer's premium on a cumulative basis: 27% of the hammer up to CHF 1,600,000, 21% of the portion from there to CHF 7,000,000, and 14.5% above. For lot 220: 0.27 x 1,600,000 = 432,000; 0.21 x (3,900,000 - 1,600,000) = 483,000; 432,000 + 483,000 = 915,000; 3,900,000 + 915,000 = 4,815,000. Estimates are set on the hammer.
