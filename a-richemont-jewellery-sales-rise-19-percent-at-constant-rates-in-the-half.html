Richemont jewellery sales rise 19% at constant rates in the half

The Maisons' operating result rose to €3.2 billion from €2.5 billion, a 35.3% margin, or 33.4% excluding one-time items. Group sales were €12.3 billion in the six months to 30 September.

By Carat Capital

13 November 2026 Corrected

What changed

Jewellery carried the half. Richemont's jewellery sales rose 19% at constant rates in the six months to 30 September. The Maisons' operating result for the half rose to €3.2 billion from €2.5 billion.

What it means

  • If you supply gold to the trade: Richemont says raw materials, notably gold, largely drove costs, mostly offset by price rises and mix.
  • If you sell watches: watchmakers grew 12% at constant rates in the September quarter, after 8% in June's.
  • What this is not: a clean profit line. Group operating profit was €3.1 billion, or €2.9 billion excluding €229 million of one-time items. The Maisons' margin excluding such items was 33.4%.

Double digits except Middle East.

Four houses, 19%. Buccellati, Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels and Vhernier grew sales 19% at constant rates in the half and 14% in the second quarter.

Margin 35.3%, or 33.4%. The Maisons' operating result was €3.2 billion. Their margin was 35.3%, or 33.4% excluding positive one-time items.

Group at €12.3 billion. Group sales were €12,282 million, about €12.3 billion. Jewellery grew by double digits in every region except Middle East & Africa, Richemont says.

Price rises mostly offset costs.

Raw materials, notably gold. Higher raw material prices, notably gold, largely drove higher production costs. Price increases over the past year and a better sales mix mostly offset them, Richemont says.

Reported gross margin rose. Group gross margin was 66.5%, up 120 basis points, helped by €229 million of positive tariff-related one-time items.

Underlying gross margin fell. Excluding those items, gross margin fell 70 basis points. Currency moves also weighed, the company says.

Watches picked up in Q2.

Q2 up 12%. Specialist Watchmakers' sales were €1.7 billion in the half, up 10% at constant rates and 12% in the second quarter.

Profit at €150 million. The watchmakers' operating result was €150 million, from €50 million in the same half a year earlier.

Not linear, it warns. Chairman Johann Rupert says recovery is unlikely to be linear in an environment that stays challenging for the industry.

  • From 13 November 2026, dailyGold on the 05:00 ET tape, $3,899.10 on 13 November. The test: whether the cost Richemont names keeps rising.
  • Year to 31 March 2027, results after the year endThe second half. The test: whether jewellery holds double-digit growth at constant rates.

Corrections1 note

Corrected, 14 November 2026: the 'What this is not' line said that excluding €229 million of one-time items, 'it was €2.9 billion', under a page whose headline figure is the Jewellery Maisons' €3.2 billion operating result. The €2.9 billion and the €229 million are Richemont's Group figures; the release gives the Maisons' underlying result only as a margin, 33.4%. The line now names the Group figure and the Maisons' margin. What was right as published: every figure.

What would change this call

If jewellery growth at constant rates falls to single digits in the second half, the first half's 19% would read as a peak, not a pace.

What constant rates means here

Richemont restates this year's sales at last year's exchange rates to show growth without currency moves. Group sales rose 16% as reported and 17% at constant rates.
