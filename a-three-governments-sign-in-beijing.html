Diamonds

Shanghai Diamond Exchange signs Beijing pact with three governments

Botswana, Namibia and South Africa signed alongside the exchange in Beijing on 15 September. De Beers chief executive Al Cook witnessed it, and eight Chinese retail brands attended without signing.

By The Diamond Desk

17 September 2026

What changed

Three producer states signed. The Shanghai Diamond Exchange, Botswana, Namibia and South Africa signed the Beijing Declaration on Natural Diamonds and Sustainable Development in Beijing on 15 September. It sets no target, spend or volume.

What it means

  • If you buy rough from Africa: the three signatory states are your suppliers, and they want origin told at the Chinese counter.
  • If you sell natural diamonds in China: nothing changes at the till yet. No programme or budget is published.
  • What this is not: a De Beers agreement. Al Cook witnessed the signing.

The signature line is narrow

Three states, one exchange. The release names the Shanghai Diamond Exchange and the governments of Botswana, Namibia and South Africa as parties. Members of the Chinese and international diamond industry joined them.

De Beers watched. Al Cook witnessed the signing for the global industry, alongside the Natural Diamond Council and the World Diamond Council. He welcomed “the Beijing Declaration as a new framework”.

Eight retail brands attended

Attendance, not signature. Chow Tai Fook, Lao Feng Xiang, Chow Sang Sang, Lukfook Jewellery, CHJ Jewelry, Chow Tai Seng, Sino Gem and Kimberlite were represented at the ceremony.

The count matters. The release calls them major Chinese jewellery retail brands. It does not say they signed, and none is listed among the declaration's four named key advocates.

No number is published

Vision, not budget. The declaration sets out consumer education on origin, support for local value creation in Africa, and market data cooperation. No spend, date or volume is attached.

Follow-up is promised. Signatory governments and industry representatives will continue exchanges to turn the vision into concrete actions, the release says. It names no schedule.

What to watch

  • Q4 2026Any budget, date or volume attached to the consumer education commitment.
  • 2027The next Forum on China-Africa Cooperation, the framework the declaration invokes.
  • OngoingWhether any of the eight retail brands signs rather than attends.

What would change this call

A signed commitment from one of the eight retail brands, or a published budget. Either would turn a declaration of intent into a programme that can be measured.

What a declaration of this kind is, and is not

A declaration is a statement of shared intent between parties. It is not a contract, it creates no obligation enforceable against a signatory, and it carries no consideration. What it does is fix a public position that later agreements can be built on and that the parties can be held to rhetorically. That matters here because the signature line and the guest list are doing different work: the three producer governments have committed their names to a position on origin disclosure in the Chinese market, while the retail groups that would have to carry that disclosure to a customer have only attended. Reading the two as one group is the error this page is written to avoid.

Method

The primary was read directly: the Media OutReach Newswire release of 17 September 2026, which is the issuing document, in addition to JCK's write-up of 16 September. The release's three categories are quoted as it draws them. It says the exchange signed “in collaboration with Government representatives from Botswana, Namibia and South Africa”, that “the signing was witnessed by” Al Cook, Amber Pepper and Emma Wade-Smith, and that the eight retail brands “were also represented at the signing ceremony”. A later paragraph refers to “signatory governments”, which confirms the three states as parties. Neither De Beers nor any retail brand is described as a signatory anywhere in the release, so this page does not print them as one. The signing date is the release's own “Sept. 15th”, consistent with JCK's “took place Tuesday” under a Wednesday 16 September byline. Archive test, both periods named: a search of the 382-article archive returns zero hits for Beijing Declaration and three for Shanghai Diamond Exchange, all opened. Those three cover Doha's bourse agreements of August 2026, a chip-substrate story and the July 2026 WFDB presidency, none of which covers a China-Africa declaration in September 2026. Different entity or different period in every case, so NEW.
