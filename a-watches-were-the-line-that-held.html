Fossil's sales fell 4.9%, and watches were the line that held

Fossil's quarter to 4 July shows net sales down 4.9% to $209.7 million. Traditional watches fell 1.5%, leathers fell 31.1% and jewellery fell 11.2%. Across six months, traditional watch sales rose.

By The Watches Desk · 2026-08-26 · 5 min read

§1A top line that circulated, and a table nobody read.

Fossil Group's quarterly report for the thirteen weeks to 4 July 2026, filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission on 13 August, puts net sales at $209.7 million against $220.4 million in the comparable quarter to 5 July 2025. That is $10.7 million less, a fall of 4.9% on this desk's arithmetic against the company's own table of net sales by product class. The filing is thirteen days old and is reported here for its composition, which this desk has not seen read anywhere in the trade press, rather than for a top line that has already circulated.

§2The decline is in leather and jewellery, not in watches.

The composition inverts the way the number has been described. Traditional watches, much the largest line, fell from $178.4 million to $175.7 million, which is $2.7 million or 1.5%. Smartwatches, now a rounding item, went from $1.40 million to $1.39 million. Total watches fell 1.5%, from $179.8 million to $177.1 million. The two lines that actually fell were leathers, down from $16.9 million to $11.7 million, a drop of $5.3 million or 31.1%, and jewellery, down from $19.4 million to $17.2 million, which is $2.2 million or 11.2%. Leathers alone account for roughly half of the company's entire decline in the quarter, out of a line worth less than 6% of sales.

The watches held. The leather goods lost a third.

The Watches Desk

§3Across six months the watch line rose.

Across six months the watch line is not falling at all. Traditional watch sales for the twenty-six weeks to 4 July came to $364.5 million against $363.1 million a year earlier, which is $1.4 million more, a rise of 0.4%. Total watches were down $0.9 million, or 0.25%, on a base of $368.5 million, which is flat in any practical sense. Half-year net sales still fell 4.2%, from $453.7 million to $434.4 million, because leathers dropped 34.6% and jewellery 10.3% over the same period. A company whose watch revenue is level and whose accessories revenue is down by a third is not a watch story with accessories attached.

The mix shift is the part a jeweller should read. Watches were 81.6% of Fossil's quarterly sales a year ago and are 84.5% now, on the company's own percentages. Jewellery slipped from 8.8% to 8.2% of sales and leathers from 7.7% to 5.6%. The watch business is not merely holding, it is taking share of a shrinking company.

On the jewellery line itself the reading is plainer and worse: $17.2 million in a quarter, down 11.2%, and $37.4 million across the half, down 10.3%. That is a fashion-jewellery business contracting at roughly a tenth a year while the same firm's watches sit level, which is a data point about the accessory jewellery counter rather than about watches.

The Desk’s ViewWatches

This filing has been passed around as evidence that the American mid-market watch buyer is in trouble, and the company's own table does not support it. The watch line fell 1.5% in the quarter and rose 0.4% across six months. What is in trouble at Fossil is leather goods, down by a third, and fashion jewellery, down by a tenth, in a season when gold on this paper's own tape has gone from $4,482.20 on 20 August to $4,622.30 this morning, a rise of $140.10 or 3.13%.

The transferable point is about substitution at the low end of the counter. When metal runs, the branded fashion-jewellery shelf is the first thing a shopper skips and the watch is close to the last, because a watch is bought as an object and a plated pendant is bought as an impulse. Fossil's quarter reads as that, not as weakness in watches.
