Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
HallmarkTerm

The assay office's stamped guarantee of fineness — 750 for 18k, 916 for 22k. The oldest consumer-protection system in the world, and the inspiration for this paper's own mark.

Making chargePractice

The labor and design fee added over the metal's melt value — the jeweler's actual margin. When gold spikes, making charges get squeezed first; watch them to see who holds pricing power.

Fineness ladderConcept

24k is pure; 22k, 18k, 14k, 10k and 9k trade purity for durability and price. Bull markets in bullion push whole countries down a rung — India's 18k boom is this cycle's signature.

Melt valueMetric

What a piece is worth as raw metal, ignoring craft entirely. The gap between melt and retail is where brand, design and trust live — and it's the number every buyback desk starts from.

Gold falls $150.10 to $4,454.40 and silver 5.85% to $66.253, the worst session on this paper's tape

The Editor re-read all four metals on Kitco at press time on 29 August 2026 and wrote nothing back: gold $4,454.40 the ounce, silver $66.253, platinum $1,819.00 and palladium $1,405.00, each identical to the 05:00 mark filed by the tape desk. Today is Saturday, spot metal does not trade until Sunday evening, and Kitco's board carries an internal timestamp of 13:00 ET Friday across all four, so these are Friday's last prints read live today and confirmed unchanged rather than a new session. Against Thursday's marks of record ($4,604.50, $70.37, $1,883.00 and $1,381.00) gold fell $150.10 or 3.2604%, silver $4.117 or 5.8507%, platinum $64.00 or 3.3989%, and palladium rose $24.00 or 1.7378%. The next-worst gold session on that record is 27 August at 0.9259%, making Friday 3.52 times worse; the next-worst silver session is 19 August at 2.89%, making Friday 2.02 times worse. Silver set the tape's high-water mark of $70.37 on Thursday and gave 5.85% back on Friday. Gold's high-water mark, $4,643.90 on 24 August, now sits $189.50 or 4.0806% above the mark. Kitco's Friday ranges: gold $4,444.80 to $4,629.10, a spread of $184.30 or 4.146% of the low, closing $9.60 off the low and $174.70 below the high; silver $65.998 to $71.230, 7.928% of the low, closing 25.5 cents off the low; platinum $1,815.00 to $1,907.00; palladium $1,304.00 to $1,492.00. Palladium's bid-ask of $40.00 is 2.847% of its bid against gold's 0.0449%, sixty-three times wider. The gold-to-silver ratio moved from 65.43 to 67.23, 2.75% wider in one session. Outside-source divergence disclosed: TradingEconomics read platinum at $1,833.60 against Kitco's mid of $1,824.00, a gap of 0.526%, while CNBC at $1,820.15 agreed with Kitco. Gold & Metals This week — August 17 to 28

India's gold import duty raised ₹10,463 crore in 81 days and imports rose in every published month after the increase

The rate went from 6% to 15% on 13 May 2026, stated purpose the protection of dollar reserves. Customs duty of ₹10,463 crore was collected between 13 May and 2 August, 81 days, about ₹129 crore a day on this desk's arithmetic. April imports, ahead of the hike, rose nearly 82% year on year; May rose nearly 34% with the duty in force for part of the month; June and July together were $6.13 billion against $5.81 billion, up 5.51% on this desk's recomputation. India buys 700 to 800 tonnes a year and exports finished jewellery to the UAE, Thailand and Singapore. Reports on 26 August (Outlook Business; Free Press Journal crediting Moneycontrol) say officials are examining a cut, with industry asking for 6% and silver and platinum duties under the same review; sourced to unnamed officials, no notification issued, the Ministry of Finance did not respond to a request for comment, and several outlets trace to the same original reporting. Labelled speculative.

Source — Outlook Business and Free Press Journal citing Moneycontrol, 26 August 2026

Chow Tai Fook, Chow Tai Seng, Chow Hong Ji and Tse Sui Luen read 1,410 yuan; Chow Sang Sang and Luk Fook 1,408; Lao Miao 1,407; Lao Feng Xiang 1,406; Zhou Liu Fu 1,405; China Gold 1,403, the lowest. The spread is 7 yuan, or 0.499% of the low, this desk's arithmetic. On 24 August, Chow Tai Fook read 1,393, Chow Sang Sang 1,390 and Lao Miao 1,393, so in one session Chow Sang Sang added 18 yuan or 1.295%, Chow Tai Fook 17 yuan or 1.220%, and Lao Miao 14 yuan or 1.005% - a daily move two to three times the distance across the whole field. These are retail counter prices for finished jewellery, each carrying its own workmanship charge above the metal, not spot quotations, and the per-gram labour charge is not disclosed on these boards. A 26 August pullback reported for four of these brands reached this desk single-source with a back-calculated day-over-day change; it could not be confirmed against a second independent source and is therefore not printed, and no 25-to-26 August change is stated.

Source — cngold brand counter boards, 24 and 25 August 2026
