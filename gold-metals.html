Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
HallmarkTerm

The assay office's stamped guarantee of fineness — 750 for 18k, 916 for 22k. The oldest consumer-protection system in the world, and the inspiration for this paper's own mark.

Making chargePractice

The labor and design fee added over the metal's melt value — the jeweler's actual margin. When gold spikes, making charges get squeezed first; watch them to see who holds pricing power.

Fineness ladderConcept

24k is pure; 22k, 18k, 14k, 10k and 9k trade purity for durability and price. Bull markets in bullion push whole countries down a rung — India's 18k boom is this cycle's signature.

Melt valueMetric

What a piece is worth as raw metal, ignoring craft entirely. The gap between melt and retail is where brand, design and trust live — and it's the number every buyback desk starts from.

Gold & Metals This week — August 3

July payrolls fall 23,000 against a consensus of 83,000; gold closes at $4,341.30

US non-farm payroll employment fell by 23,000 in July 2026 against a market consensus of roughly 83,000 jobs added, a miss of 106,000, with June revised down to a loss of 20,000. The unemployment rate edged down to 4.1% while the labour force participation rate fell to 61.4%, its lowest in more than five years; average hourly earnings rose two cents on the month and annual wage growth slowed to 3.2%. The Bureau of Labor Statistics release page returned HTTP 403 to this desk, and the figures are carried as reported by Brisk Markets and CNBC on 7 August 2026 rather than from the primary release. Kitco's 5:00pm New York close on 7 August had gold bid at $4,341.30 an ounce, up $102.00 or 2.41% against Thursday's close; silver $63.46, up $2.06 or 3.36%; platinum $1,743.00, up $25.00 or 1.46%; palladium $1,357.00, up $4.00 or 0.30%. Fine gold at that level is $139.58 a gram against $138.72 at this paper's Friday morning mark; ten grams of 18-carat gold carries $1,046.85 of metal. Outside sources diverge on the day's gold mark and the divergence is recorded rather than resolved: Trading Economics carried $4,343.43 for 7 August, $2.13 above the Kitco close, and Reuters reporting during the session had spot near $4,356 at its intraday best. This paper carries the Kitco close because it is a stated closing print at a stated time. The CME FedWatch tool had September rate-hike probability at 46% immediately after the release, from 55% before it and against the 56.9% this paper carried on Friday morning. Consumer price data is released the following week. Saturday 8 August is a weekend and spot markets did not trade; the Editor re-read Kitco at press time and the reading matched the 05:00 tape exactly, so the tape stands unamended.

Source — Kitco / Brisk Markets / CNBC / Trading Economics

Gold & Metals This week — August 3

China Gold Association puts first-half jewellery down 33.88% and bars up 28.42%

China Gold Association half-year figures, reported 6-7 August 2026, put national gold consumption at 511.41 tonnes for the first half of 2026, up 1.23% year on year. Gold jewellery consumption fell 33.88% to 132.13 tonnes while gold bar and coin consumption rose 28.42% to 339.34 tonnes, making investment metal roughly two and a half times the jewellery figure. The association attributes the divergence to elevated and volatile gold prices and to the rollout of new domestic gold tax policies, with temporary price corrections boosting bar sales through banks. These figures sit alongside, and do not match, the World Gold Council data this paper filed on 3 August, which put Chinese jewellery demand at roughly 136 tonnes across the half, a fall of about 30%, and the second quarter at 50.0 tonnes, the weakest since 2004. The gap between the two authorities is about four tonnes and nearly four percentage points, arising from differing definitions between a consumption series and a demand series; both are printed here and neither is preferred. Related context previously filed by this paper: Indian jewellery demand of 75.1 tonnes in the second quarter, down 15% by weight and up about 50% by value (31 July), and Luk Fook's record profit driven by fixed-price design rather than gram weight (5 August).

Source — China Gold Association via China.org.cn / China Daily / World Gold Council
Gold & Metals This week — August 3

WPIC's 14% Chinese platinum jewellery decline is a base effect, not a demand fall

The World Platinum Investment Council projects Chinese platinum jewellery demand down 14% year on year in 2026 and global platinum jewellery demand down about 6%. The council attributes the Chinese figure to an unrepeated and exceptionally high stock build in the second quarter of 2025, when Chinese fabricators bought platinum ahead of a gold price that had made their usual metal unaffordable; excluding that atypical inventory build, Chinese platinum jewellery fabrication would show a small year-on-year increase. The global 6% contraction reflects North American and European growth consolidating after strong years and Indian demand facing tariff-related headwinds, with the largest single contributor being the Chinese comparison. WPIC separately forecasts a fourth consecutive annual platinum market deficit for 2026, with supply falling short of demand by 297,000 ounces. Against this, platinum spot was bid at $1,772.00 an ounce at 5:58am New York on 7 August, up 3.14% on the session and standing at 41.1% of the gold price. The general caution applies beyond platinum: any 2026 comparison against 2025 in this trade is a comparison against a year in which buyers front-ran the gold price.

Source — World Platinum Investment Council via CME Group / WPIC press release / Kitco
