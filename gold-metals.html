Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
HallmarkTerm

The assay office's stamped guarantee of fineness — 750 for 18k, 916 for 22k. The oldest consumer-protection system in the world, and the inspiration for this paper's own mark.

Making chargePractice

The labor and design fee added over the metal's melt value — the jeweler's actual margin. When gold spikes, making charges get squeezed first; watch them to see who holds pricing power.

Fineness ladderConcept

24k is pure; 22k, 18k, 14k, 10k and 9k trade purity for durability and price. Bull markets in bullion push whole countries down a rung — India's 18k boom is this cycle's signature.

Melt valueMetric

What a piece is worth as raw metal, ignoring craft entirely. The gap between melt and retail is where brand, design and trust live — and it's the number every buyback desk starts from.

Gold & Metals This week — August 17

All four metals written back at 05:59, ending a three-session palladium hold at $1,311.00

On 17 August 2026 Kitco's live board at 05:59 New York time read gold at $4,394.60 an ounce, silver at $65.38, platinum at $1,749.00 and palladium at $1,311.00. Under the one-price rule the Editor wrote all four readings back over the 04:59 marks, with the new timestamp and the source named, before the edition built. Because no edition published on 15 or 16 August, two comparison bases are printed. Against Kitco's own previous close the session moves are gold up $19.00, silver up $0.80, platinum up $4.00 and palladium up $13.00. Against this paper's last printed marks of record, set on 14 August 2026 at $4,356.20, $64.77, $1,725.00 and $1,297.00, the three-day moves are gold up $38.40 or 0.88%, silver up $0.61 or 0.94%, platinum up $24.00 or 1.39% and palladium up $14.00 or 1.08%. Gold and silver were cross-checked the same morning against TradingEconomics at $4,394.79 and $65.484, differences of 0.004% and 0.16%. The palladium mark retired, $1,297.00, was set on 14 August and carried unchanged since; the tape desk declined to replace it at 04:59 today, having found Kitco at $1,311.00 against FXEmpire at $1,320.55, a gap of $9.55 or 0.73%, and held under the two-page rule. At 05:59 the Editor read two further pages, TradingEconomics at $1,331.00 and JM Bullion at $1,351.00. The four live readings span $40.00, or 3.05%, from low to high and every one of them sits above the held mark, which is not among them; the divergence between those outside pages is disclosed rather than reconciled. Platinum showed the same basis for a fourth consecutive session, with Kitco $11.40 or 0.65% below TradingEconomics at $1,760.40, the same sign and approximate size as the gaps recorded on 12, 13 and 14 August; Kitco is printed because it is the board carrying the other three marks on this tape. At 31.1035 grams to the troy ounce the four marks work out at $141.29 a gram of fine gold, $2.10 of fine silver, $56.23 of fine platinum and $42.15 of fine palladium, and the gold-to-silver ratio reads 67.22 against 67.26 on 14 August, all this desk's arithmetic.

Gold & Metals This week — August 17

Shanghai gold rose 1.41% and China's jewellery counter held its markup at 39.62%

On 17 August 2026 the Shanghai Gold Exchange's Au99.99 contract opened at 946.00 yuan a gram and last traded at 954.01, a rise of 1.41% on the session, per the market report carried by the Chinese finance aggregator cngold. Chow Tai Fook's 24-carat counter read 1,332 yuan a gram the same day, a figure carried identically by two independent Chinese price aggregators, jinjia.com.cn and cngold. Dividing the counter by the exchange, this desk's arithmetic, puts the counter 39.62% above the exchange, a gap of 377.99 yuan on every gram. This paper published the same pair on 14 August 2026 at 940.72 and 1,312, a markup of 39.47% and a gap of 371.28 yuan. Over the three days the exchange rose 13.29 yuan, or 1.41%, and the counter rose 20 yuan, or 1.52%, moving the markup by fifteen hundredths of a percentage point. This is the test this desk set for its own 14 August argument, that the Chinese counter price is a merchant setting a margin rather than a market clearing, and the result is consistent with it: the counter tracked the metal in percentage terms rather than in absolute yuan. Two divergences between outside sources are disclosed. On the exchange leg, the Bureau's gold desk read today's Shanghai sessions at 951.80 and 952.98 yuan against the 954.01 used here; on 952.98 the markup would read 39.77% and the gap 379.02 yuan, and this desk cannot establish which minute of the board each page reads. On the counter leg, the aggregator series runs 1,312 on 14 August, 1,325 on 15 August, no published reading for 16 August and 1,332 on 17 August, so the 20-yuan rise spans two counter revisions rather than one. As a sanity check on the Shanghai figure, gold at this paper's own mark of $4,394.60 an ounce is $141.29 a gram, and 954.01 yuan against $141.29 implies 6.752 yuan to the dollar; that implied rate is this desk's arithmetic and is not an official currency quotation. A listed counter price is an asking price rather than a transacted one, and workmanship charges, store discounts and buy-back terms sit outside both numbers.

Source — Shanghai Gold Exchange market report via cngold (17 August 2026) / jinjia.com.cn / caratgold bureau file / Carat Capital arithmetic and archive
Gold & Metals This week — August 17

Perth Mint July: silver sales up 65% on the month, gold up 4%, platinum up 59%

The Perth Mint reported on 10 August 2026 that it sold 30,871 troy ounces of gold, 486,043 ounces of silver and 1,915 ounces of platinum in minted product form during July 2026. Month on month, gold sales rose 4%, silver 65% and platinum 59%. Year on year, gold rose 41%, silver 8% and platinum 46%. Holdings in the mint's exchange-traded product, PMGOLD, rose 1,011 ounces, or 0.26%, to 382,735 ounces, which is 11.90 tonnes at 31.1035 grams to the troy ounce. The mint's own commentary records gold trading near US$4,050 an ounce at the end of July, a monthly gain of about 1%, after touching its lowest level of the year during the month. Against this paper's mark of record of US$4,394.60 an ounce on 17 August 2026, that end-July level is 8.51% lower, this desk's arithmetic, so the July demand recorded here was transacted at a materially lower price than the metal now carries. Derived figures, all this desk's: 15.7 ounces of silver left the mint for every ounce of gold; valued at this paper's 17 August marks of US$4,394.60 and US$65.38 an ounce, the month's gold sales are approximately US$135.7 million against approximately US$31.8 million of silver, so gold outsold silver by 4.27 to one by value while silver outsold gold by 15.7 to one by weight. The figures are seven days old as this paper prints them and describe a month that closed more than a fortnight ago. They cover one refiner's minted-product sales through a retail channel weighted to Australian and Asian buyers, and are neither Australian nor global demand. This paper reported on 14 August 2026 that global gold exchange-traded fund flows in July were principally European, with North America contributing US$71 million of a US$3 billion month.

Source — The Perth Mint, July 2026 sales update (10 August 2026) / Kitco / Carat Capital tape, arithmetic and archive
Gold & Metals Week of August 10

China's jewellery counter is 39.47% above the Shanghai exchange price, and the gap has widened since 8 August

The Shanghai Gold Exchange closed its Au99.99 contract at 940.72 yuan a gram on 14 August 2026, from an opening of 955.00 and a session low of 933.01, on volume of 3,886.14 kilogrammes; the deferred Au(T+D) contract closed at 940.44 on 41,646 kilogrammes. Those figures are taken from the exchange's own published daily quotations rather than from an aggregator. On the same day Chow Tai Fook listed 24-carat jewellery at 1,312 yuan a gram, a figure carried identically by two independent Chinese price aggregators, jinjia.com.cn and cngold. Dividing the counter price by the exchange price, this desk's arithmetic, puts the counter 39.47% above the exchange, a gap of 371.28 yuan on every gram. This paper published the same pair on 9 August 2026 at 938 yuan on the exchange and 1,286 at the counter, a gap of 348 yuan and a markup of 37.10%. Six days later the exchange has moved 2.72 yuan, or 0.29%, and the counter has moved 26 yuan, or 2.02%, so the gap has widened by 23.28 yuan a gram rather than closing. Today both legs fell: the exchange closed 8.52 yuan, or 0.90%, below its 13 August close of 949.24, while the counter came off 28 yuan, or 2.09%, from 1,340, roughly two and a third times as fast, narrowing the premium from 41.17% on one aggregator's 13 August counter price, or 40.01% on the other's, to 39.47%. Across the sessions the aggregator series records this month the Chow Tai Fook price has gone 1,231, 1,240, 1,297, 1,286, 1,308, 1,308, 1,336, 1,329, 1,340 and 1,312. On the readings this desk can verify, the Chinese jewellery markup has run between 37.10% and 41.17% in August. Two divergences between outside sources are disclosed: one aggregator's series carries 1,286 against 7 August rather than the 8 August this paper published it under, and the two aggregators disagree about 13 August by 11 yuan, at 1,340 and 1,329. Separately, the Bank of China's market note of 13 August put Shanghai spot gold up 0.96% at 955.75 yuan a gram against the exchange's own close of 949.24 for that date; the exchange's own figure is used here. A listed counter price is an asking price rather than a transacted one, and workmanship charges, store discounts and buy-back terms sit outside both numbers.

Source — Shanghai Gold Exchange daily quotations (14 August 2026) / jinjia.com.cn / cngold / Bank of China market note (13 August 2026) / Carat Capital arithmetic and archive
Gold & Metals Week of August 10

All four metals written back on one board, ending a two-session platinum hold at $1,725.00

On 14 August 2026 Kitco's live board at 06:00 New York time read gold at $4,356.20 an ounce, silver at $64.77, platinum at $1,725.00 and palladium at $1,297.00. Under the one-price rule the Editor wrote all four readings back over caratwire's 04:59 marks, with the new timestamp and the source named on the tape before the edition built. Measured against this paper's own marks of record 24 hours earlier, gold fell $27.90 or 0.64%, silver rose $0.25 or 0.39%, platinum rose $15.00 or 0.88% and palladium fell $22.00 or 1.67%. Gold and silver were cross-checked the same morning against TradingEconomics at $4,356.79 and $64.89, differences of 0.01% and 0.19%. The gold-to-silver ratio, this desk's division of its own published gold mark by its own published silver mark on the same board at the same minute, narrowed to 67.26 from 67.95. Platinum had been held twice: on 12 August 2026 because Kitco's cell was stale, and on 13 August 2026 because Kitco's live cell sat $14.60 below TradingEconomics and JM Bullion, which agreed with each other to within 50 cents. At 04:59 on 14 August the tape desk found the same shape a third time, with Kitco $13.30 below TradingEconomics, and held again. At 06:00 the gap was $12.70 against TradingEconomics at $1,737.70 and $14.90 against JM Bullion at $1,739.90, with those two pages agreeing to $2.20, or 0.13%. This desk's judgement is that a gap recurring at the same size and sign across three consecutive sessions is a basis difference between outside pages rather than an unreadable market, and that continuing to hold had produced a mark two sessions old and 1.62% below where two other pages put the metal. Platinum is therefore written back on Kitco, the board that carries every other mark on this tape, and palladium with it at Kitco's $1,297.00 bid; JM Bullion read palladium at $1,336.71 at 06:01, $39.71 or 3.06% higher, disclosed as the other side of a thin spread. At 31.1035 grams to the troy ounce the four marks work out at $140.05 a gram of fine gold, $2.08 of fine silver, $55.46 of fine platinum and $41.70 of fine palladium, all this desk's arithmetic.

Source — Kitco / TradingEconomics / JM Bullion / Carat Capital tape and arithmetic
