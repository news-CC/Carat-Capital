Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
HallmarkTerm

The assay office's stamped guarantee of fineness — 750 for 18k, 916 for 22k. The oldest consumer-protection system in the world, and the inspiration for this paper's own mark.

Making chargePractice

The labor and design fee added over the metal's melt value — the jeweler's actual margin. When gold spikes, making charges get squeezed first; watch them to see who holds pricing power.

Fineness ladderConcept

24k is pure; 22k, 18k, 14k, 10k and 9k trade purity for durability and price. Bull markets in bullion push whole countries down a rung — India's 18k boom is this cycle's signature.

Melt valueMetric

What a piece is worth as raw metal, ignoring craft entirely. The gap between melt and retail is where brand, design and trust live — and it's the number every buyback desk starts from.

Gold & Metals This week — August 3

All four metals rise on a fifth session, silver up 4.71% into the payrolls print

At 5:58am New York time on 7 August, Kitco's spot page had gold bid at $4,314.50 an ounce, up $75.20 or 1.77% against Thursday's close, a fifth consecutive advance and above the seven-week high carried the previous day. Silver was bid at $64.29, up $2.89 or 4.71%, the largest single-session move of the four; platinum at $1,772.00, up $54.00 or 3.14%; palladium at $1,382.00, up $29.00 or 2.14%. The gold to silver ratio tightened to 67.1 from 69.3, more than reversing Thursday's widening. Against the 6:03am mark this paper carried on 6 August, gold added $43.60 an ounce or 1.02% and silver $2.62 or 4.25% in twenty-four hours. Fine gold at $4,314.50 an ounce is $138.72 a gram against $137.31 the previous session; platinum stood at 41.1% of the gold price against 41.0%. This desk wrote on 6 August that silver turning red beneath a gold high was the clearest internal warning the tape had printed all week; silver reversed that decline in full the following session, and the call is recorded here against the print that followed it. Context: Iran said it had reached an understanding with Oman on a proposed shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz, describing it as a temporary route and not a full reopening; ADP put July private hiring at 44,000 against about 50,000 expected, JOLTS was softer and initial claims rose to 199,000; the CME FedWatch tool showed September at 56.9% against 63.4% a week earlier. July non-farm payrolls were scheduled for 8:30am on 7 August, after this edition closed. Two divergences are recorded rather than resolved: this paper's own overnight tape read gold at $4,304.40 at 4:58am, $10.10 below the 5:58am bid, with silver at $64.16 and platinum at $1,763.00 and palladium identical at $1,382.00 on matching previous closes; and Dubai 24-carat retail was quoted at AED 517.50 a gram by Khaleej Times on 7 August, AED 510.25 by this paper's metals desk the same day and AED 514.00 by the overnight desk on 6 August. NAT1 and LGD1 were carried unchanged, the lab-grown reference holding at $709 on CaratRadar for a third straight day against $536 on StoneAlgo, a spread of roughly 24%.

Source — Kitco / FXStreet / Khaleej Times / BLS
Gold & Metals This week — August 3

China Gold Association puts first-half jewellery down 33.88% and bars up 28.42%

China Gold Association half-year figures, reported 6-7 August 2026, put national gold consumption at 511.41 tonnes for the first half of 2026, up 1.23% year on year. Gold jewellery consumption fell 33.88% to 132.13 tonnes while gold bar and coin consumption rose 28.42% to 339.34 tonnes, making investment metal roughly two and a half times the jewellery figure. The association attributes the divergence to elevated and volatile gold prices and to the rollout of new domestic gold tax policies, with temporary price corrections boosting bar sales through banks. These figures sit alongside, and do not match, the World Gold Council data this paper filed on 3 August, which put Chinese jewellery demand at roughly 136 tonnes across the half, a fall of about 30%, and the second quarter at 50.0 tonnes, the weakest since 2004. The gap between the two authorities is about four tonnes and nearly four percentage points, arising from differing definitions between a consumption series and a demand series; both are printed here and neither is preferred. Related context previously filed by this paper: Indian jewellery demand of 75.1 tonnes in the second quarter, down 15% by weight and up about 50% by value (31 July), and Luk Fook's record profit driven by fixed-price design rather than gram weight (5 August).

Source — China Gold Association via China.org.cn / China Daily / World Gold Council
Gold & Metals This week — August 3

WPIC's 14% Chinese platinum jewellery decline is a base effect, not a demand fall

The World Platinum Investment Council projects Chinese platinum jewellery demand down 14% year on year in 2026 and global platinum jewellery demand down about 6%. The council attributes the Chinese figure to an unrepeated and exceptionally high stock build in the second quarter of 2025, when Chinese fabricators bought platinum ahead of a gold price that had made their usual metal unaffordable; excluding that atypical inventory build, Chinese platinum jewellery fabrication would show a small year-on-year increase. The global 6% contraction reflects North American and European growth consolidating after strong years and Indian demand facing tariff-related headwinds, with the largest single contributor being the Chinese comparison. WPIC separately forecasts a fourth consecutive annual platinum market deficit for 2026, with supply falling short of demand by 297,000 ounces. Against this, platinum spot was bid at $1,772.00 an ounce at 5:58am New York on 7 August, up 3.14% on the session and standing at 41.1% of the gold price. The general caution applies beyond platinum: any 2026 comparison against 2025 in this trade is a comparison against a year in which buyers front-ran the gold price.

Source — World Platinum Investment Council via CME Group / WPIC press release / Kitco

Gold makes a seven-week high on a fourth session up while silver turns lower

Gold & Metals This week — August 3

Every precious metal rises for a third session as gold adds $109 an ounce in a day

At 5:58am New York time on 5 August, Kitco's spot page had gold bid at $4,157.80 an ounce, up $81.20 or 1.99% against Tuesday's close; silver $61.35, up $1.94 or 3.27%; platinum $1,747.00, up $19.00 or 1.10%; and palladium $1,355.00, up $26.00 or 1.96%. It was the third consecutive session in which all four rose. Against the $4,048.80 print this paper carried on 4 August, gold added about $109 an ounce in twenty-four hours. The gold to silver ratio tightened to 67.8 from about 69.0 and platinum stood at 42.0% of the gold price; fine gold at $4,157.80 an ounce is $133.68 a gram against $130.17 the previous session. The move followed reports that the United States, Iran and Oman were closing on an interim agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, with an announcement targeted for Wednesday; lower oil cut the inflation path and traders trimmed the odds of a September Federal Reserve increase to about 57% from about 67% a day earlier. Intraday prints were materially higher than the mark carried here, at $4,170.90 at 3:08am and $4,174.70 at 2:44am. Trading Economics printed platinum at $1,760.10 and palladium at $1,372.00, spreads of $13.10 and $17.00 against Kitco, which remains this paper's designated source. NAT1 and LGD1 both carried unchanged; the lab-grown reference was not printed because two trackers stood 28% apart, at $709 and $552, on the same week.
