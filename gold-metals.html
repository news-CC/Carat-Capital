Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
HallmarkTerm

The assay office's stamped guarantee of fineness — 750 for 18k, 916 for 22k. The oldest consumer-protection system in the world, and the inspiration for this paper's own mark.

Making chargePractice

The labor and design fee added over the metal's melt value — the jeweler's actual margin. When gold spikes, making charges get squeezed first; watch them to see who holds pricing power.

Fineness ladderConcept

24k is pure; 22k, 18k, 14k, 10k and 9k trade purity for durability and price. Bull markets in bullion push whole countries down a rung — India's 18k boom is this cycle's signature.

Melt valueMetric

What a piece is worth as raw metal, ignoring craft entirely. The gap between melt and retail is where brand, design and trust live — and it's the number every buyback desk starts from.

Gold & Metals This week — August 10

The Dubai gap holds at AED 10.44 as all three readings move together

On 11 August 2026 Kitco's live board at 06:00 New York time put gold at $4,372.80 an ounce, which is $140.59 a gram at 31.1035 grams to the troy ounce and AED 516.31 a gram at the dirham's fixed peg of 3.6725 to the dollar; both conversions are this desk's arithmetic and are published by no source. LivePriceOfGold's Dubai board, timestamped 11 August at 10:04 UTC, read AED 516.26 a gram for 24-carat gold, five fils from the peg figure and about one hundredth of one per cent away. Goodreturns' Dubai page, dated the same day, read AED 526.75 for 24-carat and AED 487.75 for 22-carat. The difference between the counter page and the peg arithmetic is AED 10.44 a gram, or 2.02% of the metal, against AED 10.66 on 10 August and AED 1.08 on 7 August, all three derived by this desk. Overnight the three readings moved almost in lockstep: the peg figure added AED 3.72, LivePriceOfGold AED 3.81 and Goodreturns AED 3.50, a spread of AED 0.31 across the three. That resolves the open question left by this paper's 10 August lead. The AED 13.25 step Goodreturns took between 7 and 8 August was a single repricing rather than a page that had stopped updating, and the margin it opened has since held through a rising metal. Under the one-price rule the tape carries the Editor's 06:00 press-time reading, written back over the 04:58 mark of $4,369.70, $64.60, $1,752.00 and $1,357.00: gold $4,372.80, silver $64.79, platinum $1,764.00 and palladium $1,362.00. Measured against this paper's own mark of record 24 hours earlier those are gains of 0.72%, 1.25%, 1.55% and 1.64%; Kitco's own session line at the same moment read gold and silver lower against its previous close, which is two conventions rather than two prices. The gold-to-silver ratio narrows to 67.49 from 67.84.

Source — Kitco / LivePriceOfGold / Goodreturns / Carat Capital arithmetic at the AED peg
Gold & Metals This week — August 10

Two Dubai gold pages read 2.11% apart on the same morning, and neither is wrong

On 10 August 2026 two of the most widely quoted Dubai gold pages published materially different rates for the same metal on the same day. LivePriceOfGold's Dubai board, timestamped 10 August at 10:07 UTC, read AED 512.45 a gram for 24-carat gold, AED 470.43 for 22-carat, AED 448.40 for 21-carat and AED 384.34 for 18-carat, and states on the page that it prints a raw gold rate to which commission and labour charges are added. Goodreturns' Dubai page, dated the same day, read AED 523.25 for 24-carat and AED 484.50 for 22-carat, reporting no change against 9 August. The gaps are 2.11% on the 24-carat line and 2.99% on the 22-carat line, both derived by this desk from the two published figures. Kitco's live board at 5:57am New York time on 10 August put gold at $4,341.40 an ounce, which is $139.58 a gram at 31.1035 grams to the troy ounce and AED 512.59 a gram at the dirham's fixed peg of 3.6725 to the dollar; both conversions are this desk's arithmetic and are published by no source. That places LivePriceOfGold AED 0.14 from the raw-metal figure and Goodreturns AED 10.66 above it. Goodreturns' own ten-day table runs AED 485.25 on 1 August, 487.25 on the 2nd and 3rd, 485.50 on the 4th, 492.25 on the 5th, 510.25 on the 6th, 510.50 on the 7th, 523.75 on the 8th and 523.25 on the 9th and 10th: a step of AED 13.25 between 7 and 8 August against a metal move of AED 3.13 over the same span, then three flat days while spot continued to move. Read against this, the three UAE readings this paper printed for the 7 August session without choosing between them, Khaleej Times at AED 517.50, one bureau desk at AED 510.25 and another at AED 514.00, sit on two different lanes rather than representing three attempts at one figure; the peg arithmetic for 7 August gives AED 509.42.

Gold & Metals This week — August 10

India hallmarked 5.9 million silver articles in FY2025-26, nearly double the year before

The Bureau of Indian Standards disclosed on 9 August 2026 that the volume of hallmarked silver articles in India rose to 5.9 million pieces in the 2025-26 financial year from 3.2 million in 2024-25, an increase of about 84% derived by this desk from the two published counts, and set out a plan to scale up testing and certification capacity to match. The bureau attributes the rise to consumers moving into silver as gold prices have risen beyond reach. About 230 bureau-recognised assaying and hallmarking centres currently handle silver testing across the country. The bureau's recognised laboratory network has grown from 147 in 2014 to 440, and government-empanelled laboratories from 24 to 350, against ten standalone bureau laboratories and roughly 270,000 product samples tested; the bureau holds about 700 mandatory standards out of 23,000 in total. Nishat S Haque, deputy director general for laboratories, said the referral and assay laboratory expansion would run across the next two years. Gold surveillance remains in-house while silver testing is outsourced to the recognised centres as capacity is built. Silver hallmarking has been voluntary in India since 2005 and no timeline has been announced for making it mandatory, so the 5.9 million figure counts articles voluntarily submitted rather than total production. A hallmark unique identification number for silver articles was introduced in September 2025. The demand backdrop previously filed by this paper is Indian gold jewellery demand down 15% by weight in the second quarter to 75.1 tonnes, its lowest second quarter since the pandemic, and Kalyan Jewellers taking more than 46% of June-quarter revenue in recycled metal.

Source — Bureau of Indian Standards via Business Today, Business Standard and The Daily Pioneer
Gold & Metals Week of August 3

The PBoC adds 640,000 ounces in July, its largest month since October 2023

The People's Bank of China increased its official gold reserves by 640,000 troy ounces, about 19.9 metric tonnes, during July 2026, published with the monthly reserve data on 7 August. Holdings rose to 76.08 million troy ounces at the end of July from 75.44 million at the end of June, roughly 2,366 tonnes against 2,346. It was the twenty-first consecutive month in which the bank reported a purchase and the largest single-month addition since the 740,000 ounces recorded in October 2023. The gold reserve was valued at $306.35 billion against $303.72 billion a month earlier, within total official foreign-exchange assets of $3.419 trillion, up from $3.416 trillion, putting gold at about 8% of the reserve. Set against this paper's reporting of 2 August, which carried World Gold Council figures showing central banks buying 289 tonnes in the second quarter, up 62%, with China taking 33 tonnes across those three months, July alone runs at close to double China's second-quarter monthly pace. The same World Gold Council data put first-half central-bank demand at 345 tonnes, the smallest first half since 2022's 241 tonnes, after a first quarter revised down to 57 tonnes. July was also the month gold closed at $4,041.70, up about half a per cent and its first monthly gain since February, so the increase in pace came into a market that had barely moved. At the $4,341.30 an ounce carried on this paper's tape, 640,000 ounces is worth about $2.78 billion, a figure derived by this desk from the published ounce count rather than reported.

Source — PBoC monthly reserve data via The Private Banker and Bloomberg / World Gold Council / Kitco
Gold & Metals Week of August 3

Shanghai brand counters run 348 yuan a gram above the exchange benchmark

The Shanghai Gold Exchange's Au99.99 contract traded at 938 yuan a gram on the morning of 8 August 2026, up 7.53 yuan from the previous close. On the same day, Chinese brand counter prices for 24-carat jewellery stood at 1,286 yuan a gram at Chow Tai Fook, 1,285 at Chow Sang Sang, 1,284 at Lukfook, 1,283 at Lao Feng Xiang and Lao Miao, and 1,281 at Zhou Liu Fu — every major brand inside five yuan of the others and roughly 37% above the exchange benchmark, a spread of 348 yuan a gram. The same Mandarin financial-press tally puts the distance from the Shuibei wholesale price in Shenzhen, where most Chinese gold jewellery is made and traded, to the highest brand counter price at 170 yuan a gram. On 6 August the major brands raised counter prices by close to 60 yuan a gram in a single session, as reported by Sina Finance, against a far smaller move in the benchmark, reversing the pattern in which brand list prices lagged the metal. This paper declined to print a Chinese brand-counter figure on 7 August because two independent reads of that day's price stood at 1,240 and 1,297 yuan a gram, the main aggregator returned empty price cells and a second site failed on its certificate; the 8 August table, cross-checked against two independent brand-price trackers, resolves it. The demand backdrop, previously filed by this paper on 7 August, is China Gold Association half-year figures putting gold jewellery consumption at 132.13 tonnes, down 33.88%, against bar and coin demand of 339.34 tonnes, up 28.42%.

Source — Tencent News (Mandarin) / Sina Finance / China Gold Association via Carat Capital
