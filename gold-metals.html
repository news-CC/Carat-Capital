Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
HallmarkTerm

The assay office's stamped guarantee of fineness — 750 for 18k, 916 for 22k. The oldest consumer-protection system in the world, and the inspiration for this paper's own mark.

Making chargePractice

The labor and design fee added over the metal's melt value — the jeweler's actual margin. When gold spikes, making charges get squeezed first; watch them to see who holds pricing power.

Fineness ladderConcept

24k is pure; 22k, 18k, 14k, 10k and 9k trade purity for durability and price. Bull markets in bullion push whole countries down a rung — India's 18k boom is this cycle's signature.

Melt valueMetric

What a piece is worth as raw metal, ignoring craft entirely. The gap between melt and retail is where brand, design and trust live — and it's the number every buyback desk starts from.

Gold & Metals This week — August 10

China's jewellery counter is 39.47% above the Shanghai exchange price, and the gap has widened since 8 August

The Shanghai Gold Exchange closed its Au99.99 contract at 940.72 yuan a gram on 14 August 2026, from an opening of 955.00 and a session low of 933.01, on volume of 3,886.14 kilogrammes; the deferred Au(T+D) contract closed at 940.44 on 41,646 kilogrammes. Those figures are taken from the exchange's own published daily quotations rather than from an aggregator. On the same day Chow Tai Fook listed 24-carat jewellery at 1,312 yuan a gram, a figure carried identically by two independent Chinese price aggregators, jinjia.com.cn and cngold. Dividing the counter price by the exchange price, this desk's arithmetic, puts the counter 39.47% above the exchange, a gap of 371.28 yuan on every gram. This paper published the same pair on 9 August 2026 at 938 yuan on the exchange and 1,286 at the counter, a gap of 348 yuan and a markup of 37.10%. Six days later the exchange has moved 2.72 yuan, or 0.29%, and the counter has moved 26 yuan, or 2.02%, so the gap has widened by 23.28 yuan a gram rather than closing. Today both legs fell: the exchange closed 8.52 yuan, or 0.90%, below its 13 August close of 949.24, while the counter came off 28 yuan, or 2.09%, from 1,340, roughly two and a third times as fast, narrowing the premium from 41.17% on one aggregator's 13 August counter price, or 40.01% on the other's, to 39.47%. Across the sessions the aggregator series records this month the Chow Tai Fook price has gone 1,231, 1,240, 1,297, 1,286, 1,308, 1,308, 1,336, 1,329, 1,340 and 1,312. On the readings this desk can verify, the Chinese jewellery markup has run between 37.10% and 41.17% in August. Two divergences between outside sources are disclosed: one aggregator's series carries 1,286 against 7 August rather than the 8 August this paper published it under, and the two aggregators disagree about 13 August by 11 yuan, at 1,340 and 1,329. Separately, the Bank of China's market note of 13 August put Shanghai spot gold up 0.96% at 955.75 yuan a gram against the exchange's own close of 949.24 for that date; the exchange's own figure is used here. A listed counter price is an asking price rather than a transacted one, and workmanship charges, store discounts and buy-back terms sit outside both numbers.

Source — Shanghai Gold Exchange daily quotations (14 August 2026) / jinjia.com.cn / cngold / Bank of China market note (13 August 2026) / Carat Capital arithmetic and archive
Gold & Metals This week — August 10

All four metals written back on one board, ending a two-session platinum hold at $1,725.00

On 14 August 2026 Kitco's live board at 06:00 New York time read gold at $4,356.20 an ounce, silver at $64.77, platinum at $1,725.00 and palladium at $1,297.00. Under the one-price rule the Editor wrote all four readings back over caratwire's 04:59 marks, with the new timestamp and the source named on the tape before the edition built. Measured against this paper's own marks of record 24 hours earlier, gold fell $27.90 or 0.64%, silver rose $0.25 or 0.39%, platinum rose $15.00 or 0.88% and palladium fell $22.00 or 1.67%. Gold and silver were cross-checked the same morning against TradingEconomics at $4,356.79 and $64.89, differences of 0.01% and 0.19%. The gold-to-silver ratio, this desk's division of its own published gold mark by its own published silver mark on the same board at the same minute, narrowed to 67.26 from 67.95. Platinum had been held twice: on 12 August 2026 because Kitco's cell was stale, and on 13 August 2026 because Kitco's live cell sat $14.60 below TradingEconomics and JM Bullion, which agreed with each other to within 50 cents. At 04:59 on 14 August the tape desk found the same shape a third time, with Kitco $13.30 below TradingEconomics, and held again. At 06:00 the gap was $12.70 against TradingEconomics at $1,737.70 and $14.90 against JM Bullion at $1,739.90, with those two pages agreeing to $2.20, or 0.13%. This desk's judgement is that a gap recurring at the same size and sign across three consecutive sessions is a basis difference between outside pages rather than an unreadable market, and that continuing to hold had produced a mark two sessions old and 1.62% below where two other pages put the metal. Platinum is therefore written back on Kitco, the board that carries every other mark on this tape, and palladium with it at Kitco's $1,297.00 bid; JM Bullion read palladium at $1,336.71 at 06:01, $39.71 or 3.06% higher, disclosed as the other side of a thin spread. At 31.1035 grams to the troy ounce the four marks work out at $140.05 a gram of fine gold, $2.08 of fine silver, $55.46 of fine platinum and $41.70 of fine palladium, all this desk's arithmetic.

Gold & Metals This week — August 10

Gold ETFs took in $3bn in July; Europe supplied $2bn of it and North America $71mn

The World Gold Council reported on 6 August 2026, under the title Europe's golden heatwave, that global physically backed gold exchange-traded funds took in US$3 billion in July 2026, reversing two consecutive months of outflows. Collective holdings rose 23 tonnes to 4,068 tonnes and assets under management rose 1% to US$530 billion. Holdings remain 108 tonnes, or 2.59%, below the record of 4,176 tonnes set on 27 February 2026, that subtraction being this desk's. By region, Europe took US$2 billion, of which the United Kingdom contributed US$875 million and Switzerland US$657 million; Asia took US$616 million, with China the strongest contributor and Japan in outflow; North America took US$71 million; and other regions, chiefly Australia and South Africa, took US$140 million. Britain and Switzerland together raised US$1,532 million against US$827 million from Asia, North America and all other regions combined, a ratio of 1.85 to one, and North America supplied 2.5% of the named regional total; both figures are this desk's arithmetic. The named regional components sum to US$2,827 million against a rounded headline of US$3 billion because every component is rounded, a difference this desk states rather than reconciles. Year to date, global gold ETF inflows amounted to US$11 billion, equivalent to a 39-tonne increase in holdings. Average daily trading volumes across the gold market fell 3.5% to US$356 billion in the month and over-the-counter volumes fell 3.4% to US$205 billion. This paper has previously reported global jewellery demand of 278 tonnes in the second quarter of 2026, the smallest since the pandemic, on 31 July 2026; Indian jewellery demand of 75.1 tonnes, down 15% by weight, on the same day; China's gold jewellery consumption at its lowest since 2004, on 3 August 2026; and China's heaviest month of bullion buying since 2023, on 9 August 2026. This is the first coverage of the World Gold Council's monthly ETF flows series in this paper's archive, and the report is eight days old as it prints.

Source — World Gold Council, Gold ETF Flows: July 2026 (6 August 2026) / Carat Capital arithmetic and archive
