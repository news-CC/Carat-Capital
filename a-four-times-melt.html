Five times its metal: a Garrard egg makes £123,500

A 22-carat gold Cadbury 'Conundrum' egg by Garrard sold for £123,500, about $166,600, at Batemans on 1 August. That is close to six times its estimate and roughly five times the melt value of its nine ounces of gold.

By The Auctions Desk9 August 2026Brief 60 sec · Full read 3 minCorrected · see The depth
What changed

A chocolate company's promotional egg has set an auction record most jewellery houses would take. A 22-carat gold 'Conundrum' egg made by Garrard & Co. of London in 1983 sold for £123,500, about $166,600, at Batemans Auctioneers & Valuers in Stamford, Lincolnshire on 1 August, a result reported by Forbes on 8 August.

What it means · The Desk’s View

Gold at record levels has quietly changed what an auction result means, and this one shows the arithmetic in the clear. When bullion was cheap, five times melt on a signed object read as a strong price.

With gold at $4,341.30 an ounce it means the maker's name and the documentation are carrying more than $130,000 of the total on their own, and the metal is the smaller part of a record. For anyone consigning this autumn the practical instruction is that the premium here is documentation, not carat weight, and the paperwork wants assembling before the estimate is agreed.

For anyone buying, the melt figure is the floor, and that floor rose 4.40% in three sessions last week, which makes an under-documented lot expensive in two directions at once.

IA promotional egg sets a jewellery record

The house calls it a world auction record for a bejewelled egg of its type. It made close to six times its pre-sale estimate and about eighteen times the £6,500 it fetched the last time it came up.

Forbes dates that earlier sale to 2018 and the Batemans catalogue entry for the same lot is dated July 2017; this paper carries both readings rather than picking one.

The number worth holding against it is the metal, and this paper printed it wrong the first time. Forbes gives the egg at nine ounces of 22-carat gold, which is 91.7% fine.

Nine troy ounces is about 8.25 ounces of pure gold and nine avoirdupois ounces about 7.52, and Forbes does not say which it means.

At the $4,341.30 an ounce this paper's tape carried on the day of publication, that is between roughly $32,600 and $35,800 of bullion, a range derived by this desk from the reported weight and fineness rather than published by anyone. The record is about five times the metal, not four.

The rest is signature and story, and a trade that sets its own making charges should be able to say precisely what each is worth, because that difference is the whole business of a goldsmith.

IIThe maker's name against the melt figure

What the buyer took home is a piece of British advertising history with a hallmark on it. Cadbury's commissioned twelve individually designed 22-carat eggs from Garrard in 1983 for a nationwide treasure hunt, burying Perspex caskets across the country, each holding a clue to one egg; a thirteenth was made in secret.

This one, called Cuckoo Cuckoo, is engraved and enamelled in translucent green, blue, orange and white, worked with a cat, a weather vane, stars, mice, a castle and a treasure hunter finding a golden egg. Greg Bateman, managing director at Batemans, called the eggs "a fascinating piece of British cultural history".

IIIA desk with almost nothing to price

The result lands on a desk that has had very little to price. This paper has filed one auction story in the past three editions, Bonhams' three-city Chanel series on 8 August, and made the same point there.

The house estimated a Robert Goossens costume bangle made for Chanel around 1960 at $13,000 to $20,000, against Chanel Fine Jewelry earrings from 2024 in 18-carat white gold and diamonds at £2,000 to £3,000. Base metal and paste, correctly attributed, carried roughly five times the estimate of current-production gold and stones.

The Garrard egg is the same trade running the other way: real gold, sold on who made it, for whom, and in what year.

02Corrections1 note

Correction, 10 August 2026: this article first put the egg at 323.6 grams of 22-carat gold, about 296.6 grams fine, and derived a metal value of about $41,400, giving a headline multiple of four times melt. Those figures came from a Batemans catalogue entry describing the secret thirteenth egg, a heavier object than the one that sold. Forbes reports the egg that sold, Cuckoo Cuckoo, as one of the twelve made for the treasure hunt and gives its weight as nine ounces. The weight, the metal value, the multiple and the headline are corrected above. The hammer price, the date, the auction house and the estimate multiple were right as printed. This paper's standards desk found the error on 9 August.
