Seventy dollars to Southampton: the jewelry trade pitches its summer tent

The Hamptons Jewelry Show returns July 23–26 — 36,000 square feet at the Southampton Fairgrounds, a $200 opening preview, $70 day passes, and two new pavilions aimed squarely at where American luxury money spends August.

By The Retail Desk

19 July 2026

What changed

The second annual Hamptons Jewelry Show opens Thursday, running July 23–26 across a 36,000-square-foot complex at the Southampton Fairgrounds on County Road 39.

What it means

  • Watch the copycats, not the receipts. If a curated retail fair can hold margins in the Hamptons for a second summer, Aspen, Palm Beach and Nantucket editions are a matter of time — and the case for meeting the customer where she vacations, rather than where the trade convenes, gets a proof point the majors will have to answer.

Source: The show · at a glance

Price the ticket like the zip code

Entry is priced like the neighborhood: $200 for the VIP opening preview on Thursday evening, 5 to 9:30 p.m., and $70 for a day pass Friday through Sunday, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m.

The producers — the team behind the Hamptons Fine Art Fair, with 40-year trade veteran Hilary Joy Diaz curating — are betting that the American jewelry customer worth traveling for is already out east.

Year two adds structure to the bet. A Piazza Italia pavilion gathers Italian designers and goldsmithing under one banner, while a new Studio Artists Galleria showcases individual artisans.

The exhibitor list mixes returning names — Brilliant Stars, Getana & Co., Greenwich Bazaar, Heera Moti, Shana Kroz, Treasure Jewelry, Jenni Gault — with more than a dozen newcomers including Parisian watchmaker Charles Oudin, German Kabirski, Ana Katarina and the Carbon Watch Company, per National Jeweler.

An art fair, not a trade floor

The format itself is the interesting part. Trade shows are built where wholesale buyers are; the Hamptons show inverts the model and builds where the end customer summers, collapsing designer, dealer and collector into one tent with a ticket price that filters for intent.

It is closer to an art fair than to Vegas — which is precisely the comparison its producers, who run one, intend.

The marginal dollar summers out east

Nothing about a fairgrounds tent replaces the order books written at the majors. But as a reading of where the trade thinks the marginal American luxury dollar lives in July — within driving distance of a $70 ticket and a sandy parking lot in Southampton — it is hard to improve on.
