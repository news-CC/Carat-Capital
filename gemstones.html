What’s moving now

Market · Global

The big three outrun diamonds again

Untreated Burmese rubies, Kashmir-quality sapphire and vivid Colombian emerald keep setting per-carat records. Scarcity is structural: the great mines are old and the new finds are small.

Certification · Lucerne

Origin is the price

A ruby's passport matters as much as its color. Lab reports naming Mogok or Kashmir multiply value — which makes origin science, and origin fraud, the desk's permanent beat.

Supply · Lusaka

Tender economics tighten midstream

Producer tenders in Zambia and Mozambique now set benchmark rough prices with auction transparency — squeezing the traditional dealer margin and professionalizing a famously handshake market.

The working vocabulary

The trade’s terms, plainly told. More in the Field Guide.

The Big Three Term
Emerald, ruby, sapphire — the trio that anchors colored-stone value. Everything else, however beautiful, trades as 'semi-precious' economics with rare exceptions like paraíba and spinel.
Treatment disclosure Practice
Most gems are heated, oiled or filled to improve appearance; the sin isn't treatment, it's silence. 'No indications of heating' on a lab report can multiply a stone's price tenfold.
Origin determination Science
Labs read a stone's chemistry and inclusions like a birth certificate — Mogok ruby, Panjshir emerald, Ceylon sapphire. It's expert judgment, not barcode certainty, and labs occasionally disagree.
Pigeon's blood Grade
The trade's most contested color term: the pure vivid red, historically Burmese, that marks the summit of the ruby market. Whose lab gets to say it is a running commercial war.

  4. This week, August 20 to September 23

    GSI begins issuing Pigeon Blood, Royal Blue and Padparadscha reports in India, the first time the colour trade names have been granted at home

    Gemological Science International has begun issuing coloured stone reports carrying the recognised colour trade names, reported by Rapaport on 19 August 2026: Pigeon Blood for ruby, Royal Blue and Padparadscha for sapphire. The reports issue from GSI's Jaipur, Mumbai, New York and Bangkok operations, and it is the first time such reports have been issued in India, where stones previously had to travel abroad to be granted a name. GSI defines each designation by specified ranges of hue, tone and saturation assessed under standardised lighting and supported by spectroscopic and colorimetric analysis, and grants them only to untreated stones; heated, diffused or otherwise enhanced material is not eligible whatever its colour. Meenu Brijesh Vyas, GSI's global head gemologist, said of the change: That ends today. SSEF's published criteria are comparable: pigeon blood red requires strong vivid red saturation with no or slight purplish tint, strong ultraviolet fluorescence from high chromium and low iron, no colour zoning from above, freedom from eye-visible dark inclusions and no fissure or cavity filling, and applies only to rubies showing no indication of treatment; royal blue requires strong vivid blue without greenish, greyish or purple tint, an iron-titanium charge transfer spectrum, colour homogeneity and good transparency.

    Source: Rapaport, 19 August 2026; SSEF published colour-term criteria

  5. This week, August 20 to September 23

    GIA and Gübelin confirm Ethiopia as a fourth source of Paraíba tourmaline, three months after SSEF called it inconclusive

    The Gemological Institute of America and the Gübelin Gem Lab announced jointly on 27 August 2026 that Ethiopia is a new source of copper-bearing tourmaline meeting the widely accepted definition of Paraíba tourmaline, which had previously been produced in Brazil, Nigeria and Mozambique. The two laboratories reached the conclusion independently and on separate material. Gübelin first encountered stones with an unfamiliar chemical signature in early May 2026. GIA began examining goods represented as Ethiopian in June 2026 and later received a parcel of more than one hundred rough and faceted samples. Both name elevated concentrations of vanadium, lead and gallium as the separating markers and describe them as measurable determinations; neither has published thresholds, and the detailed findings are going to Gems & Gemology. Colours reported run from blue to bluish green, violet, purple and colourless, with colour zoning and compositional variation within single stones. In June 2026 the Swiss Gemmological Institute SSEF reported credible accounts of an Ethiopian deposit and said its own preliminary trace-element work on submitted stones showed considerable overlap with previously known localities, particularly Brazil, calling origin determination on some specimens difficult and in some cases inconclusive. No price, parcel value or carat weight has been published for Ethiopian material. For price context this paper reported on 30 July 2026, from Rapaport's January survey, that Mozambican Paraíba had moved from around $7,000 a carat to between $10,000 and $12,000 a carat.

    Source: GIA and Gübelin joint statement via JCK, 27 August 2026; SSEF, June 2026; National Jeweler, 23 June 2026
