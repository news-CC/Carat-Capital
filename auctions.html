Auctions & Estateswhere the trade marks itself to market

Every hammer price is a data point the whole industry reprices against. The auction desk previews and reports the jewelry sales at Christie's, Sotheby's, Phillips and Bonhams, tracks private treaty and estate flows, and reads provenance the way analysts read balance sheets.

The briefing — what's moving now

Updated each edition
B-01

Signed period jewels carry the season

Cartier Art Deco, JAR, early Bulgari — signed and dated material clears high estimates while generic goods labor. The market is paying for authorship, not just material.

Results · Geneva
B-02

Estate supply is the decade's quiet flood

Generational wealth transfer is bringing decades of collections to market. The houses are building entire departments to catch it — and the trade buys its future inventory there.

Supply · New York
B-03

Private sales eat the middle

Six-figure stones increasingly trade by private treaty instead of the room — faster, quieter, fee-flexible. The public sale is becoming the shop window; the deal happens after.

Structure · Global

Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
Hammer vs. premiumTerm

The hammer price is what the gavel confirms; the buyer's premium (20–27%) goes to the house on top. Read carefully: reports mix the two, and the difference is the house's entire business.

ProvenanceConcept

A jewel's chain of ownership. A documented duchess multiplies value like a lab report multiplies a ruby's — history is the one gem that can't be mined.

ReservePractice

The confidential minimum below which a lot won't sell. 'Bought in' means the reserve wasn't met — auction-speak for a price the market refused.

Fresh to marketSignal

A piece unseen for decades. Freshness is the auction world's scarcity premium; a jewel flipped twice in five years trades tired.

Auctions This week — August 17 to September 3

A stainless Patek Philippe Calatrava reference 448 found in a Cornish house clearance makes £59,000 at Hansons, 11.8 times its low estimate

A stainless steel Patek Philippe and Co Calatrava, reference 448, serial number 822731, case number 610667, dated to the 1930s, sold for £59,000 at Hansons Cornwall in Penzance on 25 August 2026. The estimate was £5,000 to £10,000, so the result is 11.8 times the low estimate and 5.9 times the high. The watch was found in a jewellery box during a house clearance in Camborne after the owner's death and had been a family gift for decades. The buyer bid by telephone from Hong Kong, after competition reported from Japan and China. Reported dollar equivalents for the same £59,000 differ between outlets, $79,892 (National Jeweler) and $78,765 (syndicated wire copy), implying conversion rates of 1.3541 and 1.3350; this paper prints the pound. No report states whether £59,000 is the hammer price or includes the buyer's premium. Calendar context checked by this desk the same morning: Sotheby's next jewellery sales are The Gem Drop, New York, 14 to 17 September and High Jewelry, Hong Kong, 17 September; Bonhams closed jewellery sales on 1 and 2 September and publishes no totals.

Source — National Jeweler, 1 September 2026; InStore; Talker News, 31 August 2026; Sotheby's upcoming auctions calendar and Bonhams jewellery results page, both read directly by this desk 2 September 2026.
Auctions This week — August 17 to September 3

Doyle New York takes $457,700 for a 12.46-carat ring, and an 1890 sapphire pair beats its book by 180%

Doyle's Important Jewelry sale in New York was led by a 12.46-carat cut-cornered step-cut emerald cut, D colour, VS2, between tapered baguettes, at $457,700 against a $350,000 high estimate, which is $107,700 or 30.8% above the ceiling on this desk's arithmetic, and $36,733.55 a carat. Rapaport's report of 24 August, drawing on Doyle's own website, puts the whole sale above $8 million. A JAR ring of about 1980 with a 5.78-carat G VS2 cushion modified brilliant made $165,600 against $80,000 to $120,000, or 1.38 times the high. A Cartier Mahango Panthere necklace of more than 500 diamonds weighing about 30 carats made $133,850 against $50,000 to $70,000, 1.91 times the high. A pair of 1890 earrings holding an oval 4-carat Ceylon sapphire and an oval 4.17-carat Kashmir sapphire, each ringed by ten old-mine diamonds, made $140,200 against $30,000 to $50,000, which is 2.80 times the high and 180.4% above it, the widest margin in the top five. A Tiffany and Co. Fleurage bracelet, eight rounds of 10.80 carats and 439 smaller rounds of 25 carats, made $127,500 inside a $100,000 to $150,000 estimate, 15% below the high, the one lot of the five that did not beat its book. The five together made $1,024,850, at most 12.8% of a sale of more than $8 million. Doyle publishes no origin or treatment detail in the results this desk read, so the sapphires' laboratory origin call and heat status are unknown and are not asserted here.

Source — Rapaport News, 24 August 2026
Auctions This week — August 17 to September 3

A Verdura brooch with royal provenance makes 23,750 pounds at Elmwood's, and a 1927 Van Cleef feather makes 75,000

Elmwood's Auctioneers sold a Verdura unicorn-head brooch, about 1.10 carats of diamonds under a circular cabochon turquoise crown, made by Fulco di Verdura, for 23,750 pounds or $32,400 at its London sale of 19 August 2026, reported by Rapaport on 24 August. It belonged to Virginia Fortune Ryan Ogilvy, dowager countess of Airlie, a lady-in-waiting to Queen Elizabeth II for close to fifty years. Three lots named in the same report sold for more: a 1927 Van Cleef and Arpels Art Deco diamond feather brooch at 75,000 pounds or $102,229, an Art Deco Egyptian Revival tiara at 65,000 pounds or $88,595, and a Cartier tutti frutti brooch of about 1940 at 60,000 pounds or $81,780. The Van Cleef made 3.16 times the royal brooch on this desk's division. Each dollar figure divided by its sterling figure returns 1.36305, 1.36300, 1.36300 and 1.36421, so one conversion rate near $1.363 to the pound was applied across the sale rather than four separately struck prices. The four named lots total 223,750 pounds or $305,004. Elmwood's published no sale total in the report this desk read, so the four lots' share of the sale is not stated. Priced as jewellery the Verdura returned $29,455 for each carat of diamond content, which is the signature and the provenance rather than the goods.

Source — Rapaport News, 24 August 2026
Auctions This week — August 17 to September 3

Bonhams Sydney runs 39 of its first 48 lots away, with the 4.74ct D flawless still to come

Bonhams opened its Fine Jewellery sale in Sydney at 18:00 AEST on 25 August 2026, which is 04:00 New York time, and this desk read the house's own live board at 06:00 New York time, two hours in. Of the first 48 lots, 39 carried a result, eight showed an estimate and no price, and lot 11, a Concord 18ct gold and diamond lady's bracelet watch estimated AU$10,000 to AU$15,000, was marked withdrawn. Lots showing an estimate and no price are recorded as that and not as bought in, because a live page can lag the room. The sale carries 135 lots; 87 were still to come at press time. Prices are as the house publishes them, inclusive of a buyer's premium stated at 23% on the hammer. The leading lot, an unmounted 4.74 carat round brilliant, D colour and internally flawless, is estimated at AU$120,000 to AU$180,000 on Bonhams' own catalogue and had not been reached; Rapaport reported the sale on 23 August 2026 with the upper estimate alone, AU$180,000 or about $129,030, and this desk adds the lower figure from the house. It is the only lot in the sale estimated above AU$100,000 and the house's own filter puts the catalogue range at AU$1,000 to AU$180,000. Two lots tied for the top of the run at AU$55,350, a Van Cleef & Arpels white gold and diamond Frivole necklace and an Alhambra necklace from the same maison, both estimated AU$30,000 to AU$50,000 and both clearing above the high estimate. Below them a Hardy Brothers emerald and diamond ring took AU$19,680, a Cerrone diamond ring AU$18,450 and a Chopard Les Chaines bracelet AU$15,990. The eight lots without a price span AU$2,000 to AU$60,000 in estimate, so the run did not fail at the top and clear at the bottom, but the three largest estimates anywhere in the first 48 lots are all among them and none of the eight carries a maison name. Bonhams does not publish estimates against sold lots, so this desk cannot state what the 39 that cleared were expected to make.

Source — Bonhams, Fine Jewellery Sydney, live board read 25 August 2026 06:00 ET / Rapaport, 23 August 2026 / Diamond City, 25 August 2026
Auctions This week — August 17 to September 3

Bonhams opens 287 lots of jewellery in Los Angeles inside eight days

Bonhams' own Los Angeles listing, read 21 August 2026, shows Weekly: Jewelry running online from 18 to 25 August with 112 lots, and Modern Native American Art and Jewelry as a live sale on 26 August with 175 lots, 287 lots in total. Neither sale has a published result. A correction of this paper's own record is carried in the article: the Bureau filed the weekly sale at 48 lots and the house's listing says 112, so the house's figure was printed. The aggregator entry for this series resolves to a February 2025 edition of the same sale, which is why the house's own page was used to establish both counts and both dates. That February 2025 edition offered work by Paloma Picasso for Tiffany, Valentin Magro, Gübelin, Gucci and Ippolita in a band running from about $100 to $4,000, cited as a description of a prior edition rather than of the sale now open, because Bonhams has published no verifiable estimate range for the current one. A lot-level estimate reported elsewhere for a Jesse Monongya necklace could not be verified against the house's catalogue and was not printed. The 26 August date settles the question that caused this desk to kill an earlier version of the story on 17 August 2026.

Source — Bonhams Los Angeles auction listing, read 21 August 2026 / Carat Capital archive, 17 August 2026
