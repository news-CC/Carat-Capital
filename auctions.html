Auctions & Estateswhere the trade marks itself to market

Every hammer price is a data point the whole industry reprices against. The auction desk previews and reports the jewelry sales at Christie's, Sotheby's, Phillips and Bonhams, tracks private treaty and estate flows, and reads provenance the way analysts read balance sheets.

B-01

Signed period jewels carry the season

Cartier Art Deco, JAR, early Bulgari — signed and dated material clears high estimates while generic goods labor. The market is paying for authorship, not just material.

Results · Geneva
B-02

Estate supply is the decade's quiet flood

Generational wealth transfer is bringing decades of collections to market. The houses are building entire departments to catch it — and the trade buys its future inventory there.

Supply · New York
B-03

Private sales eat the middle

Six-figure stones increasingly trade by private treaty instead of the room — faster, quieter, fee-flexible. The public sale is becoming the shop window; the deal happens after.

Structure · Global

Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
Hammer vs. premiumTerm

The hammer price is what the gavel confirms; the buyer's premium (20–27%) goes to the house on top. Read carefully: reports mix the two, and the difference is the house's entire business.

ProvenanceConcept

A jewel's chain of ownership. A documented duchess multiplies value like a lab report multiplies a ruby's — history is the one gem that can't be mined.

ReservePractice

The confidential minimum below which a lot won't sell. 'Bought in' means the reserve wasn't met — auction-speak for a price the market refused.

Fresh to marketSignal

A piece unseen for decades. Freshness is the auction world's scarcity premium; a jewel flipped twice in five years trades tired.

Auctions This week — August 17

Bonhams opens 287 lots of jewellery in Los Angeles inside eight days

Bonhams' own Los Angeles listing, read 21 August 2026, shows Weekly: Jewelry running online from 18 to 25 August with 112 lots, and Modern Native American Art and Jewelry as a live sale on 26 August with 175 lots, 287 lots in total. Neither sale has a published result. A correction of this paper's own record is carried in the article: the Bureau filed the weekly sale at 48 lots and the house's listing says 112, so the house's figure was printed. The aggregator entry for this series resolves to a February 2025 edition of the same sale, which is why the house's own page was used to establish both counts and both dates. That February 2025 edition offered work by Paloma Picasso for Tiffany, Valentin Magro, Gübelin, Gucci and Ippolita in a band running from about $100 to $4,000, cited as a description of a prior edition rather than of the sale now open, because Bonhams has published no verifiable estimate range for the current one. A lot-level estimate reported elsewhere for a Jesse Monongya necklace could not be verified against the house's catalogue and was not printed. The 26 August date settles the question that caused this desk to kill an earlier version of the story on 17 August 2026.

Source — Bonhams Los Angeles auction listing, read 21 August 2026 / Carat Capital archive, 17 August 2026
Auctions This week — August 17

Three closed Sotheby's jewel sales carry no published result between them

As at 17 August 2026, Sotheby's own past-results index carries no total, no sell-through rate and no top lot for three closed jewellery sales. The Gem Drop August, an online sale in New York, closed on 13 August after four days of bidding, four days before this reading. The Arcade Sale in Hong Kong ran from 31 July to 6 August and closed eleven days before this reading. The Gem Drop July closed on 16 July, thirty-two days before this reading, and its sale page likewise carries no result; this paper covered that sale on 17 July 2026 as eleven-lots-no-premium, describing it as the sixth in a monthly series. Sotheby's landing page for the Gem Drop series, read the same day, describes it as a monthly release of jewels through the house's Sealed platform and carries a heading for past auctions with no auctions listed beneath it. Lot pages and estimates remain accessible; realised prices do not. One limitation is recorded: Sotheby's renders parts of these pages through scripts this desk's fetch does not execute, so the finding is what the public page returns to a reader rather than proof that no figure exists internally. Against this, the same house's New York High Jewelry sale of 16 June 2026 made $43.4 million with 98% of its 119 lots sold, led by a 10.02-carat fancy intense blue diamond at $8.7 million, a total in the trade press the following day, and Sotheby's reported a $4.4 billion first half for 2026 with private sales at a record $826 million. The Gem Drop charges no buyer's premium, the feature that makes its performance data materially interesting to consignors and to retailers. This paper's own recommendation of 14 August 2026, that the missing Gem Drop result be retired as a story after four days, is corrected here.

Source — Sotheby's results index, Gem Drop July sale page and Gem Drop series page (all read 17 August 2026) / Carat Capital archive
Auctions Week of August 3

A 1983 Garrard 'Conundrum' egg makes £123,500, about five times its metal

A 22-carat gold Cadbury's 'Conundrum' egg, named Cuckoo Cuckoo and made by Garrard & Co. of London in 1983, sold for £123,500, about $166,600, at Batemans Auctioneers & Valuers in Stamford, Lincolnshire on 1 August 2026. Forbes reported the result on 8 August and the auction house describes it as a world auction record for a bejewelled egg of its type. The price is close to six times the pre-sale estimate and about eighteen times the £6,500 the egg fetched at its previous appearance; Forbes dates that earlier sale to 2018 while the Batemans catalogue entry for the same lot is dated July 2017, and this paper carries both readings rather than resolving them. Forbes gives the egg at nine ounces of 22-carat gold, engraved and enamelled in translucent green, blue, orange and white with a cat, a weather vane, stars, mice, a castle and a treasure hunter finding a golden egg. At 91.7% fineness that is between about 7.52 and 8.25 troy ounces of pure gold, depending on whether Forbes means troy or avoirdupois ounces, worth roughly $32,600 to $35,800 at the $4,341.30 an ounce this paper's tape carries from Kitco's 5:00pm New York close on 7 August. That metal figure is derived by this desk from the reported weight and fineness and is not published by any source. Corrected 10 August 2026: this entry first carried 323.6 grams and about $41,400 of bullion, taken from a catalogue entry describing the secret thirteenth egg rather than the one that sold, and called the result four times melt; on Forbes's nine ounces it is about five times. The egg was one of twelve individually designed 22-carat eggs commissioned by Cadbury's in 1983 for a nationwide treasure hunt, with Perspex caskets buried across the United Kingdom each holding a clue to one egg; a thirteenth was made in secret. Greg Bateman, managing director at Batemans, called the eggs a fascinating piece of British cultural history.

Source — Forbes / Batemans catalogue via the-saleroom / Kitco
Auctions Week of August 3

Bonhams sets three Chanel sales for September across New York, Paris and London

Bonhams announced on 7 August 2026 a three-city Chanel auction series for September. New York holds Chanel: From the Vault II from 7 to 17 September, led by a gold-tone bird cage minaudière from Virginie Viard's Métiers d'Art Paris-31 rue Cambon collection, set with simulated turquoise and multicoloured crystals, estimated at $20,000 to $30,000; highlights go on view at the house's Manhattan flagship from 17 August. Paris holds Chanel: From the Vault II Paris Edition from 14 to 24 September with roughly 400 lots spanning Karl Lagerfeld's 1990s work to Viard's recent collections, led by a white tweed jacket from the autumn/winter 2025 ready-to-wear collection at €2,000 to €3,000, about $2,300 to $3,460, and including a Lagerfeld-era brooch with rhinestones and turquoise-coloured paste at €700 to €900, about $810 to $1,040. London holds Designer Handbags and Fashion from 21 September to 1 October and carries the heaviest jewellery weighting, with a private collection of Chanel costume pieces from the 1960s to the 1990s led by a filigree snake bangle made by Robert Goossens for Chanel Haute Couture circa 1960, estimated at $13,000 to $20,000, against a pair of Chanel Fine Jewelry Comète Géode earrings from 2024 in 18-carat white gold and diamonds estimated at £2,000 to £3,000, about $2,690 to $4,040. The signed costume bangle therefore carries roughly five times the estimate of the current-production gold and diamond earrings from the same maison, which prices attribution and documentation rather than materials. This is the first substantial auction-house announcement this desk has been able to file in three editions.

Source — JCK / WWD / Bonhams
Auctions Week of August 3

Joopiter opens a standalone watch department with a rolling online sale closing 11 August

Joopiter, the digital auction house founded by Pharrell Williams, opened Keeping Measure: Watches and Timepieces, the first sale from the standalone watch department it announced in July under Nate Borgelt, formerly of Bonhams. Lots close on a rolling basis through 11 August. The top lot is an Omega Speedmaster engraved for Alan Bean, the fourth man to walk on the moon, one of thirty examples of the reference produced after the first moon landing and distributed to President Nixon, Vice President Agnew and astronauts of the period; this is number 26, engraved for Bean's command of the 1973 Skylab mission, estimated at $250,000 to $350,000 with bidding at $230,000. Other lots include an HYT Moon Runner Red Magma from 2022 at $50,000 to $70,000 and a Ulysse Nardin with a Gay Frères bracelet from around 1960 at $15,000 to $25,000, alongside Patek Philippe, Audemars Piguet, Piaget, Cartier, Ressence, Speake-Marin and Ōtsuka Lotēc. The sale runs against an empty summer calendar; the next priced offering of note is Sotheby's Gem Drop in New York, 10 to 13 August. Watch auction sales reached $680 million in the first half, up 45%, with 73 lots clearing $1 million.

Source — JCK / stupidDOPE / Joopiter
