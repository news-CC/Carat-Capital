Signed period jewels carry the season

Cartier Art Deco, JAR, early Bulgari — signed and dated material clears high estimates while generic goods labor. The market is paying for authorship, not just material.

Private sales eat the middle

Six-figure stones increasingly trade by private treaty instead of the room — faster, quieter, fee-flexible. The public sale is becoming the shop window; the deal happens after.

Structure · Global

Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
Hammer vs. premiumTerm

The hammer price is what the gavel confirms; the buyer's premium (20–27%) goes to the house on top. Read carefully: reports mix the two, and the difference is the house's entire business.

ProvenanceConcept

A jewel's chain of ownership. A documented duchess multiplies value like a lab report multiplies a ruby's — history is the one gem that can't be mined.

ReservePractice

The confidential minimum below which a lot won't sell. 'Bought in' means the reserve wasn't met — auction-speak for a price the market refused.

Fresh to marketSignal

A piece unseen for decades. Freshness is the auction world's scarcity premium; a jewel flipped twice in five years trades tired.

Auctions Week of August 3

A 1983 Garrard 'Conundrum' egg makes £123,500, about four times its metal

A 22-carat gold Cadbury's 'Conundrum' egg, named Cuckoo Cuckoo and made by Garrard & Co. of London in 1983, sold for £123,500, about $166,600, at Batemans Auctioneers & Valuers in Stamford, Lincolnshire on 1 August 2026. Forbes reported the result on 8 August and the auction house describes it as a world auction record for a bejewelled egg of its type. The price is close to six times the pre-sale estimate and about eighteen times the £6,500 the egg fetched at its previous appearance; Forbes dates that earlier sale to 2018 while the Batemans catalogue entry for the same lot is dated July 2017, and this paper carries both readings rather than resolving them. The catalogue puts the egg at 323.6 grams of 22-carat gold, engraved and enamelled in translucent green, blue, orange and white with a cat, a weather vane, stars, mice, a castle and a treasure hunter finding a golden egg. At 91.7% fineness that is roughly 296.6 grams, or 9.54 troy ounces, of pure gold, worth about $41,400 at the $4,341.30 an ounce this paper's tape carries from Kitco's 5:00pm New York close on 7 August. That metal figure is derived by this desk from the stated weight and fineness and is not published by any source. The egg was one of twelve individually designed 22-carat eggs commissioned by Cadbury's in 1983 for a nationwide treasure hunt, with Perspex caskets buried across the United Kingdom each holding a clue to one egg; a thirteenth was made in secret. Greg Bateman, managing director at Batemans, called the eggs a fascinating piece of British cultural history.

Source — Forbes / Batemans catalogue via the-saleroom / Kitco
Auctions Week of August 3

Bonhams sets three Chanel sales for September across New York, Paris and London

Bonhams announced on 7 August 2026 a three-city Chanel auction series for September. New York holds Chanel: From the Vault II from 7 to 17 September, led by a gold-tone bird cage minaudière from Virginie Viard's Métiers d'Art Paris-31 rue Cambon collection, set with simulated turquoise and multicoloured crystals, estimated at $20,000 to $30,000; highlights go on view at the house's Manhattan flagship from 17 August. Paris holds Chanel: From the Vault II Paris Edition from 14 to 24 September with roughly 400 lots spanning Karl Lagerfeld's 1990s work to Viard's recent collections, led by a white tweed jacket from the autumn/winter 2025 ready-to-wear collection at €2,000 to €3,000, about $2,300 to $3,460, and including a Lagerfeld-era brooch with rhinestones and turquoise-coloured paste at €700 to €900, about $810 to $1,040. London holds Designer Handbags and Fashion from 21 September to 1 October and carries the heaviest jewellery weighting, with a private collection of Chanel costume pieces from the 1960s to the 1990s led by a filigree snake bangle made by Robert Goossens for Chanel Haute Couture circa 1960, estimated at $13,000 to $20,000, against a pair of Chanel Fine Jewelry Comète Géode earrings from 2024 in 18-carat white gold and diamonds estimated at £2,000 to £3,000, about $2,690 to $4,040. The signed costume bangle therefore carries roughly five times the estimate of the current-production gold and diamond earrings from the same maison, which prices attribution and documentation rather than materials. This is the first substantial auction-house announcement this desk has been able to file in three editions.

Source — JCK / WWD / Bonhams
Auctions Week of August 3

Joopiter opens a standalone watch department with a rolling online sale closing 11 August

Joopiter, the digital auction house founded by Pharrell Williams, opened Keeping Measure: Watches and Timepieces, the first sale from the standalone watch department it announced in July under Nate Borgelt, formerly of Bonhams. Lots close on a rolling basis through 11 August. The top lot is an Omega Speedmaster engraved for Alan Bean, the fourth man to walk on the moon, one of thirty examples of the reference produced after the first moon landing and distributed to President Nixon, Vice President Agnew and astronauts of the period; this is number 26, engraved for Bean's command of the 1973 Skylab mission, estimated at $250,000 to $350,000 with bidding at $230,000. Other lots include an HYT Moon Runner Red Magma from 2022 at $50,000 to $70,000 and a Ulysse Nardin with a Gay Frères bracelet from around 1960 at $15,000 to $25,000, alongside Patek Philippe, Audemars Piguet, Piaget, Cartier, Ressence, Speake-Marin and Ōtsuka Lotēc. The sale runs against an empty summer calendar; the next priced offering of note is Sotheby's Gem Drop in New York, 10 to 13 August. Watch auction sales reached $680 million in the first half, up 45%, with 73 lots clearing $1 million.

Source — JCK / stupidDOPE / Joopiter
Auctions Week of August 3

Watch auction sales rise 45% to $680 million with 73 lots above a million dollars

EveryWatch's first-half 2026 report put watch auction sales at $680 million, up 45% year on year, inside a total secondary market of $10.5 billion. Seventy-three lots cleared $1 million, close to three times the same period a year earlier, and three lots passed $10 million. The highest was an F.P. Journe Chronometre a Resonance Souscription number 007, which made $13,922,000 at Phillips New York on 14 June 2026, the highest auction price for a watch by an independent maker. Phillips also placed a Resonance Souscription number 18 at $6.3 million and a Resonance Pisa at $3 million across the spring season. The median watch across all channels sold for $5,900, up 19%, and took an average of 43 days to sell against 39 a year earlier.

Source — EveryWatch via JCK / Robb Report
Auctions Week of July 27

Heritage books a record $1.41 billion half and its largest jewelry sale ever

Heritage Auctions took more than $1.41 billion in the first six months of 2026, up 47% year on year and the highest midyear total in its fifty-year history. Its Spring Fine Jewelry Signature auction on May 4 realised $9,713,640, the highest-grossing jewelry auction in the firm's history, beating the $9.2 million set in September 2025. The top lot was a platinum ring set with a 6.59-carat octagonal Kashmir sapphire at $906,250, more than $300,000 above its high estimate. Across the majors, first-half global sales at Christie's, Sotheby's and Phillips rose 70% to $6.8 billion with fees, with Sotheby's alone at a record $4.4 billion.

Source — ARTnews / Intelligent Collector / National Jeweler / The Art Newspaper
