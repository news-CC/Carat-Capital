AGTA takes 12.5% gemstone tariff to Bangkok trade talks

Thai finished jewellery entering the United States carries combined duties of 17.5% to 19.5%, the association says. It met Thailand's vice minister for commerce and passed the discussion to the US Trade Representative.

By The Gemstones Desk24 September 2026Brief 30 sec · Full read 2 min
What changed

Finished goods pay more. The AGTA told Thai officials in Bangkok that loose coloured gemstones from Thailand enter the United States at 12.5%. Finished Thai jewellery carries combined duties of 17.5% to 19.5%.

What it means · The Desk’s View

If you import loose stones from Thailand: The rate the association named is 12.5%, five to seven points below what the same goods carry once set.

If you buy finished Thai jewellery: The band runs 17.5% to 19.5% and no change to it has been announced.

What this is not: An agreement, or news of one. No deadline, no value and no counterparty position was published.

Carat Capital's arithmetic the third column is a subtraction from the first, shown so it can be checked: 17.5 − 12.5 = 5.0 points; 19.5 − 12.5 = 7.0 points. These are percentage-point differences and not ratios, so nothing here says finished goods pay a given percentage more than loose ones. Combined the association describes the finished-jewellery figure as combined duties, meaning more than one line of duty summed, and does not break it down. What has no number the association published no value for the trade exposed, no volume, and no date by which an agreement would have to be reached; the last row records that absence rather than leaving it out.

IThe meeting had a name

One official is named. The association held a private discussion with Kirida Bhaopichitr, Thailand's vice minister for commerce, who led a delegation of senior officials. The meetings ran alongside the Bangkok Gems and Jewelry Fair.

Washington was told after. The AGTA said it shared details of the discussion with the office of the US Trade Representative. No response from that office is reported.

IINo dollar figure was published

The exposure is unpriced. The association put no value on the Thai trade at risk and named no deadline for an agreement. It said Thailand risks losing market share to competing suppliers without one.

One outlet carries it. This report rests on a single trade write-up of the association's Monday statement. A JCK headline on related AGTA tariff work was not reachable at the URL tried.

IIIThe loose rate is not new

The tariff predates this. Carat Capital reported the same 12.5% rate on loose coloured stones on 23 July 2026. The 17.5% to 19.5% band on finished Thai goods has not run here before.

The depthMethod, sources, corrections · open what you need
01What would change this call+

A published exposure figure. The case made here rests on a rate gap with no trade value attached to it; a dollar figure for Thai gem and jewellery shipments to the United States would either make the gap material or make it small. None was published, and none is estimated here.

02A combined duty is more than one line+

A tariff rate on an import line is rarely a single number. A combined duty sums a most-favoured-nation rate with whatever additional measures apply to that country and product, which is why the association gives a band of 17.5% to 19.5% for finished Thai jewellery rather than a figure. The loose-gemstone rate it names, 12.5%, is quoted flat. Because the two are not built the same way, the difference between them is a percentage-point gap between two stated rates rather than a clean comparison of like duties.
