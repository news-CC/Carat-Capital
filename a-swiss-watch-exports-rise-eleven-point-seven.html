Watches

Swiss watch exports rise 11.7%, a fourth straight monthly gain

September shipments reached CHF 2,225.9m, FH figures show. The US rose 85.7% and France 128.2%, gains FH ties to a base effect and possible trade reallocation, while China fell 14.2%.

By Carat Capital

20 October 2026

What changed

A fourth monthly gain. Swiss watch exports rose 11.7% to CHF 2,225.9 million in September, the fourth consecutive monthly rise, FH figures show.

What it means

  • If you hold US allocation: Shipments rose 85.7%, and FH puts it down to a favourable base.
  • If you sell into China: Exports there fell 14.2%, which FH also blames on the base.
  • What this is not: Retail sales. FH counts shipments leaving Switzerland, not watches sold.

Steel and bimetal led value

Nine months positive. From January to September, exports reached CHF 19.5 billion, up 2.7% on the same months of 2025.

Steel drove volume. Steel watches rose 13.0% in value, to CHF 698.7 million, and 21.5% in units.

Bimetal rose fastest. Gold-steel models gained 29.3% to CHF 418.5 million, while precious-metal watches rose 1.3%.

Sub-CHF 500 watches jumped. Watches priced below CHF 500 at export rose 39.5%, which FH singles out as a particularly sharp increase.

Two markets carried the month

America swung back. US exports rose 85.7% to CHF 292.9 million. In August they had fallen 19.4%.

France needs caution. France rose 128.2% to CHF 246.5 million. FH says the figure may reflect trade reallocated from other markets in the region.

The rest fell. On our arithmetic, the US and France added about CHF 274 million, more than the CHF 233 million total gain. The other markets shipped about CHF 40 million less.

What to watch

  • 19 November 2026FH's October figures, the date its statistics page gives for the next release.
  • MonthlyWhether France's rise reverses as EU trade flows settle, which FH says to read with caution.

What would change this call

An October in which the US falls back below its 2025 level. Then September was a base effect and nothing more, and the four-month run rests on France.

What FH counts

FH publishes the value and units of watches leaving Switzerland each month, by market, material and export price. These are exports to distributors and subsidiaries, not sales to consumers, and FH says they can be revised. A favourable base means the same month a year earlier was weak, so the percentage flatters the level.
