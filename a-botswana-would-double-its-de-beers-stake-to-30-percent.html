Botswana would double its De Beers stake to 30%, its president says

President Duma Boko told parliament the state's 15% would rise once the Penny-led consortium buys Anglo American's shares. Anglo says binding documents are still to be agreed.

By Carat Capital

10 November 2026 Corrected

What changed

Boko says it would double. President Duma Boko told parliament on 9 November that Botswana's 15% of De Beers would rise to 30% after the consortium buys Anglo American's stake.

What it means

  • If you buy De Beers rough: the state that runs Debswana's mines with De Beers would own twice its present share of the seller.
  • If you follow the sale: Anglo American says it would keep working towards binding documents.
  • What this is not: a priced deal. No price for Botswana's added stake is reported.

Key figures

Botswana's planned stakeon completion, Boko says30%
Botswana's stake nowBoko said, in parliament15%
Anglo American's stakefor sale since 202485%
Bloomberg's 13 October figurereported: would likely reachabout 25%

Source: President Duma Boko's speech to Botswana's parliament, 9 November 2026, as reported by Reuters, AP and JCK on 9 November · proposed, not signed

The president put 30% on record.

Double the current share. Boko said the stake would double from 15% to 30% "when this transaction is complete", Reuters reported. JCK carries both figures; AP carries the 30% and Anglo's 85%.

Above Bloomberg's figure. Bloomberg reported on 13 October that Botswana was close to a deal lifting its stake to about 25%. The figure Boko gave parliament is five points higher.

Largest holder, if signed. Boko said Botswana would become the single largest shareholder in De Beers, Reuters and AP report.

Anglo has not signed yet.

Not yet finalised. Anglo American said it would keep working with Botswana, other governments and the consortium towards binding transaction documents, Reuters reported. AP said the purchase had not been finalised.

Penny names four governments. Gareth Penny, the former De Beers chief executive who leads the consortium, said it was working with Botswana, Angola, Namibia and Oman towards co-ownership. Boko gave no details for Namibia or Angola, AP reported.

First refusal gave leverage. Botswana holds a right of first refusal on Anglo's stake, Reuters reported, which makes its backing key for the sale.

Element Six joins a US plan.

Boko cites planned plant. Boko said Element Six, De Beers' synthetic diamond business, would take part in a planned $600 million US plant, which Reuters first reported in January.

Boko says Washington asked. The US government asked Element Six to partner on synthetic diamond technology, Boko said.

What to watch

  • Anglo American's next statement on De BeersBinding documents with the consortium. The test: whether they carry Botswana at 30% and name a price.
  • Not yet scheduledNamibia and Angola. The test: whether either government names the share it would take.

The story so far

  1. 18 October 2026Bloomberg reported Botswana close to a deal lifting its stake to about 25%.
  2. 18 October 2026Richemont and Signet were reported in talks to join the Global Diamond Consortium.
  3. 30 July 2026Bloomberg put the consortium's price for Anglo's 85% at about $1 billion.
  4. 20 July 2026A minister told parliament the state's 15% carried freedom to join, pre-empt or bid with a partner.

What would change this call

If the binding documents give Botswana less than 30%, or the consortium does not sign with Anglo, Boko's figure reads as a negotiating position rather than an outcome.

How De Beers is owned

Anglo American owns 85% of De Beers, AP reports, and Botswana 15%, Reuters and JCK report; Anglo put its stake up for sale in 2024. Botswana holds a right of first refusal on that stake, Reuters reports, so its consent shapes any sale. The two also run Debswana, the joint venture that operates Botswana's largest mines.
