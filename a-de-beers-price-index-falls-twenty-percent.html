De Beers' rough price index falls 20% as sales volume nearly doubles

Third-quarter output fell 11% to 6.8 million carats as Orapa and Venetia slowed. Rough sales rose 4% to $731m, and 2026 guidance rises to 25–28 million carats with all of Gahcho Kué.

By Carat Capital

20 October 2026 Corrected

What changed

Prices fell as volume rose. De Beers' rough price index fell 20% year on year in the third quarter, while consolidated sales volume rose 91% and sales revenue 4%.

What it means

  • If you buy at the Sights: The index fell 6% on the second quarter, though De Beers reports signs of stability.
  • If you plan Q4 supply: Jwaneng maintenance is expected to cut output well below third-quarter rates.
  • What this is not: A clean price read. The average fell 45%, the index 20%; we read the gap as mix.

Maintenance cut the output

Botswana lost most carats. Production fell 11% to 6.8 million carats. Botswana produced 4.8 million, down 20%, because of planned plant maintenance at Orapa.

Venetia is winding down. South Africa's output fell 37% to 0.4 million carats as Venetia ramped down ahead of a planned two-year pause. De Beers expects no more Venetia carats this year.

Canada doubled. Gahcho Kué's attributable output rose 108% to 1.06 million carats on planned processing of higher-grade ore.

More carats, cheaper carats

Volume carried revenue. Consolidated sales reached 8.7 million carats against 4.6 million a year earlier, bringing in $731 million from two Sights.

The quarter's average fell. The average realised price fell 45% to $84 a carat, against $154 a year earlier.

De Beers explains the year. Year to date the average fell 37% to $97. De Beers cites an 18% lower index and a more balanced sales mix.

The index fell less. The price index, an average across the period's Sights, stood at 65 against 81 a year earlier and 69 in the second quarter.

Gahcho Kué lifts the guidance

Ownership changed hands. De Beers took full ownership of Gahcho Kué on 16 October by enforcing security over Mountain Province's 49% interest. It will report the mine at 100% from the fourth quarter.

Guidance rose. Production guidance for 2026 moves to 25–28 million carats, from 21–26 million. De Beers says the change reflects the Gahcho Kué ownership, not new global supply.

Costs fell too. Unit cost guidance drops to about $70 a carat from about $80, on Gahcho Kué's larger share, the Venetia pause and cost control.

What to watch

  • Q4 2026Planned plant maintenance at Jwaneng, which De Beers expects to cut output substantially from third-quarter rates.
  • Next SightWhether the price index holds at 65 or falls again.
  • Q4 2026 reportThe first quarter with Gahcho Kué at 100%, which raises De Beers' reported carats without adding to world supply.

What would change this call

A rise in the price index at the next Sights. Then the third quarter's 20% fall marks the bottom of the cycle, and the 91% volume rise reads as restocking at a low.

How the De Beers price index works

The index averages De Beers' rough prices across the Sights in a period, relative to 100 at December 2006. The average realised price also moves with the mix of goods sold; De Beers attributes the year-to-date fall to both. The 2025 values are restated to include the effect of De Beers' stock rebalancing.
