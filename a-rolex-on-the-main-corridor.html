2,800 square feet for the crown: Tysons gets its Rolex

Liljenquist & Beckstead, family jewelers since 1978, opened a 2,800-square-foot Rolex boutique in Tysons Galleria on July 22 — bronze-brass façade, walnut walls, and a Rolex-trained watchmaking workshop built to be watched.

By The Watch Desk

23 July 2026

What changed

The Washington suburbs now have a Rolex address to match their tax bracket. Liljenquist & Beckstead opened a 2,800-square-foot Rolex boutique on July 22 along the main corridor of the recently renovated Tysons Galleria in McLean, Virginia, the fourth location for a family firm that has sold jewelry in the capital's orbit since 1978.

What it means

  • A visible workshop is the smartest square footage in watch retail; it converts a waiting-room chore into proof of competence, and proof of competence into the thing Rolex actually rations, trust. The dependency question is real but academic: no family jeweler has ever gotten smaller by building the crown a nicer room.
  • The desk notes the address, a renovated luxury mall rather than a Manhattan avenue, and reads it as the American watch market's actual center of gravity: the affluent suburb, not the flagship district.

An address to match the bracket

Lisa Colen, the company's director of operations, called it "a distinguished home for Rolex in the Mid-Atlantic".

The build is the point

The build is the point. A sawtooth storefront in bronze and brass gives way to American walnut ceilings and walls, travertine-framed display windows and a lounge with private and semi-private consultation rooms.

The theatrical centerpiece is a Rolex-trained watchmaking workshop set behind glass, with a dry bar positioned so customers can watch a movement being serviced while they wait. Service, the least glamorous half of the watch business, has been promoted to the shop window.

Fewer doors, grander ones

The opening extends a pattern this page has been logging all year: the crown's retail footprint is consolidating into fewer, grander doors operated by its most trusted partners. Beaverbrooks just converted a 165-year-old Sheffield showroom into its fourth Rolex-anchored boutique; the brand's certified pre-owned program keeps pulling secondary-market demand into the same authorized rooms.

For independent jewelers, a dedicated Rolex boutique is simultaneously the best franchise in retail and a deepening dependency on a single Geneva landlord.
