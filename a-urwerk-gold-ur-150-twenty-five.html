Urwerk launches a gold UR-150 at $140,000, 25 pieces

The 18-carat yellow gold case is listed at CHF 105,000 before tax in Switzerland, 19.3% over the titanium version. Urwerk made 50 of that one, per variant.

By The Watches Desk

11 September 2026

What changed

Gold, and half the run. Urwerk has put the UR-150 in 18-carat yellow gold as the Golden Scorpion, limited to 25 pieces at $140,000 each in the United States.

What it means

  • If you retail independents: the gold premium here is 19.3% over the titanium list, on half the production.
  • If you value a UR-150: the 2024 Titan at CHF 88,000 is the comparable, not the gold.
  • What this is not: a US price verified at a dealer. It is a trade-press figure in a different currency from the Swiss list.

Gold costs 19.3% more

Seventeen thousand francs. The gold Scorpion lists at CHF 105,000 before tax against CHF 88,000 for the titanium Titan, a difference of CHF 17,000.

The run halved. Urwerk is making 25 Golden Scorpions. Each 2024 variant ran to 50 pieces.

Two prices, two bases

Dollars against francs. JCK reports $140,000 each in the United States. Monochrome reports CHF 105,000 excluding taxes. Neither source states the rate or the duty between them.

Printed, not reconciled. Both figures stand as their sources give them. Converting one into the other would require an exchange rate and a tax treatment that neither publishes.

The case is the story

Grooves, not a movement. The 2N yellow gold case middle is satin-finished and microblasted, with concentric grooves, over a sandblasted Grade 5 titanium back. The satellite hour display is unchanged.

Same size, new metal. At 42.5mm wide the Golden Scorpion matches the 2024 case within half a millimetre. The change is material and run size, not dimension.

What would change this call

A US list under $130,000 at an authorised dealer. That would make $140,000 a converted Swiss price rather than a set US one, and the two-bases reading above would collapse into one.

How a satellite hour display works

Urwerk replaces hands with carriers that rotate three or four hour numerals around the dial. Each numeral travels along a printed minute track and then drops away as the next takes its place. The complication is read as a moving position rather than a pointer, and it is the house signature across the UR range.

Method

Three pages were read directly rather than summarised: JCK's 9 September report, which gives $140,000 each and 25 pieces; Monochrome's specification panel for the Golden Scorpion, which gives CHF 105,000 excluding taxes, 25 pieces and a 42.5mm case; and Monochrome's 2024 panel for the original Scorpion, which gives CHF 88,000 for the Titan, CHF 89,000 for the Dark, 50 pieces per variant and a 42.49mm case. The percentages are Carat Capital's arithmetic on the Swiss francs only: 105,000 against 88,000 is +19.32%, rounded to 19.3%, and 89,000 against 88,000 is +1.14%. Outside sources disagree in effect on the price, and that disagreement is disclosed rather than resolved: $140,000 is a United States retail figure and CHF 105,000 is a Swiss list excluding taxes, and neither publisher gives a rate or a duty, so no conversion is attempted and neither figure is presented as the other. Archive test, both periods named: the archive holds no article mentioning Urwerk, the UR-150 or the Scorpion in any period, so there is nothing to advance on. This item was not filed by the watches beat; it was found in the same feed that desk had already fetched.
