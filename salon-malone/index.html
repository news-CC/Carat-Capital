The call

What Malone actually says

It opens honest, makes one offer, names two concrete times and takes the first yes. A warm no ends the call with no rebuttal.

Under the call

It listens on a budget of milliseconds

A turn has to land inside about nine hundred milliseconds or the pause sounds wrong to a human ear. That single constraint picks every part of the stack.

~900 msTurn budget

Past roughly this, a person hears the gap and knows what they are talking to. It is the constraint every other number here was chosen to fit inside.

nova-2-phonecallTranscriber

Trained on 8 kHz telephony rather than studio audio. A general-purpose speech model hears a phone line as damage and spends its accuracy repairing it.

120Max reply tokens

A hard cap, not a suggestion. Long answers are exactly what makes a voice agent sound like it is reading to you, and the cap is cheaper than a prompt asking nicely.

0.3 sEndpointing wait

Semantic, not silence-based: it predicts where your sentence ends instead of waiting out a gap. Most of the perceived lag in a voice agent hides in that wait.

180 sHard ceiling

The call ends itself. Nobody is ever kept on the phone by a machine that will not stop, and the agent has a hang-up tool so it does not have to wait to be released.

How it runs

Six steps, then it is quiet

Upload, scrub, dial, book, report. There is no dashboard to learn and nothing for your clients to install.

  1. 01

    Tell us about the salon

    Two minutes on the start form: the salon, your timezone, your average ticket, roughly how many clients have drifted, and the offer you want Malone to make. The form tells you which plan fits before you pay for anything.

  2. 02

    Send the list

    Export the clients who have not booked in six months — a CSV from whatever your booking software is. We find the columns ourselves: name, phone, last visit, consent. There is no template to fill in and no mapping screen.

  3. 03

    We scrub it before anyone rings

    Rows without consent are dropped. Bad numbers are dropped. Anyone on the do-not-call list is dropped. You see the counts for every reason before a single call goes out.

  4. 04

    Malone calls, inside your hours

    One attempt per person, ever, in your salon's local time. Malone opens by saying it is a virtual concierge, offers two concrete times, and takes the first yes.

  5. 05

    You get the email immediately

    Name, phone, the slot they asked for, and what was said. It lands while the chair is still open, so your front desk can confirm it the same hour.

  6. 06

    Friday, the arithmetic

    One email: dialled, reached, booked, opted out, and recovered revenue at your average ticket. If the number is bad, you will see it on Friday, not in six months.

The arithmetic

Empty chairs, priced honestly

A two-chair salon, $120 average ticket, eighteen months of clients who drifted off. Every number below is an assumption we picked to show the shape of the arithmetic.

  1. Names exported from the booking system 0your list
  2. Survive the scrub (consent + valid phone) 0we drop the rest
  3. Answer the phone 035% of dials
  4. Book a specific time 012% of conversations
  5. Actually sit in the chair 080% show rate
  6. Recovered revenue, month one $025 shows × $120

Against $399 for the month. Two numbers on purpose: our Friday email counts booked visits at your ticket — 31 × $120 is $3,720 — and the $3,000 above is that same month if only 25 of them show. No-shows count in ours, so your book is the last word. Your list, your offer and your show rate will move every line — up or down. We report the real ones every Friday.

Legal & safe

The rules are in the code

Six things the system will not do — each enforced where the call is actually placed, and each checked in more than one place.

No consent, no call

Consent is a gate at import, not a policy in a document. A row that is not marked consented is dropped before it can enter the dial queue, and you see the dropped count with a reason for every row.

Opt-out is instant and global

“Take me off the list” writes to a do-not-call list shared across every salon we run, before the call report is even accepted. That number is never dialled again, by us, for anyone.

One attempt per person, ever

Not a retry policy — a condition in the query that picks who to call. There is no second pass, no drip, no next-month list. If the window closes before we reach them, the attempt is given back, not spent.

Your hours, not ours

The calling window is evaluated in each salon's own timezone. A timezone we cannot read fails closed: no call goes out at all rather than one going out at the wrong hour.

Malone says what it is

The disclosure is in the opening line and repeated whenever anyone asks. It never claims to be a person, never argues, and hands anything off-topic back to your front desk number.

Voice and email only

No SMS, no chatbot, no app for your clients to install. The only two things that ever happen are a phone call to them and an email to you.

Read this part twice

We are engineers, not your lawyers. Outbound calling is regulated — TCPA in the US and its state analogues, with rules on consent, calling hours, disclosure and do-not-call handling — and what counts as valid consent for a voice call is not obvious from a spreadsheet column named “consent”. The gates above are how we built the system; they are not legal advice and they are not a compliance programme. Have your own counsel review the consent language your clients agreed to before the first list is dialled.

Straight answers

The questions that actually decide it

Will this annoy my clients?
It is built so it cannot become a nuisance. One call per person, ever — no retry queue, no drip, no second pass next month. Ninety seconds is the target and three minutes is a hard stop. A warm no ends the call immediately, with no rebuttal. It only dials inside the calling window, read in your salon's own timezone — never at 7am, never late. The part that decides whether it is welcome is the offer, and we write that with you before anything dials.
Do I need their consent?
Yes, and we enforce it rather than trusting it. Your export needs a consent column; rows that do not say yes are dropped at import and never enter the dial queue, and you get the dropped counts with a reason per row. We cannot manufacture consent for you — it has to be consent your clients actually gave your salon. Have your counsel look at the language they agreed to before the first campaign.
What if someone asks to be removed?
Malone says “done — you're off the list, be good” and ends the call. That number is written to a global do-not-call list before the call report is accepted, and pulled out of every pending queue we run. If the write ever failed, the report is rejected so it is delivered again — the one thing we will not risk losing is an opt-out. It can never be dialled again, by us, for anyone.
Can I hear it before it calls anyone?
Yes, and that is how every account starts. The first campaign we run for you is a one-row list: your own mobile, your salon name, your offer. You pick up, you hear exactly what your clients would hear, and the booking email lands in your inbox. Nothing else is dialled until you say go.
What if nobody books?
You will know by Friday, in writing, with the dial and answer counts behind it. Usually the offer is the problem rather than the list, so we rewrite it and run the remainder. The pilot is $299 once with no auto-renew, and the monthly plans are month to month — cancel and the calling stops that day. We would rather lose a subscription than defend a bad number.
Will they know it is not a person?
Yes. Malone says it is the salon's virtual concierge in its opening line, and answers straight away if anyone asks whether it is a bot or a recording. It never claims to be human and it never dodges the question. Anything it cannot handle — prices, anything medical, anything off-topic — goes to your front desk number.
How many times will you call one person?
Once. Ever. A win-back campaign that pesters people costs you more goodwill than it recovers, so there is no mechanism in the product to call anyone twice.
What happens to my client list?
It is used to run your campaigns and nothing else. We never sell it, share it, or dial it for another salon. Recordings and transcripts stay with our telephony provider — we store the link, not a copy. Ask us and we delete your list. The full detail is in the privacy notice.
Is there texting?
No. Voice calls and email only. No SMS, no chatbot, no app for your clients to install.
