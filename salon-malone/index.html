Legal & safe

The rules are in the code

Six things the system will not do — each one enforced where the call is actually placed, and each one checked in more than one place.

No consent, no call

Gate at import

A row not marked consented is dropped before it can enter the dial queue — and you see the dropped count, with a reason, for every row.

Opt-out is instant, global

Mid-call write

“Take me off the list” writes to a do-not-call list shared across every salon we run — before the call report is accepted. Never dialled again, by us, for anyone.

One attempt, ever

In the claim query

Not a retry policy — a condition in the query that picks who to call. If the calling window closes first, the attempt is given back, not spent.

Your hours, not ours

Fails closed

The window is evaluated in each salon’s own timezone — 9:00 AM to 7:00 PM by default, yours to narrow. A timezone we cannot read means no call at all.

Says what it is

Opening line

The disclosure is in the first sentence and repeated whenever anyone asks. It never claims to be human, never argues, and hands anything off-topic to your front desk.

Voice and email only

Nothing else exists

No SMS, no chatbot, no app for your clients to install. The only two things that ever happen: a phone call to them, an email to you.

Read this part twice

We are engineers, not your lawyers. Outbound calling is regulated — TCPA in the US and its state analogues — and what counts as valid consent for a voice call is not obvious from a spreadsheet column named “consent”. The gates above are how we built the system; they are not legal advice. Have your own counsel review the consent language your clients agreed to before the first list is dialled.

Straight answers

The questions that actually decide it

Will this annoy my clients? +

It is built so it cannot become a nuisance: one call per person ever, ninety seconds target, three minutes hard stop, a warm no ends the call immediately, and it only dials inside your salon’s local hours. The part that decides whether it is welcome is the offer — and we write that with you before anything dials.

Do I need their consent? +

Yes, and we enforce it rather than trusting it. Your export needs a consent column; rows that do not say yes are dropped at import and never enter the dial queue. We cannot manufacture consent — it has to be consent your clients actually gave your salon. Have your counsel look at the language before the first campaign.

Can I hear it before it calls anyone? +

Yes — that is how every account starts. The first campaign is a one-row list: your own mobile, your salon name, your offer. You pick up, hear exactly what your clients would hear, and the booking email lands in your inbox. Nothing else is dialled until you say go.

What if nobody books? +

You will know by Friday, in writing, with the dial and answer counts behind it. Usually the offer is the problem rather than the list, so we rewrite it and run the remainder. The pilot is $299 once with no auto-renew; monthly plans cancel any day and the calling stops that day.

Will they know it is not a person? +

Yes. Malone says it is the salon’s virtual concierge in its opening line and answers straight away if anyone asks. It never claims to be human and never dodges the question. Anything it cannot handle goes to your front desk number.

What happens to my client list? +

It runs your campaigns and nothing else. We never sell it, share it, or dial it for another salon. Recordings and transcripts stay with our telephony provider — we store the link, not a copy. Ask us and we delete your list.
