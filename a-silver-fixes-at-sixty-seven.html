Silver fixes at $67.035 in London, highest since 28 August

Kitco's Friday close put spot silver at $66.127, down 1.06% on the session and 1.35% below the London fix. Gold's fix eased to $4,348.15 and palladium's rose to $1,315.50.

By The Prices Desk19 September 2026Brief 25 sec · Full read 1 min
What changed

The fix outran the screen. The LBMA silver price fixed at $67.035 on 18 September, its highest since 28 August. Kitco's close for the same session was $66.127.

What it means · The Desk’s View

If you buy physical silver on the fix: Friday's cost was 1.35% above the screen close, a gap of $0.908.

If you price retail off spot: the two instruments parted on one session. Name which one your tickets use.

What this is not: a new high. August's $70.26 fix stands above every September reading.

01What would change this call+

A Monday fix back under $66. One fix is one auction at one minute of one day, and a single session that parts from spot can rejoin it on the next.

02How the LBMA silver price is set+

The LBMA silver price is an electronic auction, not a survey. Accredited participants enter buy and sell volumes in successive rounds until the imbalance falls inside a set tolerance, and the price at which that happens becomes the day's benchmark. It is struck once a day in London and settles physical contracts worldwide. Spot, by contrast, is a continuously quoted dealer bid. The two answer different questions and can differ by more than a percent on a busy session.

03Method · the desk’s arithmetic+

The four LBMA fix series were fetched directly on 19 September 2026 with a browser user agent, at 898,020 bytes for silver, 914,722 for gold, 556,807 for platinum and 553,750 for palladium; each last record is dated 2026-09-18. The superlative was traced rather than asserted: every silver fix in the series was compared with Friday's $67.035, and the most recent higher one is 28 August's $70.26, with all fourteen September fixes below it. The spot figures are Carat Capital's own tape of 19 September, which records the 18 September session as final and is the paper's mark of record under the One-Price Rule; no press-time re-read was made and the tape's marks print as filed. The gap is stated on the fix's base and the division is printed with it.
