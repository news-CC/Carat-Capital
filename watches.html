Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
Grey marketTerm

Unauthorized but legal dealers trading new watches outside official channels. Grey premiums and discounts are the truest real-time price signal in the industry — we quote them daily.

ManufactureConcept

A maison that builds its own movements rather than buying them. The word carries a price premium and a century of Swiss industrial politics.

ReferencePractice

A watch's model number — collectors trade references the way equity desks trade tickers. One digit can double a price at auction.

ComplicationTerm

Any function beyond telling the time: chronograph, perpetual calendar, minute repeater. Complexity is the currency of horological prestige — and of service costs.

Watches This week — August 17 to September 3

Hublot prices the Big Bang Original Soft Touch at $24,900 with texture overmoulded into the rubber rather than applied as a coating

Hublot introduced the Big Bang Original Soft Touch on 1 September 2026 at $24,900, in two colourways, All Black and All Red, running the in-house HUB1280 column-wheel chronograph conceived, developed and assembled at the manufacture in Nyon. The textured rubber compound is produced by an overmoulding process that embeds the texture during manufacture rather than applying it afterwards as a coating or surface treatment, and it runs across case, bezel, dial and strap. All Black revisits the monochrome concept the house first showed in 2006; All Red draws on the red ceramic introduced in 2018. Positioned against this paper's reporting of 20 August 2026 on the Federation of the Swiss Watch Industry's July figures: exports of watches with an export price above CHF 3,000 rose 12% by value inside a total up 9.6%, though that threshold is an export price and not a retail one, so the comparison is stated in print as directional only.

Source — JCK, 1 September 2026; Time and Tide Watches; Gear Patrol; Federation of the Swiss Watch Industry July 2026 figures as reported by Carat Capital, 20 August 2026.
Watches This week — August 17 to September 3

Movado's net sales rose 4.9% to $169.8 million in the quarter to 31 July, and net income was 4.10 times the prior year

Movado Group filed its Form 10-Q on 26 August for the three months to 31 July 2026. Net sales rose $7.9 million or 4.9% to $169.8 million. Watch and Accessory Brands made $142.9 million, up 5.3%, and Company Stores $26.9 million, up 2.8%, putting the watch segment at 84.2% of the company on this desk's arithmetic. Inside the watch segment, licensed brands grew $8.3 million or 9.0% while owned brands grew $0.3 million or 0.7%. United States locations made $47.5 million, up 5.9%; international $95.4 million, up 5.0%, including $0.7 million of favourable currency, without which sales would have risen 4.4%. Net income attributable to Movado Group was $12.3 million against $3.0 million, or 4.10 times, and $19.2 million against $4.4 million across six months, or 4.36 times. Gross profit rose $13.3 million and gross margin expanded approximately 530 basis points, roughly 380 of them attributed by the company to sales mix, and gross profit included $3.2 million of IEEPA duty refunds received, a little over a quarter of the quarter's net income. The company operated 57 retail outlet locations at both period ends. Carat Capital reported on 26 August that Fossil Group's net sales fell 4.9% in its own quarter to 4 July; the two quarter ends are twenty-seven days apart and are neighbouring periods, not the same one.

Source — Movado Group Form 10-Q, SEC EDGAR, filed 26 August 2026
Watches This week — August 17 to September 3

Fossil Group's quarter to 4 July falls 4.9% to $209.7 million, and the watch line falls 1.5%

Fossil Group's Form 10-Q for the thirteen weeks to 4 July 2026, filed with the SEC on 13 August, puts net sales at $209,688 thousand against $220,388 thousand for the quarter to 5 July 2025, a fall of $10.7 million or 4.9%. On the company's own table of net sales by class of similar products, traditional watches fell from $178,446 to $175,746 thousand, which is 1.5%; smartwatches from $1,400 to $1,388 thousand; total watches from $179,846 to $177,134 thousand, 1.5%. Leathers fell from $16,947 to $11,679 thousand, a drop of $5,268 thousand or 31.1%, which is roughly half the company's entire decline from a line worth under 6% of sales. Jewellery fell from $19,390 to $17,220 thousand, $2,170 thousand or 11.2%. Across twenty-six weeks traditional watch sales rose, from $363,076 to $364,477 thousand, up $1,401 thousand or 0.4%, while total watches were down 0.25%, leathers down 34.6% and jewellery down 10.3%, on half-year net sales down 4.2% from $453,681 to $434,449 thousand. Watches rose as a share of quarterly sales from 81.6% to 84.5%, jewellery slipped from 8.8% to 8.2% and leathers from 7.7% to 5.6%. The filing is thirteen days old and cannot lead under this paper's fourteen-day rule.

Source — Fossil Group Form 10-Q, filed 13 August 2026, SEC EDGAR
Watches This week — August 17 to September 3

Rolex's Dallas school took 27 of more than 560 applicants, and America's watchmaker count is down 85.2% since 1953

Matthew Burdeen, chief executive of Burdeen's Jewelry of Chicago, told Watch Insider on 14 August 2026 that the shortage of qualified watchmakers is one of the biggest structural problems in the watch industry, with intake barely replacing retirement while the installed base needing service grows; the Centurion carried the account on 18 August 2026. Burdeen's is building a 7,000 square foot watchmaking, service and operations facility due in 2027. Rolex's Watchmaking Training Center in Dallas took 27 students from more than 560 applicants in 2024, a figure Fortune reported and the Centurion cites, which is one place for every 20.7 applicants on this desk's arithmetic. North Seattle College's Watch Technology Institute accepts an average of 10 to 12 students a year on an eight-quarter certificate. The institute's own employment page returned HTTP 403 to this desk on 25 August 2026, so its outcome figures are carried from the trade press rather than verified at source, and two different quantities circulate, an 87% employment rate over five years of cohorts and a 91% completion rate; this paper prints neither as settled. Watch Insider's study of 25 February 2026 put roughly 44,000 qualified United States watchmakers in 1953 on Department of Labor and American Watchmakers-Clockmakers Institute records against about 6,500 in 2021 on Bureau of Labor Statistics figures, a fall of 85.2% across 68 years on this desk's arithmetic. Jason Champion of the AWCI put the imbalance at at least ten employers seeking a watchmaker for every watchmaker seeking work, and Switzerland's employers' convention has worked to a need for roughly 4,000 additional watchmakers. No brand publishes a median service turnaround and no trade body publishes an aggregate repair backlog, so the shortage is argued from intake and job ratios rather than from measured queues.

Source — Watch Insider, 14 August 2026 / the Centurion, 18 August 2026 / Watch Insider, 25 February 2026 / North Seattle College Watch Technology Institute (HTTP 403 to this desk, 25 August 2026)
Watches This week — August 17 to September 3

Swiss watch exports rise 9.6% in July and the seven-month year turns positive

The Federation of the Swiss Watch Industry published July 2026 export figures on 20 August 2026. Total exports were CHF 2,628.3 million, up 9.6% on July 2025, and cumulative performance over seven months moved into positive territory at plus 0.9%, against minus 0.7% for the half on CHF 12.8 billion as this paper reported on 21 July 2026, a swing of 1.6 points. Wristwatches were CHF 2,522.7 million, up 9.8%, on 1,523,400 units, up 6.8%, or 97,000 additional watches. By material and by value: gold-steel plus 23.8%, other materials plus 11.1%, other metals plus 9.8%, steel plus 9.0%, precious metal plus 3.7%. Precious-metal units rose 0.9% to 37,100 pieces carrying CHF 912.8 million, which is 2.4% of wristwatch units and 36.2% of wristwatch value. This desk's arithmetic on the Federation's table puts the average export price of a precious-metal watch at about CHF 24,600 against about CHF 23,900 a year earlier, up 2.8%, and gold-steel at about CHF 4,970, up 29.5% on 4.4% fewer units. Markets: United States CHF 701.3 million, up 26.5%, a 26.7% share and a third consecutive month of double-digit growth; France plus 104.6%, which the release itself says does not reflect the actual trend in that market; Japan minus 3.7%, China minus 18.5%, United Arab Emirates minus 3.7%; the six largest markets CHF 1,563.8 million, up 21.7%, 59.5% of the whole. By price band on value, above CHF 3,000 plus 12.0% and CHF 500 to 3,000 minus 3.9%. The next release, covering August, is due 17 September 2026.

Source — Federation of the Swiss Watch Industry, July 2026 release (read 20 August 2026) / Carat Capital archive, 21 July 2026
