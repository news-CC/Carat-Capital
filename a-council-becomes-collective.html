Natural Diamond Council renames itself The Diamond Collective

The trade's natural-diamond marketing body dropped the qualifier from its name on 22 September, with a new logo and a stated goal of creating desire. Its first fashion partnership is promised for 2027.

By The Retail Desk23 September 2026Brief 35 sec · Full read 2 min
What changed

The qualifier came off. The Natural Diamond Council renamed itself The Diamond Collective on 22 September. It changed its logo and palette the same morning, dropping the word natural from its own name.

What it means · The Desk’s View

If you retail natural stones: Point-of-sale material carrying the old council's name and logo is now out of date, and no transition period was announced.

If you brief a marketing team: The body says it will lead with desire rather than defence and will stand up a Global Steering Committee to set priorities.

What this is not: New money. The announcement names no budget, no member count, no campaign spend and no timetable beyond one partnership in 2027.

IA name without the qualifier

Council becomes Collective. The Natural Diamond Council is now The Diamond Collective, an industry-funded not-for-profit. It has a new logo, a purple palette and the motto Old Soul, New Energy.

It arrived quietly. JCK reports the change came without media fanfare. It landed through the morning of 22 September as the body updated its social pages and published new ones.

The chief states it. Amber Pepper, described in the statement as chief executive of The Diamond Collective, gives the goal as “more people choosing natural diamond. More often.”

Three goals set. The body names three goals: unite, protect and inspire. Its own site now runs four consumer editions, for the United States, India, the UAE and France.

IIBranding opinion is split

One sees nothing new. Keran Smith of LYFE Marketing told JCK a name change matters to consumers only when it signals something meaningful, and doubts this one does.

One sees a risk. Andrea Quagliato Lebrón of Might & Matter called the move from Council to Collective a strong one. The warning was that dropping natural leaves a name lab-grown stones could sit inside.

IIIThe market it renames into

One carat is lower. RAPI's 1.00 ct index read $3,914.90 on 22 September against $4,125.20 on 1 January, down 5.1% on the year.

A year behind. The same index sits 12.0% under its $4,448.65 level of 1 September 2025.

Small stones differ. RAPI at 0.30 ct reads $972.39 against $845.11 on 1 January, up 15.1%.

Only band higher. That is the only weight band on this paper's price list standing above where it started the year.

01What would change this call+

A budget, or a member list. This is a rebrand with no money attached to it in public; a published campaign spend, or the departure of a funding producer, would change what the name change is worth reading as.

02Why dropping one word is the whole decision+

A trade marketing body's name is its category boundary. 'Natural Diamond Council' told a consumer, in three words, what the organisation was for and what it was against, and it did so by accepting a qualifier that the body's own statement now calls playing defence: the original had to be labelled as natural because something else had taken the unqualified word. 'The Diamond Collective' refuses that framing and reclaims the plain noun. The cost is that the plain noun is exactly what the organisation spent a decade arguing should not be shared. A consumer who has never heard of either name loses nothing. A retailer explaining the difference at a counter loses the one piece of naming that did the explaining for them, and a lab-grown producer gains a name it could plausibly sit inside. That is the trade being made, and it is not a small one.
