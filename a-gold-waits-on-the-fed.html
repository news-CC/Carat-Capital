Gold near $4,061 waits on the Fed

Gold ended the week around $4,061 an ounce, up roughly 2% on the week but still short of $4,100, as traders positioned for a Federal Reserve widely expected to hold rates when it meets July 28 and 29.

§1A market parked, not frightened.

Gold closed the week near $4,061 an ounce, a gain of about 0.3% on Friday and roughly 2% across the five sessions, but still on the wrong side of the $4,100 line it has been probing for a fortnight. The metal is holding a high plateau rather than breaking out, which is the posture of a market waiting for someone else to move first. This week, that someone is the Federal Reserve.

The policy committee meets July 28 and 29, and the consensus expects it to leave rates where they are, echoing the European Central Bank's hold on Thursday. The case for standing still got firmer late in the week when jobless claims came in at 187,000 against a forecast of 212,000, a labor market stronger than the models penciled in. A stronger jobs picture cuts both ways for gold: it eases recession fear, but it keeps alive the prospect of higher rates later in the year if inflation stays above target.

§2The whole game is the rate.

That rate-and-gold relationship is the whole game at these levels. Higher interest rates raise the opportunity cost of holding an asset that pays no coupon, so a hawkish surprise on Wednesday would give the metal a reason to slip back toward $4,000, while any dovish tilt would hand it the excuse to test $4,100 again. The white metals told their own story on Friday: silver added about 1.2% to $58.22, while platinum slipped 1.1% to $1,574.

§3The bench prices a floor and a ceiling.

For the jewelry trade, the number that matters is less the daily print than the shelf it sits on. Gold has spent July defending $4,000 rather than charging past it, and a market that merely holds a record is easier to design around than one that lurches. Manufacturers pricing autumn collections are treating $4,000 as the floor and $4,100 as the ceiling until the Fed says otherwise.

This is a coiled-spring week, and the spring is wound by Wednesday's statement, not by the tape itself. Gold at $4,061 is neither cheap nor frightened; it is parked.

The risk is asymmetric for the counter, because another leg higher squeezes margins that record metal has already stretched thin, while a pullback toward $4,000 would be the first good news the gold bench has had in a month.
