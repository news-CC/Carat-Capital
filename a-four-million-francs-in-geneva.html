Patek Philippe split-seconds carries CHF 4m estimate at Phillips

The 1945 chronograph heads Phillips' Geneva Watch Auction XXIV on 7 and 8 November, a sale of more than 200 lots. An F.P. Journe carries CHF 1m to CHF 2m and a Paul Newman Daytona CHF 1.6m.

By The Watches Desk

18 September 2026

What changed

Phillips set the estimate. A 1945 Patek Philippe split-seconds chronograph carries an upper estimate of CHF 4 million, or $4.9 million, at Phillips' Geneva Watch Auction XXIV.

What it means

  • If you own a vintage Patek: the upper estimate is CHF 4 million. No low estimate is published.
  • If you consign to the autumn season: this sale opens it, on 7 and 8 November.
  • What this is not: a price. An estimate is the house's opinion before the room bids.

Patek leads the catalogue

Upper estimate only. The watch has a silver-toned dial with steel hour markers and a tachymeter scale. Phillips gives an upper estimate only, and no low estimate is published for it.

Two Rolexes and two Journes

Journe carries a band. An F.P. Journe wristwatch with a hand-engraved pink-gold dial carries CHF 1 million to CHF 2 million.

Rolex takes two places. A yellow-gold Daytona with a Paul Newman dial carries CHF 1.6 million, and a 1952 Rolex with a Stelline dial CHF 1.2 million.

The autumn season opens here

Two days, 200 lots. The sale runs on 7 and 8 November with more than 200 timepieces, held with Bacs & Russo, and opens the autumn live season.

The last Geneva sale. Phillips' Geneva Watch Auction XXIII realised $96.3 million in May.

What would change this call

A published low estimate. An upper figure alone says what the house hopes for and nothing about what it will let the lot go for, and the low is the number that governs the room.

Why an upper estimate alone tells you less

Auction houses normally publish a band. The low estimate is the anchor: it sits at or near the reserve, and it is the number a bidder works back from. The high is the ceiling the specialist thinks the piece can reach on a good day. When only the high is published, as here on four of the five lots, the reader is given the ceiling and not the floor, and the spread that carries most of the information is withheld until the catalogue is complete. That is a normal stage of an auction announcement rather than a concealment, but it means no ratio can be computed yet for this sale.

Method

Rapaport's report of 16 September 2026 is the source for the lots, the estimates and the dates, and it attributes them to Phillips' own announcement of Tuesday 15 September. The dollar figures are Rapaport's conversion and are printed at its own precision rather than recomputed at a rate this desk cannot date.
