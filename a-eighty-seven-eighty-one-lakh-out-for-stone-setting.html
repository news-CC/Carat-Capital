₹87.81 lakh of gold never returns from a Wadaj workshop

A C.G. Road jeweller handed 646.470 grams in 17 sets to a Nava Wadaj workshop on 22 December 2025. Two days later three mobile numbers went dead and the rented shop was empty.

By The Counter Desk

12 October 2026

What changed

The shop emptied overnight. A C.G. Road jeweller gave 17 gold sets worth ₹87.81 lakh to a Nava Wadaj workshop for stone-setting. Two days later the shop was gone.

What it means

  • If you send work out to a karigar: The first three sets came back on time. That is what the receipt bought.
  • If you underwrite trade credit: Nothing here was bonded, insured or held against security. A receipt was the whole protection.
  • What this is not: A conviction, a recovery, or a figure any court or valuer has tested. One complainant's valuation and one police complaint.

Every figure is as stated in a police complaint by the jeweller who says he lost the goods. Nothing is adjudicated, no arrest is reported, and the three people named have not been heard.

A receipt bought the trust

Three sets first. Dipen Ashokbhai Soni, his wife Khushbu and their partner Parth Jigneshbhai Soni ran S.S. Ornaments in Nava Wadaj. They finished three sets on time in December 2025.

Then seventeen more. On 22 December the jeweller handed over 17 gold sets and earrings for stone-setting. Khushbu Soni issued the receipt, the newspaper reports.

Ten at night. Asked for the goods back on 24 December, Dipen said to come at 10 pm. All three mobile numbers were switched off after that.

Two addresses, both empty

The shop went. The rented shop in Nava Wadaj had been cleared the night before, the jeweller found on enquiring the next day.

The house too. The three had a residence at New Ranip. That was found locked as well.

Wadaj station now. The complaint sits with Wadaj police, who have begun investigating. No arrest, no recovery and no charge sheet is reported.

The gap is ten months

December to October. The handover was 22 December 2025 and the report is dated 11 October 2026. The newspaper gives no reason for the gap.

Nothing was secured. Stone-setting work moves finished stock to a third party against a slip. No bond, insurance or security appears anywhere in the account.

What to watch

  • Not announcedWhether Wadaj police make an arrest or recover any of the 17 sets. Neither is reported.
  • OngoingWhether the complaint produces a charge sheet naming all three, including the partner who did not issue the receipt.
  • OngoingWhether any Gujarat jewellers' association publishes guidance on securing karigar consignments. None appears in the account.

What would change this call

A recovery, or a court. Everything priced here is one complainant's valuation inside one police complaint, and the three people named have not been heard. A charge sheet, a seizure of the 17 sets, or an independent valuation would each change the reading. So would a reason for the ten-month gap between the handover and the report, which the newspaper does not give.

Why stone-setting work leaves a jeweller unsecured

Jadtar is stone-setting, and in the Indian trade it is almost always sent out. A retailer with a showroom does not keep a setter on the premises; he hands finished or semi-finished gold to a karigar's workshop, which sets the stones and returns the piece. The transaction is ordinary, it happens thousands of times a week in Gujarat alone, and the whole of the retailer's protection is normally a receipt in the workshop's own hand. There is no bond, no escrow, no bailment insurance in common use and no registry of who holds whose stock. That is why the weight matters more than the money in a case like this one: 646.470 grams is a figure both sides can agree on and a court can test, while a valuation of finished sets carrying stones is an assertion until someone appraises them. It is also why the receipt is the only document in the account. When a workshop vacates its premises, the retailer is left holding a piece of paper against a counterparty whose address has stopped being an address, and the loss converts from a trade debt into a police matter with nothing securing it.
