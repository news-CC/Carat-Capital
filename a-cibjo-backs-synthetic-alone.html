Diamonds

CIBJO backs 'synthetic' alone, FTC holds three terms to 2028

The confederation's general assembly and board took up the change in Vicenza on 7 September. Its lab-grown committee had asked to keep one alternative term, and its coloured stone commission voted the same way.

By The Diamond Desk

8 September 2026

What changed

The Blue Books change word. CIBJO's general assembly and board took up the switch to synthetic on 7 September, putting the confederation alongside the London Diamond Bourse and the African Diamond Producers Association in recommending that term alone.

What it means

  • If you print tags outside the US: check your supplier's wording. CIBJO now recommends synthetic alone.
  • If you sell in America: nothing changes. The FTC still allows three terms, and reopens its guides in 2028.
  • What this is not: a ban. Cavalieri says each country follows its own terminology.

Two commissions voted the same way

Diamond and coloured stone. CIBJO's diamond commission proposed the change, having begun studying it in 2025 after the Paris congress. Its coloured stone commission voted for synthetic on 6 September.

The board decides. Under CIBJO's procedures a final nomenclature decision rests with the board, on guidance from Sector A and the general assembly. Both met on 7 September.

One alternative term was requested

The committee asked. CIBJO's Laboratory-Grown Diamond Steering Committee requested that one alternative term, laboratory-grown, be kept, its chair Wesley Hunt told the congress.

A letter sought delay. Hunt said CIBJO had received a letter earlier in September from senior industry participants asking it to defer any final decision. Cavalieri said it would proceed.

The reason given. Udi Sheintal, who heads CIBJO's diamond commission, told the room: “This is not about marketing. It is about telling the truth to the consumer.”

America keeps three terms

Nothing moves before 2028. The Federal Trade Commission allows 3 terms: synthetic, lab-grown and lab-created, a position set in its 2018 Jewelry Guides. The next revision is slated for 2028.

AGTA is lobbying. The American Gem Trade Association told the congress it will keep pressing the FTC to correct the 2018 decision, and is briefing members of Congress.

The market splits by country

Two words, two maps. France and Russia allow only synthetic, while the United States and India use lab-grown or laboratory-grown, CIBJO's release says.

What would change this call

The CIBJO board reopening the Blue Book wording to admit laboratory-grown as a permitted alternative. Its own lab-grown committee asked for exactly that, and the Blue Books are revisable documents.

What the Blue Books are

The Blue Books are CIBJO's grading and nomenclature standards for diamonds, coloured stones, pearls, precious metals and gemmological laboratories. They are guidance rather than law. They bind CIBJO's members and are widely cited by national trade bodies and laboratories, but a jurisdiction's own regulator sets what a tag must say in that market, which is why the FTC's three terms are unaffected.
