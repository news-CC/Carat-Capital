DGCX hands its trading and clearing engine to TCS

Tata Consultancy Services will supply BaNCS for central counterparty clearing and trading, and Quartz for surveillance and connectivity. TCS shares rose 5.19% to ₹2,315 on the National Stock Exchange on 15 September.

By The Metals Desk

20 September 2026 Corrected

What changed

The exchange changes engine. The Dubai Gold and Commodities Exchange has appointed Tata Consultancy Services to replace its trading, clearing and surveillance systems, announced on 15 September.

What it means

  • If you clear on DGCX: The counterparty system is being replaced. No cutover date has been published.
  • If you hold TCS: The market priced the win at 5.19% on the day, on one outlet's reading.
  • What this is not: A contract value. Neither side published what the deal is worth.

Four asset classes on one stack

The scope is broad. DGCX lists derivatives across 4 asset classes, energy, currencies, bullion and equities, and the replacement covers trading, clearing and surveillance for all of them.

Crypto is named. The exchange says the new foundation is meant to carry new asset classes, including crypto-related products.

No price was published

The value is absent. Neither DGCX nor Tata Consultancy Services published a contract value, a term length or a cutover date.

The market priced it. TCS shares rose 5.19% to ₹2,315 on the National Stock Exchange on 15 September, on one outlet's figure, which does not say where in the session the print fell.

Corrections1 note

Citation corrected, 21 September 2026. As published on 20 September this article called the 5.19% and ₹2,315 an intraday figure and said its source, Whalesbook, described the move as intraday rather than as a close. Whalesbook's text was fetched again on 21 September: the words intraday, intra-day, closing and day's high appear nowhere in it, and its own publication stamp of 15:35 India time falls five minutes after the National Stock Exchange's 15:30 close. The source makes no such distinction, so the qualifier has been removed from the dek, the key figures, the body and the table note, and the piece now says only that the source does not state where in the session the print fell. Both figures, 5.19% and ₹2,315, were correct as published and are unchanged. Found by this paper's standards desk.

What would change this call

A programme that slips, or a cutover that is never dated. Exchange platform replacements are announced at signature and measured at go-live, and no go-live has been published here.

What a clearing engine does

A derivatives exchange runs three layers that are often bought together. The trading layer matches orders. The clearing layer stands between buyer and seller as central counterparty, holds margin and guarantees settlement if one side fails, which is the layer that carries the exchange's own risk. The surveillance layer watches the order book for manipulation and reports to the regulator. Replacing all three at once is the widest version of this kind of project, because the clearing layer cannot be cut over independently of the book it clears. Connectivity, the gateway members' systems talk through, is the fourth piece named here.

Method

Business Standard's and The Tribune's reports of 15 September 2026 were both read for the component list, and they carry it identically: TCS BaNCS for central counterparty clearing and securities trading, Quartz for market surveillance, Quartz Gateway for market connectivity, and a generative AI layer for reporting and analytics. The asset classes, energy, currencies, bullion and equities, and the intention to extend to crypto-related products, are from the same reports. The share figure is the weakest evidence in the piece and is marked as such rather than levelled up: 5.19% and ₹2,315 come from one outlet, Whalesbook, which places the move on the National Stock Exchange on 15 September but never says whether the level is a close or a mid-session print. Its own publication stamp reads 15:35 India time, five minutes after the 15:30 close, which points to a close without settling it; NSE's own daily data was not reachable this morning, so the timing is left unstated rather than guessed, and the key figures and the body say single-outlet. No contract value, term length or cutover date was published by either party, and none is estimated here; the key figures print that absence as a zero rather than leaving the cell out. The Bureau's original filing carried this as a single Arabic-language source and also carried a same-day-settled gold contract as the background to it. The English-language primaries above supersede the first point; the second was not verified and is not printed.
