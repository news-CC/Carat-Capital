Barrick's Q3 all-in sustaining cost rises 24% to $1,911 an ounce

The cost was $1,538 a year earlier. Barrick produced 816,000 ounces in the third quarter and realised $4,243 an ounce; attributable output was 2% lower on the year.

By Carat Capital

10 November 2026

What changed

Costs rose 24%. Barrick's all-in sustaining cost rose to $1,911 an ounce in the third quarter, from $1,538 a year earlier. Its realised gold price rose 23%.

What it means

  • If you track mine supply: Barrick kept 2026 output guidance of 2.90 million to 3.25 million ounces.
  • If you hold the shares: it bought back $761 million of shares in the quarter.
  • What this is not: a margin squeeze. On the year the realised price gained $786 an ounce and the cost $373.

Costs rose with royalties and diesel.

Cost up 24%. All-in sustaining cost rose to $1,911 an ounce, Barrick says, on lower grades, higher royalties, diesel and more sustaining capital.

Inside guidance. The figure sits inside 2026 guidance of $1,760 to $1,950, set on a $4,500 gold price assumption.

Attributable output fell 2%.

Mostly weather and access. Attributable production of 816,000 ounces was 2% below a year earlier. Barrick puts it largely down to weather at Veladero and delayed access to high-grade ore at North Mara.

Up 6% without divestments. Excluding divested assets, Barrick says output rose 6% on the year. Against the second quarter it rose 3%.

The price fell from Q2.

Down 4% on Q2. The realised price was 4% below the second quarter's $4,417. The market price fell 5% over the same quarter, Barrick's table shows.

Dividend and buybacks. Barrick declared a quarterly dividend of $0.175, payable on 15 December, and bought back shares under its $3.0 billion programme.

What to watch

  • Fourth quarter 2026North Mara's high-grade ore, which Barrick expects to reach. The test: whether full-year output lands inside 2.90 million to 3.25 million ounces.

What would change this call

If the fourth quarter's cost comes in above the $1,950 top of guidance, the third quarter reads as a step on a rising path rather than a quarter inside the range.

How all-in sustaining cost is built

AISC adds sustaining capital, general and administrative costs and other sustaining items to the cost of producing an ounce. It is not a GAAP measure; Barrick reports it on its attributable share of each mine, divided by ounces sold.
