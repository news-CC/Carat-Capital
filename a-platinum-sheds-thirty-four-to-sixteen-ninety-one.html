Platinum sheds $34 to $1,691 on the 2 October close

Gold closed $4,139.80, silver $60.274 and palladium $1,152.00 on the same mark, all four lower. Kitco's own board puts the session changes smaller, measured against Thursday's close.

By Carat Capital

3 October 2026

What changed

Every metal finished lower. The 2 October session closed with all four precious metals down. Platinum fell hardest, to $1,691.00 on the Kitco bid, a $34.00 loss against the 2 October opening mark.

What it means

  • If you quote metal over the weekend: The board is frozen at Friday's close; there is no Saturday session to read.
  • If you bought on Friday morning: Platinum is $34.00 cheaper an ounce by the close, gold $44.10.
  • What this is not: A single day's move on the house figures. Those run from Friday morning to Friday's close.

Platinum fell the furthest.

Platinum lost most. Platinum closed $1,691.00, down $34.00 against the opening mark and $23.00 on Kitco's own session change of 1.3419%. It is the largest faller on either base.

Gold gave up forty-four. Gold closed $4,139.80, a fall of $44.10 against the opening mark, against Kitco's session change of $36.70, or 0.8787%. Silver closed $60.274 and palladium $1,152.00.

Ranges were wide. Gold traded between $4,124.50 and $4,226.20, a span of $101.70, and closed $15.30 above the session low. Platinum ranged $1,671.00 to $1,749.00.

The board is frozen.

One timestamp, four metals. All four metals carry the identical originalTime of 2026-10-02T17:00:00Z, which appears nine times in the saved page. An identical freeze across four instruments is the board's own evidence of a closed session.

Two bases, both true. The change against the opening mark runs from the 2 October morning reading to the same day's close. Kitco's change field runs from the previous session's close, which is why it is smaller on all four.

Palladium quotes forty wide.

Forty dollars wide. Palladium's bid of $1,152.00 against an ask of $1,192.00 is $40.00 wide, or 3.47% of the bid. Gold's spread is $2.00, platinum's $10.00 and silver's $0.250.

Gold trades tightest. Gold's $2.00 spread is 0.048% of its bid, the narrowest of the four by a wide margin. A spread is a quoted cost of dealing, not a price move.

HSBC trims its forecast.

Four-four-ninety for 2026. HSBC cut its 2026 average gold forecast on 1 October, to $4,490 an ounce from $4,560. The bank said gold may be nearing a bottom after further short-term pressure.

Still above the close. The cut average sits above Friday's close. The division is 4,139.80 over 4,490, or 92.2% of the forecast and 7.8% below it. The new number implies a level above where the metal finished.

What to watch

  • Monday 5 October 2026The next session, and whether platinum holds $1,691.00 and gold $4,139.80. No 3 or 4 October session exists to read.
  • December 2026The further US rate rise HSBC's economists are reported to expect, named as a reason the bank trimmed its average.
  • 2026 full yearWhether HSBC's $4,490 average survives the final quarter. With three months left most of that average is already realised, and the realised year-to-date average is not computable from the sources read here.

What would change this call

A base that flatters the fall. The largest figures above, the $44.10 on gold and the $34.00 on platinum, are measured against a morning reading rather than a previous close, and a morning reading taken at the top of an intraday range will always produce the bigger number. Kitco's own session changes are the smaller and more conventional measure, and they are printed beside the house figures for exactly that reason. If a reader cares only about session-over-session direction, the right column is the right one and the falls are 0.88% to 1.34% rather than 1.05% to 1.97%. What would not change on either base is the ranking: platinum fell most and gold least.

Why a bid and an ask differ

Every figure in the close column is a bid, the price a dealer will pay, and the ask beside it is the price the same dealer will sell at. The gap between them is the spread, and it is a quoted cost of dealing rather than a market move: a metal whose spread widens has not fallen, it has become more expensive to trade. The spread is set by how much inventory a dealer must carry and how easily it can be hedged, which is why gold at $2.00 on a $4,139.80 bid is the tightest of the four and palladium at $40.00 on an $1,152.00 bid is the widest by a factor of seventy-two on the same division. Palladium is the thinnest of the four markets by volume and its spread reflects that, not a view on its price. The house convention since 31 August 2026 is to carry the bid as the single mark, so every price above is a bid and the asks are stated only to size the spreads.

Method

Kitco's board was fetched with a browser identity at press time and saved as raw bytes before any figure was written: 200, 197,730 bytes. The press-time re-read under the One-Price Rule returned the same values the tape filed at 05:00, so the opening mark stands and no write-back was needed. Each digit printed was grepped in the saved page, with hit counts: '4139.8' 2, '4141.8' 1, '4226.2' 1, '4124.5' 1, '60.274' 2, '60.524' 1, '62.201' 1, '59.573' 1, '1691' 2, '1701' 1, '1749' 1, '1671' 1, '1152' 5, '1192' 1, '1227' 1, '1143' 1, and the frozen stamp 'originalTime":"2026-10-02T17:00:00Z' 9. The percentage figures in the right column are Kitco's own changePercentage field; the raw float substrings '0.878726', '0.971001', '1.341890' and '1.030927' each return 2 hits in the saved bytes and are printed rounded to four decimals. Spreads and ranges are Carat Capital's subtraction on Kitco's own bid, ask, high and low fields. The 2027 pair, 4,825 from 4,925, is NOT printed in this article: Newsquawk returns zero hits for both digits and TradingView's page returned 200 with zero hits for all four, so only the 2026 figures are carried, each on two independent doors.
