India's polished carat fetches $671.39, its grown carat $66.51

GJEPC's April-to-August statement puts natural polished exports 7.38% lower in value on 6.75% more carats. Grown polished shipped 80.24 lakh carats against natural's 67.34. The council's figures are provisional.

By Carat Capital27 September 2026Brief 30 sec · Full read 2 min
What changed

Both carats got cheaper. GJEPC's April-to-August statement prints natural polished at $671.39 a carat and grown polished at $66.51. India shipped more of both and earned less on each.

What it means · The Desk’s View

If you cut and polish for export: Volume is not the constraint. Natural carats shipped rose 6.75% while their value fell 7.38%, so what moved is the rate the goods fetched.

If you price grown goods: The council's own per-carat print is $66.51, about a tenth of the natural figure. That is the ratio to hold against a supplier's quote.

What this is not: A crossover. Grown carats already exceeded natural ones in the same five months of 2025, at 65.04 lakh against 63.08 lakh.

The 2025 per-carat pair, US$773.87 and US$73.42, is Carat Capital's division of the council's own columns. The falls 671.39 ÷ 773.87 = 86.76% of the prior year, so 13.24% below it; 66.51 ÷ 73.42 = 90.59%, so 9.41% below. IValue fell, carats rose

Down 7.38%. Natural polished exports ran at US$4,521.12m from April to August, against US$4,881.59m a year earlier. The council calls every figure provisional.

Up 6.75%. Volume went the other way, to 67.34 lakh carats from 63.08 lakh. More goods left India for less money.

IIThe grown carat is a tenth

Sixty-six fifty-one. The council prints grown polished at US$66.51 a carat for the five months. Natural prints at US$671.39, about ten times as much.

Carats, not value. Grown polished made 80.24 lakh carats against natural's 67.34 lakh. On value it is US$533.67m against US$4,521.12m.

IIIStudded gold carried the total

Up 2.58% overall. Gross gem and jewellery exports reached US$11,467.49m for the five months, 2.58% above last year. Polished diamonds fell inside that total.

Studded up 26.73%. Studded gold jewellery rose to US$2,878.59m while plain gold jewellery fell to US$2,153.58m.

The depthMethod, sources, corrections · open what you need
01What would change this call+

The DGCI&S reconciliation, or a revision to either column. Both per-carat figures are quotients of a value and a volume the council prints as provisional, so a revision to either input moves the output, and a large enough revision to volume alone would change the direction of the per-carat fall without any price changing.

02Why an average carat falls when no stone is repriced+

A per-carat average is not a price. It is a total divided by a count, and it moves whenever the mix of what was counted changes, even if every individual stone sold for exactly what it sold for last year. Ship the same goods at lower prices and the average falls. Ship more small, cheap goods alongside the same large ones and the average also falls, with no stone repriced at all. The two cases look identical in this table and the report does not separate them. That matters here because the volume line rose while the value line fell, which is the signature of a mix shift as much as of a price cut. What can be said from these figures alone is that India was paid less per carat shipped. What cannot be said from them is that any given stone fetched less, or by how much. Separating the two needs a size-band breakdown, which this report does not carry.
