Central banks buy 321.2t of gold in Q3; jewellery down 17% on year

Total demand, including OTC, slipped 1% on the year to 1,368.6 tonnes, the World Gold Council says. ETFs added 211.2 tonnes, bar and coin fell 19% on the year, and jewellery had its weakest third quarter in the series.

By Carat Capital

3 November 2026 Corrected

What changed

Central banks carried Q3. Central banks bought 321.2 tonnes of gold in the third quarter, up 42% on the year. Total demand slipped 1%, the World Gold Council says.

What it means

  • If you restock for the holidays: Jewellery took 312.9 tonnes, down 17% on the year; its value rose 2% to $43bn.
  • If you price off gold: The tape's $3,926.50, at 13:51 EST on 3 November, sits 7.9% under the Q3 LBMA average of $4,262.2.
  • What this is not: A record year in tonnes: nine-month demand is 3 tonnes short of 2025's.

Key figures

Central bank buyingQ3, +42% on the year321.2t
Jewellery consumptionQ3, weakest third quarter in the series312.9t
ETF inflowsQ3, after a 47.3t outflow in Q2211.2t
Total demand, incl. OTCQ3, −1% on the year1,368.6t

Source: World Gold Council, Gold Demand Trends Q3 2026, released 3 November 2026; data to 30 September. Spot gold: Carat Capital tape, Kitco bid, 13:51 EST 3 November.

Investment split in two.

ETFs turned back. Gold ETFs and similar products added 211.2 tonnes after an outflow of 47.3 tonnes in Q2. The council says the inflow seemed driven by concerns over fiscal sustainability and inflation, among other things.

Bars and coins fell. Bar and coin demand fell 19% on the year to 267.8 tonnes. The council says investors waited for a clearer price trend.

Q2 reads lower than in July.

The total was revised. The council's July table put Q2 total demand at 1,268.9 tonnes. Its 3 November table gives 1,254.5 tonnes for the same quarter.

Central banks revised up. Q2 central bank buying now reads 296.3 tonnes, against 288.9 in July. Q3's figure is the highest of the five quarters in the new table.

Mines set a third-quarter record.

Output rose 3%. Mine production rose 3% to 1,070.9 tonnes, a third-quarter record, the council says. Recycling fell 8% to 314.5 tonnes, a three-year low.

What to watch

  • Around 7 November 2026The People's Bank of China's October reserve figures. The test: whether official buying carries into the fourth quarter.
  • Q4 2026 report, early 2027The council's full-year count. The test: whether central bank buying at least matches 2025, as the council expects.

Clarified, 4 November 2026: this page said the council "ties" the ETF inflow to fiscal and inflation concerns; the council says the inflow "seemed to be driven" by them, "among other things". The table omitted the council's jewellery inventory row (27.2 tonnes; 43.6 in Q3 2025), so its rows did not sum to the total; the row is now shown. A sub-head now says "third-quarter record", "a record year" is qualified "in tonnes", the PBoC date reads "around 7 November", and the jewellery line now carries the 17% fall the headline gives. No printed figure changed. Raised by Carat Capital's Standards desk on 3 November 2026; the 17% line by its Attention desk on 4 November.

What would change this call

A fourth quarter in which official buying falls back toward Q1 2026's 57.7 tonnes would test the council's view that the year's central bank buying at least matches 2025.

How the council counts demand

Total demand adds jewellery, technology, investment and central bank buying, plus an 'OTC and other' line. In this table total demand equals total supply in every quarter.
