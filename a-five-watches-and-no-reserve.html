Watches

Five seized watches worth GBP 1 million go to no-reserve sale

Wilsons Auctions will sell timepieces forfeited from an HMRC payroll-fraud investigation online from 29 to 30 October. A Philippe Dufour Simplicity leads; every lot sells whatever the hammer price.

By The Watches Desk

9 October 2026

What changed

Forfeited watches reach a sale. Wilsons Auctions will sell watches forfeited from an HMRC payroll-fraud investigation without reserve over 24 hours from 29 October. Five seized timepieces are worth around GBP 1 million as a group.

What it means

  • If you collect independents: A Philippe Dufour Simplicity with no reserve is the rarest combination here. The one watch that never needs a floor is being sold without one.
  • If you broker watches: An unreserved government sale is public price discovery on references that normally trade privately. It will be quoted at you afterwards.
  • If you insure or appraise: The forfeiture chain is complete and the seller is acting for government bodies, so title is not the open question here.
  • What this is not: A valuation, and not a GBP 7 million sale. That figure is what HMRC found was spent on watches over three years, not the value of the lots on offer.

A payroll-fraud investigation

Seven million spent. HMRC investigated suspected payroll fraud in the United Kingdom. It found more than GBP 7 million had been spent on luxury watches over three years.

Five seized watches. Its findings included five timepieces worth around GBP 1 million, seized from an individual attempting to leave the country, later forfeited.

No reserve on anything

Every lot sells. Wilsons is selling as proceeds of crime for United Kingdom government bodies and law enforcement. It says it will sell every watch whatever the hammer price.

Twenty-four hours only. The Unreserved Government Auction runs online from 29 October at 11 a.m. to 30 October at 11 a.m. GMT.

A Simplicity leads it

Dufour at the front. The Philippe Dufour Simplicity leading the sale was launched in 2000 and has an 18-karat rose gold case, a white dial and leather straps.

Four more named. A Patek Philippe Perpetual Calendar Split Seconds Chronograph, two Audemars Piguet Royal Oaks and an Omega Speedmaster Racing are also listed.

Two figures never given

No estimates published. Wilsons has published no estimate for any individual lot. The GBP 1 million is the only figure bounding the five seized watches, and it covers them as a group.

No lot count. The house has not said how many lots the sale carries in total, so the named watches cannot be sized against the book.

What to watch

  • 29 to 30 October 2026The 24-hour online window, and what an unreserved Dufour Simplicity clears at in public.
  • Before 29 October 2026Whether Wilsons publishes a catalogue with per-lot estimates and a total lot count.
  • After the saleWhether the results are quoted as comparables for Dufour and Royal Oak references that normally trade privately.

What would change this call

A withdrawal of lots before the window, or a catalogue that turns out to carry reserves on some material. The no-reserve claim and the GBP 1 million seizure figure both rest on the auction house's own statement, with no HMRC publication cited and no per-lot estimate to check them against.

What no reserve actually removes

A reserve is a confidential minimum below which a lot will not sell, usually set at or below the low estimate. Removing it does two things and not a third. It guarantees a sale, which is why government and insolvency sellers use the format: the mandate is to convert assets, not to defend a price. And it changes bidder behaviour at the bottom of the book, because the opening bid no longer has to clear a hidden floor. What it does not do is cap the price. An unreserved sale of scarce references can clear well above a reserved one, because the removal of the floor pulls in bidders who then compete. The consequence for anyone reading the results afterwards is that a no-reserve result is a real transaction price but a poor comparable: it was produced under a selling constraint that a private owner does not face, and it carries buyer's premium on top of whatever is reported.
