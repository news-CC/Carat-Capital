Sotheby's says Romanov sapphire bracelet sold for over CHF 4.1m

AP reports the 197.80-carat sapphire, estimated at CHF 850,000 to 1.7 million, went to a private collector in Geneva on 11 November. AP gives the price without saying whether it includes the buyer's premium.

By Carat Capital

13 November 2026

What changed

Reported above expected range. A 197.80-carat Romanov sapphire on a diamond bracelet sold for more than CHF 4.1 million at Sotheby's Geneva on 11 November, AP reports.

What it means

  • If you bid on historic jewels: the price, more than CHF 4.1 million, passed the CHF 1.7 million high estimate, AP reports; Sotheby's has not published its figure.
  • If you value sapphires: one named stone at one sale, not a per-carat market price.
  • What this is not: a house result. The sale page showed none on 13 November.

AP reports the price.

Reported: over CHF 4.1m. AP reports the bracelet sold for more than 4.1 million Swiss francs, about $4.9 million, citing Sotheby's. The buyer, a private collector, was not identified.

No house figure yet. Sotheby's sale page for Royal & Noble Jewels showed no realised price when read on 13 November. Its press page carried no release on the sale.

Imperial owners, per Sotheby's.

Worn in 1903. Dowager Empress Maria Feodorovna, mother of Nicholas II, wore the sapphire at the Winter Palace Ball of 1903, Sotheby's says, as AP reports.

Cartier mount, per Sotheby's. Sotheby's sale page describes the sapphire as mounted by Cartier. AP says the stone likely came from the Mughal Empire.

Soviet sales, per Sotheby's. After the revolution it was recorded in inventory before entering the Soviet government's sales of imperial treasures, Sotheby's says.

What to watch

  • From 13 November 2026Sotheby's own result for the lot. The test: whether the house figure states the buyer's premium basis.

What would change this call

If Sotheby's own figure shows CHF 4.1 million was the hammer price, the buyer paid more once the premium is added.

Hammer and premium

The hammer price is the winning bid. Houses add a buyer's premium on top. AP does not say which basis the CHF 4.1 million is on, and the estimate's basis is not stated either.
