GJC claims record GJS sales and publishes no figure

The 10th India Gem & Jewellery Show closed on 4 October with 400 exhibitors and more than 700 booths at Mumbai's Jio World Convention Centre. Its pre-show targets were higher than anything it reported after.

By The Retail & Technology Desk

7 October 2026

What changed

A record was claimed, not counted. GJC's closing release on the 10th India Gem & Jewellery Show credits record sales figures and publishes none. Its pre-show release had promised 10,000 visitors and 2,000 hosted buyers.

What it means

  • If you exhibited: There is no published benchmark to measure your own three days against, and no visitor count either.
  • If you plan a 2027 stand: The only hard numbers are 400 exhibitors, 700 booths and 15,000 square metres, all pre-show.
  • If you model Indian festive demand: This show gives you nothing usable. Treat the 2-4 October window as unmeasured rather than strong.
  • What this is not: Evidence the show went badly. An unpublished number is an absence of evidence in either direction.

The claim has no number

A chairman's phrase. GJC chairman Rajesh Rokde credits the edition's record sales figures with showing the industry's resilience. No figure appears anywhere in the release carrying that sentence.

The visitors vanished. The pre-show release promised more than 10,000 visitors and 2,000 hosted buyers. The closing release reports neither count, only thousands of buyers.

What is actually measured

Four hundred stands. Both releases give over 400 exhibitors and more than 700 booths. The pre-show release adds 15,000 square metres of exhibition space.

Three days, four events. The show ran 2 to 4 October, opened by Maharashtra industries minister Uday Samant, after a 1 October jewellers' business workshop.

The awards were the draw

Gold Council presented. The National Jewellery Awards ran on 3 October, presented by the World Gold Council and powered by India Gold Metaverse. The actor Sonakshi Sinha handed out prizes.

Seminars alongside. MindSpeak sessions across the three days covered sustainability, digital transformation, family-business strategy and the use of artificial intelligence in design and retail.

What to watch

  • Through October 2026Whether GJC publishes an order-value or footfall figure for the edition. Ten editions in, it has a series it could put a number on.
  • 17 October 2026India's August gem and jewellery export statistics, the nearest dated series against which festive-quarter demand can actually be measured.
  • Next GJS editionWhether the 2027 pre-show release repeats the 10,000-visitor target after not reporting against this one.

What would change this call

A published figure. An order value, a footfall count or a year-on-year comparison would turn the record claim into a measurable one, and GJC has the data from its own registration system to do it.

Why a show number is worth having

A B2B jewellery fair's order value is the trade's cleanest read on festive-quarter restocking, because it records what retailers committed to buy rather than what consumers said they would spend. Organisers who publish it, and several Indian shows do, create a comparable series across editions. One that claims a record without a figure gives the trade a sentiment reading dressed as a measurement, and nothing to hold the next edition against.

Method

Three documents were read in this run. The pre-show release was fetched to raw bytes at jewelbuzz.in, HTTP 200, 435,281 bytes, datePublished 2026-09-30T18:41:35+05:30, carrying GJC's own announcement text; it is the only one of the three stating 10,000+ visitors, 2,000 hosted buyers and 15,000 sq. meters, and those figures were read inside its own body. The inaugural release was fetched at aninews.in, HTTP 200, 115,211 bytes, updated Oct 03, 2026 17:15 IST, dateline Mumbai (Maharashtra) [India], October 3, with the body stating the show was inaugurated on 2nd October and runs until October 4. The closing release was fetched at heerazhaveraat.com, HTTP 200, 3,936,124 bytes, datePublished 2026-10-05T11:22:03+00:00. On that third page the article's own paragraphs were isolated before any figure search, and this is the step that matters here: a whole-page search of that 3.9-megabyte document returns twenty-two hits for crore and ten for a rupee mark, and every one of them sits in the site's breaking-news rail or its You May Also Like block, belonging to other stories. Inside this story's own paragraphs the counts are 400 one hit, 700 one, record sales one, MindSpeak one, crore zero, rupee mark zero, 10,000 zero, 2,000 zero. That isolation is what establishes the central claim, and a whole-page grep would have produced the opposite and wrong answer. GJC's own site was also reached, gjc.org.in, HTTP 200, 35,966 bytes, and carries only a navigation shell with no release text; two likely release paths, /press-release and /domestic-news, both returned 404 at 272 bytes. So the council's release reaches this paper only through syndication, and that limit is disclosed rather than presented as a direct read of the council's own publication. One further discrepancy between the three, disclosed rather than resolved: the inaugural release describes Srinivas Injeti as Chairman, IAGES, while the closing release describes him as Chairman NSE and Chairman of the board IAGES. No title is printed for him above. ARCHIVE: searched website/content/articles.json for India Gem & Jewellery Show, zero hits; AIGJDC, zero; National Jewellery Award, zero; Diwali Edition, zero.
