Rings.com puts the lab-grown 1-carat median back at $571

The September edition read 251,098 listings at seven US retailers on 17 September. Ritani raised its one-carat asking median 26% while Brilliant Earth cut its 1.5-carat cell 32%.

By The Retail Desk21 September 2026Brief 25 sec · Full read 2 min
What changed

The median is published again. Rings.com's September index puts the one-carat lab-grown market median at $571 a carat. It is the first one-carat median the index has published since June.

What it means · The Desk’s View

If you price lab-grown at retail: Four US counters now quote between $281 and $796 for the same one-carat spec.

If you restock lab-grown goods: Ritani reversed August's cuts at all three weights. Brilliant Earth moved the other way.

What this is not: A wholesale price. These are listed asking prices, not transactions.

Carat Capital's arithmetic the distance between the cheapest and the dearest published one-carat counter is a division, not a published figure: 796 ÷ 281 = 2.83 times. On August's published pair it was 856 ÷ 223 = 3.84 times, so the spread narrowed. Not published the index states Ritani's August one-carat median at $223, Grown Brilliance's as unchanged at $500 and Brilliant Earth's at $856, but does not state With Clarity's August one-carat figure, so no change is shown in that row. Two counters are absent Clean Origin and Blue Nile did not clear the index's 30-listing minimum at one carat this edition. VRAI publishes $1,583 but is graded by Diamond Foundry rather than to the IGI specification, and is excluded from the median.

ITwo counters moved, two did not

Ritani reversed course. Its one-carat median rose from $223 to $281 a carat, up 26%, the opposite direction from August's cut across the same cells.

Brilliant Earth cut. Its 1.5-carat median fell 32%, from $1,151 to $784 a carat, the largest single-cell move on the panel this edition.

Grown Brilliance held. All three of its cells moved by 3% or less while its listed one-carat inventory fell from 795 stones to 435.

IIWhy two weights stay withheld

The rule is composition. The index withholds a blended median when the contributing retailers change, so the figure measures prices rather than who is in the panel.

One carat passes. The same four retailers published a one-carat figure in August and September, so the eligibility test clears and the median is republished.

The other two fail. Blue Nile's newly published 1.5-carat and 2-carat cells, and Clean Origin's 1.5-carat cell, were not in August's contributor set.

IIIListings, not transactions

The basis matters. Every figure is a publicly visible list price at the moment of collection, including automatic site-wide reductions but excluding promotional codes.

The sample is large. 251,098 listings across seven retailers were read on 17 September, and the next edition is due the week of 26 October.

01What would change this call+

A month in which the four contributing retailers change again. The median is withheld whenever the contributor set moves, which is why it went unpublished in July and August; one seller thinning at one carat takes the figure off the page regardless of where prices go.

02How this median is built+

The index reads publicly visible list prices for a fixed specification, a one-carat E VS1 round excellent stone graded by IGI, at a fixed panel of seven United States online sellers. A retailer's cell publishes only if at least 30 listings clear the specification; below that the cell is shown as thin and left blank. The blended market median is then taken across the sellers that published, but only when the same sellers published in the prior edition as well. That last test is why a figure can vanish for two months while every underlying price is still being collected: the rule is protecting against a median that measures panel composition instead of price.

The September 2026 edition was fetched directly on 21 September 2026 and read for each figure printed here rather than for a summary. The main table's columns are total stone price; at one carat the total and the per-carat figure are the same number, which is why the one-carat column is used and the heavier columns are not.
