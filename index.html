The Wire
MINUS TWENTY-THREE THOUSAND — US non-farm payrolls fell 23,000 in July against a consensus of roughly 83,000 jobs added, a miss of 106,000, with June revised to a 20,000 loss. The unemployment rate edged down to 4.1% while the participation rate fell to 61.4%, its lowest in more than five years; average hourly earnings rose two cents and annual wage growth slowed to 3.2%. The Bureau of Labor Statistics release page returned 403 to this desk and the figures are carried as reported by two outletsGOLD CLOSES AT $4,341.30 — Kitco's 5:00pm New York close on Friday had gold bid $4,341.30, up $102.00 or 2.41% against Thursday; silver $63.46, up $2.06 or 3.36%; platinum $1,743.00, up $25.00 or 1.46%; palladium $1,357.00, up $4.00 or 0.30%. Fine gold is $139.58 a gram. Outside sources diverge: Trading Economics carried $4,343.43 for the same day, $2.13 higher, and Reuters reporting had spot near $4,356 at its intraday best. This paper carries the Kitco close as a stated closing print at a stated timeGOLD KEPT IT, THE OTHERS GAVE IT BACK — Against this paper's own 5:58am marks on Friday, gold added a further $26.80 into the close while platinum gave back $29.00, palladium $25.00 and silver $0.83. Platinum was up 3.14% on the session in the morning and closed up 1.46%; palladium was up 2.14% and closed up 0.30%. The gold to silver ratio tightened to 67.11 by morning and widened back to 68.41 at the close, from 69.04 on ThursdaySEPTEMBER PRICED OUT TO 46% — The CME FedWatch tool had the probability of a September rate hike at 46% immediately after the payrolls release, from 55% before it; this paper carried 56.9% on Friday morning. Ellen Zentner of Morgan Stanley Wealth Management noted that next week's inflation data will still likely be the deciding factor. Consumer prices are released next weekBONHAMS RUNS CHANEL IN THREE CITIES — Bonhams has set three Chanel sales for September: New York 7 to 17, Paris 14 to 24 with roughly 400 lots, and London 21 September to 1 October. London carries the jewellery weight, with a private collection of 1960s to 1990s Chanel costume pieces led by a Robert Goossens filigree snake bangle circa 1960 at $13,000 to $20,000, against Chanel Fine Jewelry 18-carat white gold and diamond Comète Géode earrings from 2024 at £2,000 to £3,000. New York highlights go on view at the Manhattan flagship from 17 AugustTWENTY-NINE PER CENT SAID DIAMOND — HKTDC surveyed 1,507 buyers and exhibitors at its twin Hong Kong shows, which ran 29 July to 2 August at the convention centre. Diamond led the most-in-demand question at 29%, ahead of ruby at 25% and pearl at 20%, while trendy fashion jewellery led growth potential at 57% against precious jewellery at 35% and designer at 21%. Yellow gold was the preferred metal for 40%. Respondents expect one to two years before confidence is fully restoredEIGHT POINT SIX UP, NINE DOWN — Tenoris figures reported by JCK put US jewellery sales up 8.6% across the first half on a 19% rise in average purchase price, with unit sales falling; the implied unit change, derived by this desk rather than published, is about −9%. June sales rose 13% year on year and sales below $1,500 continued to decline. It is the same shape this paper filed from Brilliant Earth, Richemont and Luk Fook in the past fortnightNY NOW REBOOKS 96% — NY Now's summer market ran 2 to 4 August at the Javits Center with 460 exhibitors, more than 70 of them new or returning, in the first edition under Rockview Management Group after Emerald sold the show. Buyer attendance rose 5% year on year, 107 prospective exhibitors walked the floor, and organisers report a 96% renewal rate for the winter 2027 market, which opens 31 JanuaryWHAT DID NOT PRINT TODAY — Etsy is cutting 220 jobs, 12% of its workforce, in product and engineering, with second-quarter sales up 6%; carried as context because no jewellery-seller-specific impact has been reported. Four slate items were killed on verification: the NDC and GJEPC Mumbai strategy meeting (already published here on 5 August), Rio Tinto's Diavik half-year production (published three times, most recently 29 July), Antwerp's half-year trade figures and the Israel Diamond Exchange leadership change (both previously published). Angola's rough production ranking was pulled because the desk's figures did not match its own cited source. Sotheby's Arcade Hong Kong closed on 6 August and has published no result for a third consecutive day. On the diamond lines NAT1 and LGD1 are carried unchanged; the lab-grown reference reads $711 on CaratRadar against $510 on StoneAlgo, a spread of roughly 39% this desk carries rather than reconciles. RAPI returned 403 for a fourth day
