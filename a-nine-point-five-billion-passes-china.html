UK's $9.5bn passes China as gold ETF inflow leader

Global holdings rose 67 tonnes to a record 4,256 tonnes in September while assets under management fell 7% on the month to US$574bn. The quarter took in US$31bn.

By The Metals Desk

12 October 2026

What changed

Britain took the lead. UK-listed funds have drawn US$9.5bn this year, marginally overtaking Chinese funds as the largest country-level source of gold ETF inflows.

What it means

  • If you allocate institutionally: Holdings rose while assets fell. The gap is price, not redemptions.
  • If you sell gold at retail: Investment demand held through a falling price. Buyers treated the pullback as an entry, not an exit.
  • What this is not: A reading on jewellery demand, a forecast, or a figure newer than 30 September. The council published it on 7 October and nothing has moved since.

Key figures

Total assets under managementdown 7% month on monthUS$574bn

Source: World Gold Council, Gold ETF Flows: September 2026, published 7 October 2026 and unchanged since; read five days later. Flows data as of 30 September 2026. All figures are the council's own and in US dollars.

Holdings rose as assets fell

Sixty-seven tonnes in. Global holdings gained 67 tonnes in September to a record 4,256 tonnes. Total assets under management fell 7% on the month to US$574bn.

Price did that. The council attributes the decline in assets to a lower gold price, not to selling. The two series moved in opposite directions for the month.

The quarter grew. Across the third quarter total assets rose 9% and collective holdings grew by 211 tonnes. Inflows reached a record US$31bn.

Europe outbought North America

Fourteen billion in Europe. European funds drew US$3.6bn in September and US$14bn across the quarter, a record. North America took US$4bn and US$12bn.

First since 2021. The council says this is the first quarter in which Europe beat North America while both recorded inflows since the second quarter of 2021.

Britain led it. UK-listed funds added US$2.2bn in the month and US$7.5bn in the quarter. German, French and Swiss-listed funds also recorded inflows.

Twelve weeks out of thirteen

A consistent run. UK funds recorded inflows in 12 of the 13 weeks to 25 September, which the council calls the most consistent run since 2022.

Gilts beside gold. The council links the run to a rising gilt term premium and to investor concern over inflation uncertainty and fiscal sustainability.

Asia bought too. Asian funds added US$2.3bn, a third straight month of inflows, with China the largest contributor. India, Japan, South Korea and Singapore also took money.

What to watch

  • Early November 2026The council's October flows report, which shows whether the UK's lead over China survived a single month.
  • OngoingWhether holdings keep rising while assets fall. The two parted company in September on price alone.
  • Next Fed decisionThe council names a 25 basis point rise during September, and a signal of further tightening, as pressure on the gold price.

What would change this call

A month of outflows, or a revision. The series is the council's own and is restated in later reports when fund filings change, so the September point is not final. The UK's lead over China is described by the council itself as marginal, which means a single month of Chinese buying could reverse it. Nothing here has moved since 7 October, and a reading five days old is not a reading of this morning.

Why holdings and flows are two different series

The council publishes two things on the same page and they answer different questions. Demand captures the change in the physical gold held by the funds, measured in tonnes. Fund flows capture the net amount of money in US dollars that came into or went out of those funds. Assets under management are a third thing again: tonnes multiplied by the gold price, which means they move when either the metal or the price moves. That is why September can show holdings at a record and assets down 7% in the same sentence without contradicting itself. The funds bought 67 tonnes; the price of what they already held fell further than the new metal added. Reading the AUM line as a demand signal is the common error, and it is the one that would have had investors selling gold ETFs in a month when they bought a record quarter's worth. The tonnage line is the demand line. The dollar line is the price line wearing a demand label.

Method

One primary, fetched at press time independently of the filing desk, 200, 81,366 bytes. Exact-digit greps on the saved raw bytes, with hit counts, for every figure in the headline, dek and key-figure cells: '4,256' 3; '574' 3; '67t' 4; '31bn' 3; '10bn' 3; '7%' 3. The page's own sentence, quoted so it can be compared with what is printed above: 'Despite a lower gold price, global holdings rose 67t to a record 4,256t, while total AUM declined 7% m/m to US$574bn'. The US$9.5bn year-to-date figure and the US$7.5bn quarter figure for UK-listed funds, the 12-of-13-weeks run, the 211 tonnes and 9% quarterly figures and every regional leg in the table were each read out of the report's own body text in the same saved file. The page carries no JSON-LD datePublished; the publication date is taken from the report's own printed line, 'Published: 7 October, 2026', and the age is disclosed in the key-figure as-of line and in the article. No second source is cited and none is claimed: this is the council's own series and no independent party publishes it. ARCHIVE, by entity and by period, against Carat Capital's archive, every hit opened and read rather than counted: 'gold ETF' returned 6 articles and all six were read. Ours of 2026-08-14 covers JULY 2026 global flows (US$3bn in the month, holdings up 23 tonnes); the new item covers SEPTEMBER 2026 AND Q3 2026 (US$10bn in the month, US$31bn in the quarter, holdings up 67 tonnes to 4,256 tonnes) — SAME ENTITY, DIFFERENT PERIOD, therefore ADVANCED, not a repeat. The other four hits are India-only or China-only sub-series from 22 July, 22 September and 23 September and a Perth Mint piece of 17 August, each a different entity and a different period. A separate grep for '4,256' returned one hit, which was opened: it is a gold ASK price of $4,256.50 in a 24 September markets piece, a coincidence of digits and not this report's tonnage.
