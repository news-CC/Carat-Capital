24-carat pink diamond carries a CHF 15 million high estimate

Christie's puts the emerald-cut, fancy-intense-pink, type IIa stone in its Geneva Magnificent Jewels sale on 10 November. Bulgari set it in a ring in the 1950s for a noble family that has held it since.

By The Auctions Desk

9 October 2026

What changed

Christie's priced a pink. Christie's has estimated a 24-carat emerald-cut fancy intense pink diamond at CHF 10 million to CHF 15 million. Bulgari set it as a ring in the 1950s.

What it means

  • If you bid colour: The band is 50% wide on a single stone. That is the house saying it does not know where this clears.
  • If you hold pink inventory: A type IIa fancy intense pink of this size trading in public sets a visible comparable for everything below it.
  • If you consign: A Bulgari setting and one noble family since the 1950s is the part of this lot nobody can replicate.
  • What this is not: A result, or an independently documented provenance. The India story is family lore relayed by the auction house, the estimate is a pre-sale opinion, and no reserve has been published.

Ten to fifteen million

Fifty percent wide. Christie's estimate runs CHF 10 million to CHF 15 million. The high is one and a half times the low, a band the house has left deliberately open.

Twelve to eighteen. Christie's gives the same band as $12 million to $18 million. The stone is emerald-cut, graded fancy intense pink, and type IIa.

A ring from the 1950s

Bulgari set it. Bulgari mounted the diamond as a ring in the 1950s. The house has stayed in one noble family since acquisition, according to Christie's.

Family lore, not record. Christie's attributes the India origin to family lore from that household. It is the owner's account relayed by the auction house, not a documented chain.

Two pinks, same day

A second stone. Carat Capital reported on 15 September that Christie's estimated a 14.63-carat David Morris pink ring at up to CHF 2.1 million.

Different catalogues. That ring leads a 123-lot single-owner sale. The Bulgari stone sits in Magnificent Jewels. Two separate consignments, two separate books.

What would change this call

A withdrawal before the sale, or a result at or below the low estimate, which would make this a story about one house's pricing ambition rather than about pink demand. A pre-sale estimate is an opinion that has not been tested by a bidder.

What a 50% estimate band tells you

An auction estimate is the house's published opinion of where a lot should clear, and its width is information. A narrow band is a claim to know the market; a band whose high is one and a half times its low is a claim that the market for this object is thin enough that two serious bidders could land far apart. For large fancy-colour diamonds the comparable set is a handful of stones over a span of years, so the estimate is set from few data points and the reserve, which is confidential and usually at or below the low estimate, does the real work. Buyer's premium sits on top of the hammer price, so the price a buyer pays is above any figure quoted here. Per-carat arithmetic on a rounded catalogue weight is indicative only: at 23.5 carats the high estimate would be CHF 638,298 a carat and at 24.5 it would be CHF 612,245.
