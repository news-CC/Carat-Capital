RAPI's 1-carat holds at $3,912.13 for a fifth morning

The index has posted no new point since 16 September, though it moved to daily publication on 7 September. IDEX's broad one-carat driver averaged $3,051.17 on the same morning, 22.0% below it.

By The Diamond Desk21 September 2026Brief 25 sec · Full read 1 min
What changed

The one-carat stopped moving. RapNet's one-carat index has carried $3,912.13 since 16 September, its newest published point, with no new print in the five mornings since.

What it means · The Desk’s View

If you buy one-carat polished: The benchmark has not moved in five days. The broad counter sits 22.0% under it.

If you quote off RAPI: Your reference is five days old. Check the date on the point, not the day you read it.

What this is not: A frozen market. The index has not printed, not the same thing.

IThe index went quiet

Five mornings, one level. RapNet's one-carat index has carried $3,912.13 on every morning since 17 September, its source not having published a newer point.

Daily was the promise. The index moved from monthly to daily publication on 7 September. It has printed no new point in the five days since 16 September.

IIThe broad counter sits lower

Two numbers, one carat. IDEX's price-driver average for a one-carat round read $3,051.17, against RAPI's $3,912.13, a gap of 22.0%.

The gap inverts. At half a carat the broad counter sits above the narrow one, $1,379 against $1,315, a gap of 4.8% the other way.

IIILab-grown moved the same week

The other counter repriced. A lab-grown retail index published on 18 September reset its one-carat median to $571, while the natural benchmark stood still.

01What would change this call+

A new RAPI point. The level here is not a market judgement, it is the absence of a fresh print; one new daily point ends the story whichever way it goes.

02Why two indices disagree by a fifth+

RAPI is the average asking price of the ten per cent lowest-priced stones on RapNet within a narrow band, D to H colour and IF to VS2 clarity, GIA graded. It is a floor for a top specification rather than a market mean. IDEX price drivers average a far broader spread of colour and clarity, D to K and IF to I1 at one carat, and weight each driver by its share of the market. A broad average of an inclusive band will sit below a floor for a narrow one, which is what the one-carat row shows. At half a carat the relationship inverts because the two bands overlap differently at that weight. Neither is wrong; a single diamond price does not exist.
