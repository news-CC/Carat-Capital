Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
Lab-grown (LGD)Term

Diamonds grown in weeks by HPHT or CVD — chemically identical to mined, economically a different universe. Their price curve is a technology curve, which is the whole story.

ClientelingPractice

One-to-one retail relationship management — the private-client WhatsApp thread, remembered anniversaries, first calls on new goods. The oldest luxury skill, now run on software.

OmnichannelConcept

The customer researches at midnight, tries on Saturday, buys by DM on Tuesday. Winning retailers price, stock and staff as one continuous counter across all of it.

Digital product passportRegulation

An EU-led ID standard giving each piece a scannable record of origin, materials and custody. Coming for jewelry the way nutrition labels came for food.

Retail & Tech This week — August 10

Titan says natural-diamond pricing has stabilised, on a 43% jewellery quarter

In remarks published on 12 August 2026, Arun Narayan, chief executive of Titan's jewellery business, said the company has seen more stability in the pricing of both solitaires and small diamonds, and Ajoy Chawla, Titan's managing director, said that at the market level natural-diamond pricing has stabilized and that the lab-grown-versus-natural narrative has faded from the conversation. Titan reported a resurgence in diamond-jewellery buying in the fourth quarter of its fiscal 2026 and into the first quarter of fiscal 2027, with demand strengthening through the April-to-June period. Titan's consolidated income for the three months to 30 June 2026 was Rs 20,753 crore, up 40% year on year, with profit before tax of Rs 2,429 crore, up 64%, at a margin of 11.7%. The jewellery division recorded Rs 18,253 crore, up 43% excluding bullion and digital gold, at an EBIT of Rs 2,360 crore and a margin of 12.9%. Watches and wearables recorded Rs 1,543 crore, up 21%, at an EBIT margin of 19.1%. The group added a net 77 stores in the quarter to reach 3,680 worldwide as at June 2026. This paper published the same quarter on 28 July 2026 from the company's own business update at approximately 41% revenue growth and 39% for jewellery; the 43% figure excludes bullion and digital gold and is a different basis rather than a revision. A third basis circulating in secondary coverage, revenue from operations of Rs 21,356 crore and growth of 29.25% against Rs 16,523 crore, is internally consistent on its own terms and does not agree with the 40% consolidated-income headline; the divergence is disclosed rather than resolved. Titan is a retailer of natural diamonds and the statement is made by an interested party on its own earnings call. This paper reported Indian gold jewellery demand down 15% by weight in the second quarter of 2026 even as its value rose.

Source — Rapaport (12 August 2026) / afaqs (8 August 2026) / Sahi (7 August 2026) / Carat Capital archive
Retail & Tech This week — August 10

Signet gives Zales and Banter to Mattel's former global head of dolls

Signet Jewelers announced on 11 August 2026, from Hamilton, Bermuda, that Jamie Cygielman has been named president of Zales and Banter, effective 24 August 2026. She will be based in Dallas and reports directly to J.K. Symancyk, Signet's chief executive officer. She joins from Mattel, where she was global head of dolls, a portfolio she reached after serving as general manager and president of American Girl and which she later widened to include Barbie. The same release named Pam Cloud president of Blue Nile with effect from 10 August 2026, based in New York City; Cloud spent more than 25 years at Tiffany and Co., latterly as senior vice president and chief merchandising officer, an appointment this paper reported on 6 August 2026. Two of Signet's four brand families therefore changed president within eight days, and the two appointments are different in kind: one seat was filled from inside the jewellery trade and the other from a toy company. Under Grow Brand Love, the strategy launched by Symancyk in March 2025, Signet is organised into four customer families: Kay and Peoples for core milestone and romantic gifting, Zales and Banter for style and trend, Jared for inspired luxury, and Blue Nile for luxury. The stated aim of the strategy is to have the company's chains operate as brands rather than as retail banners and to grow share in adjacent categories, notably everyday jewellery. Gold spot stood at $4,415.20 an ounce on this paper's tape the same morning, up 0.97% on the paper's previous mark of record and up 6.2% on the $4,158.30 the tape carried on 5 August 2026.

Source — Signet Jewelers (11 August 2026) / Forbes / Rapaport / Carat Capital archive and tape
Retail & Tech This week — August 10

Kalyan Jewellers launches a Tamil Nadu-only banner, to be grown by franchise

Kalyan Jewellers announced on 3 August 2026 the launch of Akshaya Thanga Maligai, a second retail brand intended to trade only in the Indian state of Tamil Nadu. The first showroom opens at Panagal Park in T. Nagar, Chennai, on 21 August 2026, selling hallmarked gold and certified diamond jewellery designed to regional preferences. The company has said it will expand the brand within Tamil Nadu through a franchise model before considering any other state, and has appointed the actor Sivakarthikeyan to support its visibility. Rajesh Kalyanaraman, executive director, framed the reasoning as regional markets offering significant long-term growth opportunities when approached with a truly local proposition. Kalyan Jewellers itself operates more than 400 showrooms across India, the United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, the United States and the United Kingdom. No investment figure was disclosed. The choice of franchising rather than owned showrooms places the working capital for the gold inventory on franchisee balance sheets at a point when gold spot stood at $4,372.80 an ounce on this paper's tape the same morning. The demand backdrop previously filed by this paper is Indian gold jewellery demand down 15% by weight in the second quarter of 2026 to 75.1 tonnes, its lowest second quarter since the pandemic, with the value of that demand up about 50%, and Kalyan taking more than 46% of its June-quarter revenue in recycled metal and more than 55% in the month of June alone.

Source — The Retail Jeweller (3 August 2026) / Carat Capital archive
Retail & Tech This week — August 10

Etsy cuts 220 roles from product and engineering in a quarter it beat

Etsy disclosed on 6 August 2026 a restructuring removing approximately 220 roles, about 12% of its marketplace workforce, falling mainly on the product and engineering organisation, at an estimated charge of about $35 million, primarily severance and benefits, to be booked in the third quarter of 2026 and completed within it. Approximately 1,600 employees remain. The company stated that the reduction is not a cost-cutting measure and is not related to artificial intelligence, describing the intent as flatter teams with wider remits, fewer handoffs and quicker decisions. The plan was announced alongside second-quarter results for the period ended 30 June 2026: revenue of $668.3 million, up 6.2% year on year against a FactSet consensus of $646.1 million; gross merchandise sales from continuing operations of $2.58 billion, with Etsy-only gross merchandise sales up 7.5%; a revenue take rate of 25.9%; active sellers up 5.9% to 5.7 million; and active buyers down 0.4% to 87.0 million. The reported net loss of $46.7 million is driven by $161 million of losses on discontinued Depop operations rather than by the core marketplace. Kruti Patel Goyal took the chief executive role earlier in 2026. Wedbush read the reduction as supportive of the gross merchandise sales trend. Jewellery and personal accessories is one of the six largest categories by gross merchandise sales in Etsy's own filings; third-party estimates place it near 30% of the platform total, a figure this desk carries as an outside estimate because Etsy does not publish a category split.

Source — Etsy Q2 2026 Form 10-Q / Reuters / Shopifreaks / MarketScreener
Retail & Tech This week — August 10

QVC Group leaves Chapter 11 in 113 days having cut more than $5 billion of debt

QVC Group formally emerged from Chapter 11 protection on 7 August 2026, having filed on 16 April 2026, a span of 113 days derived by this desk from the two dates. The restructuring removed more than $5 billion of debt. As part of the exit the company issued $1.2 billion of take-back notes due 2032 carrying a 10% coupon, disclosed in a regulatory filing, and secured a $600 million asset-based lending facility; the equity was approved for trading on Nasdaq under the ticker QVCG. David Rawlinson, chief executive since 2021, stepped down at the exit and was succeeded by Mike George, who previously ran QVC for more than a decade, as interim chief executive officer and board chair with immediate effect, alongside a newly appointed eight-member board. The company entered court with a pre-negotiated restructuring plan intended to compress the timetable, and the plan received judicial approval in July before the exit closed. Fine and fashion jewellery has been among the top-selling categories on the QVC platform, making the outcome directly relevant to suppliers who use long-form television demonstration as a volume channel. Alex Hennick, president and chief executive of A.D. Hennick & Associates, said the company still has brand recognition, loyal customers and strong vendor relationships. The 10% coupon on the new paper is the credit market's price for the business after the debt reduction and is carried here as the material figure alongside the headline cut.

Source — JCK / Retail Dive / Bloomberg / PYMNTS
