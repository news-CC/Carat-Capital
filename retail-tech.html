Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
Lab-grown (LGD)Term

Diamonds grown in weeks by HPHT or CVD — chemically identical to mined, economically a different universe. Their price curve is a technology curve, which is the whole story.

ClientelingPractice

One-to-one retail relationship management — the private-client WhatsApp thread, remembered anniversaries, first calls on new goods. The oldest luxury skill, now run on software.

OmnichannelConcept

The customer researches at midnight, tries on Saturday, buys by DM on Tuesday. Winning retailers price, stock and staff as one continuous counter across all of it.

Digital product passportRegulation

An EU-led ID standard giving each piece a scannable record of origin, materials and custody. Coming for jewelry the way nutrition labels came for food.

Retail & Tech This week — August 3

More than 46% of Kalyan's quarterly revenue came in as recycled gold

Kalyan Jewellers reported consolidated revenue from operations of ₹10,589 crore for the quarter to 30 June 2026, up 46% from ₹7,268 crore a year earlier, and consolidated net profit of ₹349 crore, up 32%. The results were announced on 4 August 2026. Same-store sales in India grew about 28%, international revenue rose 35%, and Candere, the group's digital business, grew 112%. The company operates 524 showrooms worldwide, 354 of them Kalyan-branded in India. The company states that its gold-recirculation campaign, run under the name Shine with India, contributed more than 46% of revenue across the quarter and more than 55% in the month of June alone, reducing its reliance on imported gold. In the same week, MMTC-PAMP, India's only LBMA Good Delivery refiner of gold and silver, launched a limited-edition 10-gram commemorative coin on 4 August struck entirely from 999.9-plus recycled gold, capped at 8,000 pieces in assayer-certified packaging carrying unique identification numbers, unveiled in New Delhi by the Olympic silver medallist Mirabai Chanu. The volume backdrop, previously filed by this paper, is Indian gold jewellery demand down 15% by weight in the second quarter to 75.1 tonnes, its lowest second quarter since the pandemic, against a value up about 50%. A 46% revenue rise against a national tonnage down 15% therefore combines a price effect, an exchange effect and a store-count effect; the company's own same-store figure of about 28% is the comparable measure.

Source — Business Standard Capital Market / Indian Television / The Tribune
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

P N Gadgil retires Litestyle for YOOU and sets a 100-store target for 2030

P N Gadgil Jewellers announced on 8 August 2026 that it has replaced Litestyle by PNG, its lightweight diamond jewellery line, with a new brand called YOOU, offered in 9, 14, 18 and 22-carat gold and positioned for everyday rather than occasion wear. The line currently trades through 13 exclusive brand outlets across Maharashtra and Goa plus shop-in-shop counters across five states, against a stated target of 100 stores by 2030. Sara Tendulkar continues as brand ambassador, carried over from Litestyle. Saurabh Gadgil, chairman and managing director, described the brand as built for contemporary collections and everyday wear. The parent company reported first-quarter revenue of ₹2,413 crore, up 41% year on year, and has said it plans to open roughly 103 stores across its whole portfolio by the end of the current financial year. The segment context previously filed by this paper is that lightweight, low-carat diamond jewellery aimed at everyday wear is the most exposed category to lab-grown substitution: on 7 August this paper reported lab-grown diamond studs selling at $29.99 in the American mall channel. Two Indian jewellers filed news in the same week and both point the same way, Kalyan sourcing more than 46% of revenue from recycled gold and P N Gadgil building a chain around 9 to 18-carat pieces bought without an occasion, both responses to a gold price that has made the traditional heavy-gold Indian purchase unaffordable at the old frequency.

Source — ScanX / InvestyWise / Carat Capital archive
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

HKTDC survey of 1,507 puts diamond first on demand and fashion jewellery first on growth

The Hong Kong Trade Development Council surveyed 1,507 buyers and exhibitors at its twin fairs, the Hong Kong International Jewellery Show and the Hong Kong International Diamond, Gem and Pearl Show, which ran together from 29 July to 2 August 2026 at the Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre. On the most-in-demand stone, 29% named diamond, 25% ruby and 20% pearl. On growth potential by segment, 57% named trendy fashion jewellery, 35% precious jewellery and 21% designer jewellery. Yellow gold was named the most popular precious metal by 40% of respondents. Respondents described themselves as cautious about 2026 and said they expect one to two years before confidence is fully restored. Hong Kong's own 2025 trade data provides the base: exports of fine jewellery rose 5%, driven by precious metal prices rather than units; exports of pearls, gemstones and rough diamonds fell 8%; exports of imitation jewellery fell 26%. The twenty-eight-point gap between the fashion jewellery and precious jewellery answers on growth is the widest signal in the survey and sits against a week in which gold closed at $4,341.30 an ounce, $139.58 a gram. Related context previously filed by this paper: China Gold Association half-year figures showing Chinese gold jewellery consumption down 33.88% by weight while bar and coin demand rose 28.42% (7 August).

Source — HKTDC via Rapaport / HKTDC Media Room
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

US jewellery value rose 8.6% in the first half on a unit count that fell about 9%

Figures from the analytics firm Tenoris, reported by JCK on 5 August 2026, put United States jewellery sales up 8.6% across the first half of 2026 while the average purchase price rose 19% and unit sales declined. June alone rose 13% year on year. Sales of items priced below $1,500 continued to decline. The implied unit change, derived by this desk from the published value and average-price figures rather than published as such, is a fall of roughly 9%. The composition reflects a spending distribution rather than a jewellery-specific trend: The New York Times has the top 10% of American households accounting for nearly half of all consumer spending, and a discretionary category inherits that shape. De Beers chief executive Al Cook, speaking at the JCK show, said larger and higher-quality diamonds are growing in value and desire. The same mechanism appears in three company results previously filed by this paper: Brilliant Earth's second quarter, revenue up 6% on total orders down 2.1% with average order value up 8% to $2,238 (7 August); Richemont's quarter to 30 June, jewellery up 24% within total sales up 20% and a seventh consecutive quarter of double-digit jewellery growth (15 July); and Luk Fook's record profit taken from fixed-price design rather than gram weight (5 August). The Knot separately reports six of ten brides in 2025 chose lab-grown centre stones.

Source — Tenoris via JCK / New York Times / company results
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

NY Now closes its first show under Rockview with buyers up 5% and 96% rebooked

NY Now's summer market ran from 2 to 4 August 2026 at the Jacob K. Javits Center in New York with 460 exhibitors, more than 70 of them new to the show or returning after an absence, across its Gift and Lifestyle, Jewelry and Accessories, and Home sections. Buyer attendance rose 5% on the previous summer edition. Organisers report a 96% renewal rate for the winter 2027 market and say 107 prospective exhibitors walked the summer floor to assess taking space. It was the first edition under Rockview Management Group, which acquired NY Now and JA New York from Emerald before that company's own acquisition by Apollo, and which has placed the industry veteran Dorothy Belshaw in charge. The winter 2027 market is scheduled for 31 January to 2 February 2027. Neither the attendance rise nor the renewal rate is broken out by section, so the jewellery-specific read is limited: NY Now is a gift and home market with a jewellery and accessories hall inside it rather than a jewellery fair. This paper filed the show's opening on 2 August. Separately and not filed as an article: Etsy is cutting 220 jobs, 12% of its workforce, in product and engineering, with second-quarter sales up 6% and first-half revenue up 5%; no jewellery-seller-specific impact has been reported and it is carried here as context only.

Source — Gifts & Decorative Accessories / National Jeweler / NY NOW
