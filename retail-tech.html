Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
Lab-grown (LGD)Term

Diamonds grown in weeks by HPHT or CVD — chemically identical to mined, economically a different universe. Their price curve is a technology curve, which is the whole story.

ClientelingPractice

One-to-one retail relationship management — the private-client WhatsApp thread, remembered anniversaries, first calls on new goods. The oldest luxury skill, now run on software.

OmnichannelConcept

The customer researches at midnight, tries on Saturday, buys by DM on Tuesday. Winning retailers price, stock and staff as one continuous counter across all of it.

Digital product passportRegulation

An EU-led ID standard giving each piece a scannable record of origin, materials and custody. Coming for jewelry the way nutrition labels came for food.

Retail & Tech This week — August 3

Brilliant Earth returns to profit on a bigger ticket and fewer orders

Brilliant Earth reported second-quarter 2026 revenue of $115.1 million, up 6% year on year, on a 2.1% decline in total orders, with average order value up 8% to $2,238. Net income was $0.8 million against a net loss of $1.1 million in the same quarter a year earlier. Gross margin was 57.9%, a 360 basis point sequential improvement. Fine jewellery bookings grew 32% year on year. The company raised full-year revenue guidance to $459 million to $462 million and guided the third quarter to roughly flat sales with adjusted EBITDA between $3 million and $5 million. The composition is the result: revenue growth came from ticket rather than customer count, which concentrates the quarter in existing higher-income demand and leaves no volume growth underneath to absorb a trim in that cohort. The pattern matches other results filed by this paper in the same period, including US jewellery revenue rising while tonnage fell (3 August) and Luk Fook's fixed-price mix carrying a record profit (5 August).

Source — Rapaport / GlobeNewswire company release / JCK
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

Claire's puts lab-grown diamond studs on a $29.99 piercing counter

Claire's is relaunching its in-store piercing experience across the United States and Canada from August 2026, the second overhaul of that service in three years, and has expanded the starter stud assortment to include lab-grown diamonds and coloured gemstones alongside existing gold and steel options. Piercing kits start at $29.99 with the piercing itself free on kit purchase. The lab-grown range includes 14-carat white gold studs at 0.10 carats total weight, sold as complete kits with aftercare solution. The significance is the price tier rather than the material: at this level grown diamond functions as a finish and hypoallergenic setting specification rather than as a gemstone purchase, and the competitive displacement falls on cubic zirconia and surgical steel rather than on natural diamond. It is the clearest published evidence to date of grown material reaching mass-market starter pricing, and follows this paper's earlier coverage of grown wholesale price declines and of retailers tiering their floors between lab-grown for everyday and natural for rarity.

Source — National Jeweler / Forbes / Retail Dive / Claire's
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

Signet gives Blue Nile to a Tiffany merchandising veteran

Signet announced on 5 August that Pam Cloud becomes president of Blue Nile effective 10 August, reporting to Joan Hilson, Signet's chief operating and financial officer. Cloud has more than thirty years in luxury retail, of which more than twenty-five were spent at Tiffany and Co., latterly as senior vice president and chief merchandising officer and a member of the executive committee. She subsequently founded Roseate Jewelry, a direct-to-consumer pearl brand. Signet acquired Blue Nile in 2022 for $360 million. The appointment places a merchant rather than a marketer in charge of a business originally built on price transparency for loose stones, which reads as a decision about assortment and presentation rather than traffic or conversion. It is the second senior jewellery appointment in a week and close to the inverse of the first: Pandora named Andre Branch, a consumer-brands executive with no jewellery background, president of its North America cluster from 15 August, filed by this paper on 5 August. Both new presidents start in mid-August, with the American fourth quarter already substantially bought.

Source — JCK / The Jewelry Wire / Rapaport
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

British buy-now-pay-later came under FCA supervision on 15 July

Deferred payment credit, the interest-free instalment model underpinning buy-now-pay-later, entered the United Kingdom's regulatory perimeter under Financial Conduct Authority supervision on 15 July 2026. The market grew from about £60 million of transaction value in 2017 to more than £13 billion in 2024, with the FCA putting British usage at around 11 million consumers. Lenders entering deferred payment credit agreements must be authorised for the relevant consumer credit activities or hold temporary permission under the temporary permissions regime, and must carry out proportionate affordability checks before extending credit and give consumers clear upfront terms covering payment dates, amounts and the consequence of a missed payment. Consumers gain access to the Financial Ombudsman Service. Firms could register for temporary permission between 15 May and 1 July 2026 and have six months from commencement to apply for full authorisation. The relevance to jewellery is direct: most independents accept a provider's checkout plug-in rather than lending themselves, so the authorisation duty sits with the provider, but the January authorisation deadline is the point at which some smaller providers may withdraw. This is a three-week-old rule change filed now for its forward consequences, not news of the day.

Source — Financial Conduct Authority / GOV.UK / Reed Smith
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

Onyx Partners bids $934 million for 117 J.C. Penney anchor stores

Onyx Partners, a Boston-area private equity firm, has offered $934 million for a portfolio of 117 J.C. Penney stores covering 15.7 million square feet across 35 states, about $8 million a store or roughly $59 a square foot. It is the firm's second attempt; a higher offer of $947 million was rejected in December. Onyx states that financing is fully in place and that it is prepared to close on 25 September. The seller is Copper Property CTL Pass Through Trust, a Jersey City vehicle created after J.C. Penney's 2020 Chapter 11 filing with a mandate to liquidate real estate for the benefit of investors. The relevance to the jewellery trade is the anchor position: J.C. Penney has operated fine jewellery departments for decades, and anchor ownership determines footfall past every specialist jeweller in a mall concourse. An implied price near land-and-shell levels rather than going-concern retail levels indicates a market clearing anchor space on the assumption the current use may not be the final use. The transaction has not closed.

Source — National Jeweler / CoStar / Retail Dive
