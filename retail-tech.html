Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
Lab-grown (LGD)Term

Diamonds grown in weeks by HPHT or CVD — chemically identical to mined, economically a different universe. Their price curve is a technology curve, which is the whole story.

ClientelingPractice

One-to-one retail relationship management — the private-client WhatsApp thread, remembered anniversaries, first calls on new goods. The oldest luxury skill, now run on software.

OmnichannelConcept

The customer researches at midnight, tries on Saturday, buys by DM on Tuesday. Winning retailers price, stock and staff as one continuous counter across all of it.

Digital product passportRegulation

An EU-led ID standard giving each piece a scannable record of origin, materials and custody. Coming for jewelry the way nutrition labels came for food.

Retail & Tech This week — August 17 to 30

US customs seizes 875 counterfeit jewellery pieces in one Hong Kong parcel bound for a Georgia residence, $3.7 million at list

United States Customs and Border Protection seized 875 counterfeit pieces of jewellery and five designer handbags from a single parcel at the Indianapolis express consignment facility, shipped from Hong Kong and addressed to a residence in Georgia. National Jeweler, reporting 27 August 2026, and Rapaport, reporting 26 August, both put the retail value at $3.7 million had the jewellery been genuine, an average of $4,228.57 across 875 pieces. National Jeweler lists counterfeit marks for Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Versace, Gucci, Tiffany and Co., Cartier and Van Cleef and Arpels; Rapaport's account names Cartier, Tiffany and Van Cleef and Arpels. The pieces were earrings, necklaces and bracelets. Brett Mueller, Indianapolis port director, credited officers and import specialists working with brand owners. The $3.7 million is a suggested retail figure measuring infringement at full price, not the sum paid for the parcel. This paper reported on 19 July 2026 that the marketplace Bezel turns away 38% of the Rolex watches submitted to its authenticators.

Source — National Jeweler, 27 August 2026; Rapaport, 26 August 2026; Carat Capital, 19 July 2026
Retail & Tech This week — August 17 to 30

Chow Sang Sang's half to 30 June takes HK$12.88 billion with profit up 139%, and the net margin roughly doubles

Chow Sang Sang reported on 27 August 2026 that revenue for the six months to 30 June 2026 was HK$12.88 billion, about $1.64 billion, up 17% year on year, with retail sales of HK$12.71 billion or $1.62 billion, up 18%, which is 98.68% of the group figure. Profit was HK$2.15 billion, about $274.6 million, up 139%. Working back from those figures, the comparable half produced roughly HK$11.01 billion of revenue and about HK$900 million of profit, a net margin near 8.17% against 16.69% this half, on this desk's arithmetic from the company's own published percentages. Same-store sales grew 11% in mainland China, made of 7% in the first quarter and 17% in the second, and 38% in Hong Kong and Macau, made of 46% then 30%. A net 47 shops closed, predominantly on the mainland. The company said key collections including Cultural Blessings and Noir recorded strong growth. Divergence disclosed: this paper reported on 22 July 2026, from the company's earlier quarterly update, a net 49 shops closed in the first half ending June at 776 stores; the interim results give 47 for the same six months. The two are not reconciled from the material this desk holds, and the likeliest explanations, a group-versus-mainland scope difference or a revision between operational update and audited accounts, are neither confirmed. The release carries no split between gold and gem-set jewellery. Implied exchange rates from the company's own dual figures are HK$7.8537 and HK$7.8296 to the dollar.

Source — Rapaport News, 27 August 2026; Carat Capital, 22 July 2026
Retail & Tech This week — August 17 to 30

A Gujarat gold board prints ₹1,63,790 for all three cities, and two of its three karat prices are pure division

TV9 Gujarati published a gold and silver rate board on 27 August 2026, bylined to Dhruval Shah and timestamped 9.45 a.m., putting 24-carat gold at ₹1,63,790 per ten grams, 22-carat at ₹1,50,140 and 18-carat at ₹1,22,840, and repeating those identical figures for Ahmedabad, Surat and Rajkot. The page credits Groww as its data source and carries its own disclaimer that the information rests on media reports. This desk fetched the page directly on 29 August 2026 and confirmed each figure and the date in the Gujarati body text rather than in a listing. The karat lines are pure ratio: ₹1,63,790 times 22/24 is ₹1,50,140.83, printed as ₹1,50,140, and times 18/24 is ₹1,22,842.50, printed as ₹1,22,840, both rounded to the nearest ten rupees and neither carrying the alloy, wastage or making premium a real 22-carat counter charges. The same page puts the ten-day high at ₹1,64,020 on 24 August and the ten-day low at ₹1,55,020 on 19 August, a range of ₹9,000 or 5.8057% of the low, and 27 July at ₹1,45,940 against 27 August at ₹1,63,790, a gain of ₹17,850 or 12.2311% in a month. The 24 August peak falls on the same date this paper's own tape recorded its gold high-water mark of $4,643.90, from an entirely separate source. The board's 27 August reading predates Friday's 3.2604% fall in spot gold. Per gram the 24-carat figure is ₹16,379.

Source — TV9 Gujarati, 27 August 2026 (Gujarati), fetched and confirmed 29 August 2026
Retail & Tech This week — August 17 to 30

Gold at ₹1.7 lakh per 10 grams means the ₹5,000 Raksha Bandhan gift ceiling buys 0.294 grams

Diamond City reported on 27 August that Ahmedabad and Surat counters held Raksha Bandhan demand at record prices, putting gold at ₹1.7 lakh per 10 grams against roughly ₹1.02 lakh a year earlier (up 66.67%) and silver at ₹2.45 lakh a kilo against ₹1.17 lakh (up 109.40%), both on this desk's arithmetic. The outlet's own claim that precious metals more than doubled holds for silver and not for gold. At ₹17,000 a gram the ₹5,000 gift band buys 0.294 grams, and the 1.5 to 2 gram pieces reported selling fastest are ₹25,500 to ₹34,000 in metal alone. Jigar Soni, president of the Jewellers Association of Ahmedabad, reports strongest demand in rakhis, coins, crockery and gifts to ₹5,000; Param Begani attributes lab-grown popularity among younger buyers to gold prices; Ashish Zaveri credits a partial price correction and reports 9-carat buying among the young. Nine-carat is 37.5% gold against 22-carat's 91.67%, or 40.9% of the content. The outlet carries a secondary-research disclaimer on its price comparisons. No volume figure in grams was published.

Source — Diamond City, 27 August 2026, with the outlet's own compilation disclaimer
Retail & Tech This week — August 17 to 30

Laopu Gold's first-half profit rose 88.18% to 4.267 billion yuan, and the interim dividend takes 74.6% of it

Laopu Gold (06181.HK) published interim results for the six months to 30 June on 25 August. The company reports two top lines and they must not be confused: sales performance, covering total product sales, was 22.779 billion yuan, up 60.6%, while recognised revenue was 19.808 billion yuan, up 60.3%. The difference is 2.971 billion yuan, or 13.04% of the sales figure on this desk's arithmetic, and summaries quoting 22.78 billion as revenue overstate the business against its own accounts. Profit attributable to owners was 4.267 billion yuan, up 88.18%; adjusted net profit on the non-IFRS measure was 4.317 billion yuan, up 83.6%, so the adjustment makes growth slower rather than faster. Gross margin rose from 38.1% to 41.3%, net margin from 18.4% to 21.5%. Basic earnings were 24.16 yuan a share against an interim dividend of 18.02 yuan, a payout of 74.59%. Offline stores were 77.8% of revenue, online platforms 22.2% and up 171.9%, revenue outside the mainland up 107.8%. Separately dated and not attached to these results: shares fell 23.76% to close at HK$302.20 on 28 July, the day of the profit alert, a month before this release.

Source — Sina Finance and Caizhongshe, on Laopu Gold interim results, 25 August 2026
