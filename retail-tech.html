Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
Lab-grown (LGD)Term

Diamonds grown in weeks by HPHT or CVD — chemically identical to mined, economically a different universe. Their price curve is a technology curve, which is the whole story.

ClientelingPractice

One-to-one retail relationship management — the private-client WhatsApp thread, remembered anniversaries, first calls on new goods. The oldest luxury skill, now run on software.

OmnichannelConcept

The customer researches at midnight, tries on Saturday, buys by DM on Tuesday. Winning retailers price, stock and staff as one continuous counter across all of it.

Digital product passportRegulation

An EU-led ID standard giving each piece a scannable record of origin, materials and custody. Coming for jewelry the way nutrition labels came for food.

Retail & Tech This week — August 3

Hong Kong hard luxury grows four times faster than the city's retail market in June

Hong Kong sales of jewellery, watches, clocks and valuable gifts reached HK$5.49 billion, about $699.9 million, in June 2026, up 20% year on year, on Census and Statistics Department figures published 4 August. Total retail sales across all categories were HK$31.49 billion, about $4.02 billion, up 5%, making hard luxury 17.4% of the city's retail spend and marking a fourteenth consecutive month of overall retail growth. Across the first half, jewellery, watches and clocks took HK$31.07 billion, about $3.96 billion, up 25%, against HK$203 billion, about $25.88 billion, up 10%, for all retail categories. The government attributed the run to continued economic expansion, rising local incomes and a steady increase in inbound visitors. The value line is flattered by a record gold price in a market that sells substantial volumes of gold by weight; Luk Fook's June quarter, filed by this paper on 21 July, showed retail value up 32% and gold same-store sales up 50% while diamond same-store sales fell 17% group-wide and 58% on the mainland.

Source — Census and Statistics Department via Rapaport / Xinhua / SCMP
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

Pandora fills a six-month North American vacancy with a beauty executive

Pandora appointed André Branch president of its North America cluster, effective 15 August 2026, succeeding Luciano Rodembusch, who left in February, leaving the seat vacant roughly six months. Branch joins from the chief executive's role at R.E.M. Beauty and previously held senior positions at Estée Lauder, L'Oréal, Diageo and Kraft Heinz across two decades in consumer brands; he has no jewellery background. Pandora describes North America as one of its largest and most important growth markets and states the brief as strengthening customer engagement, expanding reach and unlocking growth. The appointment follows a wider set of executive changes at Pandora North America reported earlier in the year. The American fourth quarter is already substantially bought by the time he starts.

Source — JCK / Jewellery Focus / Rapaport
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

Luk Fook's record year was built on fixed-price jewellery, not on the gold price alone

Luk Fook Holdings reported net profit up 88.7% to HK$2.015 billion for the year to 31 March 2026, its highest on record, on revenue up 29% to HK$17.21 billion, with gross profit up 42.9% to HK$6.31 billion and a gross margin of 36.7%. The results were disclosed in late June. The driver, stated in the Chinese-language financial press and largely absent from English pickups, is the pricing mix: sales of fixed-price jewellery, priced by the piece, rose 50.5%, while sales of gold and platinum products priced by weight rose 22%. Weight-priced goods pass the metal cost to the customer and earn the retailer a labour charge; fixed-price goods carry the margin inside the ticket, which protects gross margin when gold is at a record but caps revenue upside when volume returns and moves the price exposure onto the buying and hedging desk. This paper filed the same company's June quarter on 21 July, retail value up 32% and gold same-store sales up 50%, and its split on 22 July, diamond same-store sales down 58% on the mainland.

Source — pedaily.cn / TipRanks / Rapaport
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

Vicenzaoro September opens Hall 2 and hosts CIBJO's centenary congress

Vicenzaoro September runs from 4 to 8 September 2026 at Fiera di Vicenza, organised by Italian Exhibition Group, with roughly 1,300 exhibiting brands from 38 countries and buyers expected from more than 130 countries, on figures IEG gave JCK on 13 July. The show was reported close to sold out in late May. The new Hall 2, a two-storey central hub connected by covered walkways, is fully operational for the first time, and T.Gold, the machinery and technology section for precious-metal production, has moved inside the expo centre grounds into Hall 4; Jewellery Outlook confirmed both through interviews published 31 July. CIBJO, the World Jewellery Confederation, holds its centenary congress in Vicenza from 4 to 7 September, one hundred years after its founding in 1926, with its Assembly of Delegates and sectoral commissions meeting and amendments to the Blue Books introduced. The scheduled agenda covers the state of the diamond industry, lab-grown diamonds, the supply chain, pearls and coloured gemstones. Russia's Resolution 657, which bans the word diamond for synthetics sold there, comes into force on 1 September, three days before the congress opens.

Source — JCK / CIBJO / Jewellery Outlook / Vicenzaoro
Retail & Tech This week — August 3

Michael Hill lifts revenue 2% and same-store sales 3% while closing eight stores

Michael Hill reported group revenue of A$654.7 million, about $459.5 million, for the financial year to 28 June 2026, an increase of 2%. Same-store sales rose 3% across the group: 7% in Canada, described by the company as a record, on segment revenue of C$169.3 million; 4.8% in Australia on A$364.6 million; and 3.6% in New Zealand on NZ$108.9 million. The chain closed eight stores over the year, four in Australia and two each in Canada and New Zealand, and opened two, one in Australia and one in Canada, ending with 281 stores: 157 in Australia, 81 in Canada and 43 in New Zealand. Chief executive Jonathan Waecker said growth was significantly accelerating in Canada and New Zealand in the second half. Earnings were not disclosed alongside the sales figures.

Source — Rapaport / Michael Hill
