Talk like the trade — the working vocabulary

The trade's terms, plainly told
Lab-grown (LGD)Term

Diamonds grown in weeks by HPHT or CVD — chemically identical to mined, economically a different universe. Their price curve is a technology curve, which is the whole story.

ClientelingPractice

One-to-one retail relationship management — the private-client WhatsApp thread, remembered anniversaries, first calls on new goods. The oldest luxury skill, now run on software.

OmnichannelConcept

The customer researches at midnight, tries on Saturday, buys by DM on Tuesday. Winning retailers price, stock and staff as one continuous counter across all of it.

Digital product passportRegulation

An EU-led ID standard giving each piece a scannable record of origin, materials and custody. Coming for jewelry the way nutrition labels came for food.

Retail & Tech This week — August 10

Etsy cuts 220 roles from product and engineering in a quarter it beat

Etsy disclosed on 6 August 2026 a restructuring removing approximately 220 roles, about 12% of its marketplace workforce, falling mainly on the product and engineering organisation, at an estimated charge of about $35 million, primarily severance and benefits, to be booked in the third quarter of 2026 and completed within it. Approximately 1,600 employees remain. The company stated that the reduction is not a cost-cutting measure and is not related to artificial intelligence, describing the intent as flatter teams with wider remits, fewer handoffs and quicker decisions. The plan was announced alongside second-quarter results for the period ended 30 June 2026: revenue of $668.3 million, up 6.2% year on year against a FactSet consensus of $646.1 million; gross merchandise sales from continuing operations of $2.58 billion, with Etsy-only gross merchandise sales up 7.5%; a revenue take rate of 25.9%; active sellers up 5.9% to 5.7 million; and active buyers down 0.4% to 87.0 million. The reported net loss of $46.7 million is driven by $161 million of losses on discontinued Depop operations rather than by the core marketplace. Kruti Patel Goyal took the chief executive role earlier in 2026. Wedbush read the reduction as supportive of the gross merchandise sales trend. Jewellery and personal accessories is one of the six largest categories by gross merchandise sales in Etsy's own filings; third-party estimates place it near 30% of the platform total, a figure this desk carries as an outside estimate because Etsy does not publish a category split.

Source — Etsy Q2 2026 Form 10-Q / Reuters / Shopifreaks / MarketScreener
Retail & Tech This week — August 10

QVC Group leaves Chapter 11 in 113 days having cut more than $5 billion of debt

QVC Group formally emerged from Chapter 11 protection on 7 August 2026, having filed on 16 April 2026, a span of 113 days derived by this desk from the two dates. The restructuring removed more than $5 billion of debt. As part of the exit the company issued $1.2 billion of take-back notes due 2032 carrying a 10% coupon, disclosed in a regulatory filing, and secured a $600 million asset-based lending facility; the equity was approved for trading on Nasdaq under the ticker QVCG. David Rawlinson, chief executive since 2021, stepped down at the exit and was succeeded by Mike George, who previously ran QVC for more than a decade, as interim chief executive officer and board chair with immediate effect, alongside a newly appointed eight-member board. The company entered court with a pre-negotiated restructuring plan intended to compress the timetable, and the plan received judicial approval in July before the exit closed. Fine and fashion jewellery has been among the top-selling categories on the QVC platform, making the outcome directly relevant to suppliers who use long-form television demonstration as a volume channel. Alex Hennick, president and chief executive of A.D. Hennick & Associates, said the company still has brand recognition, loyal customers and strong vendor relationships. The 10% coupon on the new paper is the credit market's price for the business after the debt reduction and is carried here as the material figure alongside the headline cut.

Source — JCK / Retail Dive / Bloomberg / PYMNTS
Retail & Tech This week — August 10

Pandora names Paulo Garcia chief financial officer, the third top seat to turn in a year

Pandora announced on 6 August 2026 the appointment of Paulo Garcia as chief financial officer. Garcia joins the company on 1 October 2026 and assumes the role on 1 December 2026, succeeding Anders Boyer, who retires on 30 November 2026 after more than fourteen years with the company and remains until 31 March 2027 to support the handover. Garcia is a Portuguese national with twenty-five years of international executive experience across Europe and Latin America. He joins from the chief financial officer's role at Walmart de Mexico y Centroamerica, a separately listed subsidiary with about $50 billion of revenue and 240,000 employees, and was previously chief financial officer for Europe and Indonesia at Ahold Delhaize and before that at Unilever. The appointment completes a turnover of three senior seats inside twelve months at the largest jewellery company in the world by units: Berta de Pablos-Barbier takes office as chief executive on 1 January 2027, and Andre Branch was named president of the North America cluster in an announcement this paper filed on 5 August 2026 as pandora-fills-the-seat. None of the three arrives from the jewellery trade. Pandora's own published figures put the company at around 7,000 points of sale globally including more than 2,800 concept stores, and approximately 39,000 employees. The company sells lab-grown diamonds in eight markets, having added Spain and Italy in June 2026 with flagship stores in Barcelona and Milan.

Source — Pandora Group / WWD / FashionUnited / Carat Capital archive
Retail & Tech Week of August 3

More than 46% of Kalyan's quarterly revenue came in as recycled gold

Kalyan Jewellers reported consolidated revenue from operations of ₹10,589 crore for the quarter to 30 June 2026, up 46% from ₹7,268 crore a year earlier, and consolidated net profit of ₹349 crore, up 32%. The results were announced on 4 August 2026. Same-store sales in India grew about 28%, international revenue rose 35%, and Candere, the group's digital business, grew 112%. The company operates 524 showrooms worldwide, 354 of them Kalyan-branded in India. The company states that its gold-recirculation campaign, run under the name Shine with India, contributed more than 46% of revenue across the quarter and more than 55% in the month of June alone, reducing its reliance on imported gold. In the same week, MMTC-PAMP, India's only LBMA Good Delivery refiner of gold and silver, launched a limited-edition 10-gram commemorative coin on 4 August struck entirely from 999.9-plus recycled gold, capped at 8,000 pieces in assayer-certified packaging carrying unique identification numbers, unveiled in New Delhi by the Olympic silver medallist Mirabai Chanu. The volume backdrop, previously filed by this paper, is Indian gold jewellery demand down 15% by weight in the second quarter to 75.1 tonnes, its lowest second quarter since the pandemic, against a value up about 50%. A 46% revenue rise against a national tonnage down 15% therefore combines a price effect, an exchange effect and a store-count effect; the company's own same-store figure of about 28% is the comparable measure.

Source — Business Standard Capital Market / Indian Television / The Tribune
Retail & Tech Week of August 3

P N Gadgil retires Litestyle for YOOU and sets a 100-store target for 2030

P N Gadgil Jewellers announced on 8 August 2026 that it has replaced Litestyle by PNG, its lightweight diamond jewellery line, with a new brand called YOOU, offered in 9, 14, 18 and 22-carat gold and positioned for everyday rather than occasion wear. The line currently trades through 13 exclusive brand outlets across Maharashtra and Goa plus shop-in-shop counters across five states, against a stated target of 100 stores by 2030. Sara Tendulkar continues as brand ambassador, carried over from Litestyle. Saurabh Gadgil, chairman and managing director, described the brand as built for contemporary collections and everyday wear. The parent company reported first-quarter revenue of ₹2,413 crore, up 41% year on year, and has said it plans to open roughly 103 stores across its whole portfolio by the end of the current financial year. The segment context previously filed by this paper is that lightweight, low-carat diamond jewellery aimed at everyday wear is the most exposed category to lab-grown substitution: on 7 August this paper reported lab-grown diamond studs selling at $29.99 in the American mall channel. Two Indian jewellers filed news in the same week and both point the same way, Kalyan sourcing more than 46% of revenue from recycled gold and P N Gadgil building a chain around 9 to 18-carat pieces bought without an occasion, both responses to a gold price that has made the traditional heavy-gold Indian purchase unaffordable at the old frequency.

Source — ScanX / InvestyWise / Carat Capital archive
