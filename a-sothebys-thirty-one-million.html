Trophies carry Sotheby's to $31.4 million; a pink walks

Sotheby's New York High Jewelry sale of 13 June 2025 made $31.4 million with 95% sold, led by a $3.2 million Graff diamond ring. Down the street, Phillips withdrew a 3.03-carat fancy pink when bids missed its reserve.

By The Rostrum Desk24 July 2026Brief 45 sec · Full read 2 minCorrected · see The depth
What changed

The trophy end of the jewellery market is still doing the work. Sotheby's High Jewelry sale in New York on 13 June 2025 made $31.4 million with 95% of lots finding buyers, a result led, as these sales almost always are, by a handful of exceptional single stones rather than broad depth.

What it means · The Desk’s View

$31.4 million at 95% sold is a strong night, but read it with the withdrawn pink and the lesson is precision, not exuberance. The market for singular, provenance-backed stones is genuinely intact; the market for aggressively estimated ones is not.

Collectors are still paying for rarity they can name, a Kashmir sapphire, a Burmese ruby, a signed JAR, and passing on anything that feels priced for a hotter year than this one. The trophies carry the room.

They no longer forgive a reserve set in the mood of the year before.

IThe very top is a different economy

That night is a marker rather than a bulletin, and it is read here as one: the sale it belongs to is more than a year behind the tape this paper prints each morning, and the room described below is the room of that June, not of this one.

The board read like a tour of what money still chases. A Graff ring set with a 35-carat D-colour diamond took $3.2 million. A Marcus & Co. sautoir centred on a 9.6-carat Burmese ruby and a 2.02-carat fancy vivid blue diamond tied at $1.8 million apiece.

A JAR ring holding an 8.62-carat Colombian emerald made $1.6 million, and a 1930s Kashmir sapphire ring of 7.5 carats reached $1.4 million. Colour, provenance and signature, the three things a factory cannot print, carried the night.

IIColour, provenance, signature

Sotheby's framed it as continuity. Quig Bruning, the house's head of jewellery in the Americas, said provenance, rarity and exceptional craftsmanship continue to ignite the passions of collectors, and called the sale a great continuation of what he described as a phenomenal year for jewellery.

The house has reason for the confidence: at the level where a single stone clears seven figures, the buyer pool has barely noticed the macro weather that has chilled the retail counter.

IIIBut a too-high estimate still walks

The counter-example was a short walk away, on the same week's calendar. Phillips withdrew the top lot of its own New York jewels sale, a 3.03-carat fancy intense pink diamond estimated at $1.2 million to $1.6 million, after bids failed to meet the reserve.

A withdrawn headline lot is the auction world's quiet way of saying the estimate and the room disagreed, and it is a useful corrective to any story that the top is invincible. Even at the summit, price discovery still bites when a house aims too high.

02Corrections1 note

Correction, 18 August 2026: this article was published on 24 July 2026 and reported Sotheby's New York High Jewelry sale of "June 13" without a year, framing it as a current result in "a year of soft mid-market tapes." The sale it describes closed on 13 June 2025. The 2026 edition of the same sale ran on 16 June 2026 and made $43.4 million with 98% of 119 lots sold, led by an unmounted 10.02-carat fancy intense blue diamond at $8.7 million. The error came from reading a source page without checking its own publication date, the trap this paper's own bureau desk screens for daily. Every figure in this article was correct as published for the sale it actually describes: the $31.4 million total, the 95% sell-through, the $3.2 million Graff ring, the Marcus & Co. sautoir, the JAR emerald, the Kashmir sapphire and the withdrawn Phillips pink. Only the year was missing, and the present tense it was written in was wrong.
